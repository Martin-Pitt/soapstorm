# Attack on Entry 12 (Behaviour-Audit-First) — COMPREHENSION lens

## Verdict up front

Entry 12 is a well-built **fairness sub-tool** wearing a ladder-of-abstraction costume. Its four rungs form a real, working ladder *for the narrow question "is this avatar's pattern of hits suspicious"* — digging from Matrix → Avatar → Signal → concrete instant genuinely works and genuinely lets the officer step back up. But the brief's ladder is about comprehending **a raid**, and the raid itself — where fighting happened, when fronts formed and collapsed, who was even in this fight — never gets a rung in this entry. Q1 is answered by pointing at capability this entry does not design ("overlay in helicopter rung-1 mode shows all trails as one picture" — that view is never specified anywhere in the Ladder or Screens sections). The entry solves the hardest 20% of the brief (honest fairness signals) exceptionally well and quietly assumes someone else solved the other 80% (raid comprehension). Read on its own, an officer opening this floater set with no whole-raid narrative already loaded would be lost.

There is also a load-bearing self-contradiction: the thesis promises "never sorting anything by a composite number," and the Watchlist's primary and only sort key is a composite number.

## Q1 — "How did this raid go, overall? Where, when, between whom?"

Walking it: officer opens "Combat Log floater" (not this entry's — the base plan's), sets Live/whole-session view, and the entry's walkthrough says the overlay shows "all trails as one picture" with "two clusters" of deaths visible on the timeline (14:10–14:20 push, 14:35 rally). Then: "Switch to Fairness Matrix for a first honesty pass."

Failure: nothing in Entry 12's Ladder, Information Architecture, or Screens sections defines that whole-raid trails-as-one-picture view, its clustering-by-time-and-place, or a timeline with density bands. It is invoked in the walkthrough as if it already exists, but it is not among this entry's four rungs — Rung 1 here is the *Engagement Trail*, defined explicitly as "every shot a chosen avatar landed," i.e. already scoped to one avatar, not the raid. So the very first officer question in the brief is answered by silently borrowing an undesigned capability, then immediately pivoting to the Fairness Matrix — which is this entry's real contribution and answers a *different* question ("is anyone behaving badly"), not "where/when/between whom did the raid happen." An officer who trusts this entry as their comprehension tool has no page here that shows fronts forming/collapsing, no "Place" timeline (the Place noun clusters by geography only, not by time-window, so it can't show a front collapsing and reforming at a different spot), and no way to see "between whom" at the raid level — Teams only exists as an input to fairness baselines, not as a first-class overview of who fought whom, how much, and when.

Given that Q1 is explicitly the *first* officer question the brief lists, and the entry devotes zero rungs, zero panels, and zero analysis-model paragraphs to it, this is a serious comprehension failure, not a stylistic one.

## Q3 — "Why did X die at 14:32? Who, what, from where, could they see them?"

Walking it: death tick → Inspector → Damage chain (existing plan) → Fairness tab → CLEAR verdict, flip at k=5 → "Sweep candidates in-world" → officer sees Kaela step into the sightline before firing.

This one works well and is the entry's strongest walkthrough. The LOS sweep-as-a-slider (Rung 2) is a genuine Bret-Victor move: instead of a single boolean CLEAR/BLOCKED per hit, the officer watches the verdict flip as k varies and can *see* the flip point in-world, which converts "trust the algorithm's verdict" into "watch the mechanism produce the verdict." Reaction latency shown with its cue type (SOUND vs LOS vs view-cone) is honest about what kind of awareness the number claims to measure. Counter-evidence (chokepoint) is looked for and shown, not asserted. This is exactly the "dig and dig, step back down to the concrete instance" pattern the brief asks for, done correctly at the single-death scale.

Two smaller gaps: (1) the Damage chain tab is the *existing plan's*, not this entry's — the entry adds a Fairness sub-tab beside it, but never says whether/how a chain of *several* damage events (not just the killing blow) each gets their own awareness class and LOS sweep, or whether only the final blow does. For "who hit them" (plural, per Q3's wording) with multiple attackers contributing to one death, the walkthrough only shows Kaela; if two attackers hit Rook in the death window, does the Fairness tab render two independent LOS sweeps, or one? Unspecified. (2) The reaction-latency figure needs the *target's* prior firing event as an audible cue, but Rook is dead — the design says "Rook fired 0.4s prior," meaning the cue is themselves shooting, not being aware of Kaela; that's fine as a definition, but it silently assumes symmetric combat (both parties shooting) which will not hold for an ambush kill (Q3's classic "did they even see it coming" case) — an ambushed victim who never fired gets no SOUND cue and no reaction-latency figure at all, and the entry doesn't say what displays in that (very common) case.

## Q8 — "Did anyone behave suspiciously? Show evidence and honest uncertainty, not verdicts."

Walking it: Auditor Watchlist, drag threshold 2×→4× median, only Kaela's cluster survives; open her Avatar Page; 3 BLOCKED instances explained by a known chokepoint shared with two other avatars; officer concludes "geometry explains it" as a note, not a system verdict.

This is where the design's thesis collides with its own screen. Two failures:

1. **The "Signals" column is the cheat score the thesis forbids.** Section 8 (Risks) admits: "the single greatest risk is officers reading 'Signals: 4' as a score anyway... mitigate with copy discipline... and by never sorting anything by a composite number." But the Watchlist screen in section 4.1 is *explicitly* "sorted by signal count," and "Signals" is defined as a single integer that sums through-wall clusters, speed flags, and (implicitly, since it's the primary ranking column) whatever else crosses the threshold slider — heterogeneous signal types added into one number and used as the primary sort key. That is a composite number, and it is exactly the thing officers will read as a score, because the UI hands them a ranked list with one number per row and nothing else to sort by. Naming it "Signals" instead of "Suspicion" does not change what it does mechanically: a heterogeneous count, ranked, is a score with a different label. This directly undercuts Q8's "not verdicts" requirement — the Watchlist *is* a verdict-shaped artifact (a leaderboard), the mitigation is copy discipline alone, and the entry's own risk section flags this without the design actually closing the gap (it never proposes, e.g., forcing the list to be un-sorted or sorted only by avatar name/join time, or splitting into per-signal-type sublists that can't be summed).

2. **Uncertainty is honest for equipment, decorative for avatars.** The Equipment Ledger correctly uses a Wilson score interval so "33% through-wall (n=6)" reads as noise. The Watchlist and Avatar Page show `AwareMix 58/12/24/6` as bare percentages with no n and no interval — for an avatar with, say, 5 landed hits this is exactly the small-n trap the entry itself warns against for equipment, applied inconsistently to people. Given that Q8 is specifically about *not* over-reading noise into an accusation against a *person* (higher stakes than a weapon), this is a real gap: the honesty machinery the entry built (CIs, baselines, counter-evidence) is bolted onto Equipment and skipped on the exact page an officer opens right before deciding whether to discipline someone.

Once those two are fixed the Q8 walkthrough itself is fine: the threshold-sweep-then-settle interaction is good (Rung 2 in practice), the chokepoint counter-evidence is exactly the kind of exculpatory cross-reference the brief wants, and the requirement that the officer, not the tool, writes the conclusion is honored in the copy.

## Ladder integrity: is it a ladder, or a pile of views wearing rung labels?

Within the fairness sub-domain, it is a real ladder: Matrix → Avatar/Engagement Trail → Signal documentation/threshold sweep → concrete instant, each with a genuine step up and step down, satisfying the brief's transition requirement. But two structural problems remain:

- **Declaring a ceiling** ("Rung 3... it is the ceiling, deliberately... there is no rung that collapses the matrix into a single score") misreads the brief's warning. The brief's "never get stuck... in the clouds" is about the interaction discipline (always offer a way down), not about capping the abstraction levels available. A composite score isn't the only thing that could live above the matrix — a *raid-shape* view (when/where fairness questions cluster relative to the fight's timeline) could sit above it without being a score, and its absence is what leaves Q1 unanswered by this entry. Refusing to design a top rung "on principle" is a rationalization for not having designed the raid-level view at all.
- **The whole entry is a second floater bolted onto an unspecified first one.** Every walkthrough that isn't Q3/Q8-shaped (i.e., every whole-raid or narrative question) has to reach outside Entry 12 into "the Combat Log floater" and just asserts it does the right thing. That's a legitimate scoping choice for a competition entry focused on fairness, but it means this entry cannot be graded as "does the officer comprehend a combat session" on its own — only as "does the officer comprehend one avatar's fairness profile," which is a real and useful thing, just not the brief's Q1.

## Freeform combat (allegiance drift, intra-group skirmish, mid-fight arrivals)

The intra-group-skirmish and FFA walkthroughs are handled competently: falling back to who-shoots-whom clustering, computing baselines *per inferred cluster* rather than per group tag, and explicit UI fallback text when no team split is trustworthy ("no stable teams, treat as individuals") are all correct instincts and match the brief's demand that allegiance be inference-with-confidence, not fact.

What's missing: **allegiance drift over time within one session** is asserted as a brief requirement ("Team membership can change during a session") but the analysis model treats team clustering, and by extension "per-team baselines," as apparently static per-session quantities (section 6's "team-conditioned baselines" language doesn't mention a time axis). If Team A splits into A1/A2 forty minutes in, does the "per-team baseline" recompute retroactively only for the post-split window, or does it silently average pre-split (undivided) and post-split (divided) behaviour into one number? The entry never says, and a mid-session split is exactly the scenario (explicitly called out in the brief) where a time-blind baseline would misattribute behaviour. Likewise, an avatar arriving mid-fight has few qualifying instances; the `≥N` filter correctly excludes them from *contributing to* the baseline, but nothing stops the Watchlist/Matrix from *displaying* a low-n figure for that avatar with the same visual weight as a well-sampled one (see the AwareMix gap above) — mid-fight arrivals are precisely the population most likely to get a spurious flag from a few unlucky BLOCKED verdicts.

## In-world overlay while alt-camming

This is the strongest part of the entry, mechanically. Reusing the plan's depth-tested/depth-off dual-pass rendering for the view cone and LOS fan (so occlusion by walls is directly visible, not asserted by a colour) is the right call, and gating engagement trails to per-avatar toggles (not always-on) shows real awareness of clutter at 100-avatar scale. Two practical concerns for an officer actually alt-camming:

- The Engagement Trail is "for the whole session," drawn as "all of a selected avatar's landed-hit lines... at once." For a high-volume shooter (dozens of hits over 1-2h), that is a dense line fan from a stationary camera; the entry doesn't say whether it's filterable by time window from the overlay itself (only "toggled per-avatar from the Auditor") — an officer who wants "just this skirmish's shots from this avatar" has to go back to a 2D floater to narrow it, breaking the promise that stepping between rungs happens in place.
- The minimap-fairness ring and the awareness glyph and the view cone and the LOS candidate fan can all be live simultaneously on one selected hit; there's no discussion of what happens when two selected avatars' overlays are pinned at once (the pinboard mechanism in the IA suggests officers will want to compare two avatars side by side) — layering rules for multiple simultaneous selections in-world are unaddressed, only single-selection is described.

## What must be kept

- The Wilson-interval Equipment Ledger and the explicit "never labelled wallhacking" documentation-per-signal page — this is the single best piece of uncertainty honesty in the entry and should survive into any merged design.
- The counter-evidence requirement baked into the Avatar Page (every clustered signal must show whether Place/geography explains it) — this is a real, structural anti-witch-hunt mechanism, not just a caveat in prose.
- The LOS travel-time sweep as a literal, clickable slider with a marked flip point, and "sweep candidates in-world" — this is the entry's clearest Bret-Victor-correct move and directly answers Q3 well.
- Per-cluster (not per-group-tag) baseline computation for intra-group skirmishes, and explicit UI fallback text for untrustworthy team splits in FFA.
- The Risks section's self-awareness about the "Signals: 4" scoring risk — correct diagnosis, just needs the fix (see above) actually applied to the screen, not left as a caveat.
