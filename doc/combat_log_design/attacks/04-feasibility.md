# Attack on Entry 04 — The Cartographer (ANALYSIS + FEASIBILITY lens)

## Verdict up front

The entry's own risk section already confesses its two biggest problems ("front/territory math is the riskiest derived quantity," "small multiples are the most XUI-novel piece, unproven"). That candor is a point in its favor, but confessing a risk is not the same as closing it. Several load-bearing quantities in this design have no stated computation path at all, one whole map mode is promised in a wireframe with no analysis-model entry to back it, and the design's glanceable summary numbers ("A holds 58%") violate the brief's own uncertainty mandate at the exact spot where an officer is most likely to just believe the number.

## Fatal

**1. The front-normal axis — the coordinate transform the entire Rung 3 view depends on — has no computation path.**
Section 4.2 states "x = time, y = distance along the front's normal (auto-computed axis; officer can override... if the fight is L-shaped)." No algorithm is given anywhere in the entry for computing this axis automatically. Candidates would be: PCA over the zero-crossing contour points, the vector between the two staging-area centroids, or the gradient direction of `O(A)−O(B)` averaged over the session — but the entry names none of them, and each behaves differently (a bent/L-shaped front, which the entry admits happens, has no single "normal" at all; PCA over a bent line gives a misleading axis, not a warning to fall back to manual). Since the Front Timeline is presented as "the brief's higher rung" and gets its own tab plus small multiples built on top of it (Rung 4), an unspecified axis computation is not a minor gap — it is the load-bearing derived quantity for a quarter of the design's screens, and it is asserted as automatic ("auto-computed") when no path to compute it exists.

**2. The mode row promises a whole-map "Sightline" layer that the analysis model never defines.**
§4.1's mode switcher lists `[Now|Trails|Heat|Flow|Front|Sightline|Duels]` as a field the canvas can paint over the whole map. §6 ("Sightline fan") only defines a *single-vantage* fan: "for a vantage and radius R, sample ring points... cached by (vantage, cursor-time) key," aggregated to cells "for the map layer." That aggregation step is the entire missing piece: to paint a map-wide field you need LOS verdicts for every cell (or a representative sample of shooter/victim pairs) across the visible time window, which means thousands of `lineSegmentIntersectWorldGeometry` calls against world geometry per mode-toggle or per scrub tick, with no incremental/dirty-region strategy analogous to the one given for the heat/flow/territory grid. Heat, flow, and territory each get an explicit incremental-recompute story in §6; Sightline-as-a-map-layer does not, yet it sits in the same always-visible mode row implying the same interactive cost. As specified this layer is either (a) not actually computable at the promised granularity, or (b) silently degrades to "nothing until you click something," which is a different UI than the wireframe shows.

## Serious

**3. Compounded inference rendered as a bare, confident number at the most-trusted glance point in the UI.**
The War Map status line reads "A holds 58% (14:40)" (§4.1) and the Teams tab reads "territory 42%" (§4.4) with no inline uncertainty glyph. Both numbers are a composite of two independently-uncertain quantities: `teamConfidence(avatar,team)` (itself a soft clustering margin) folded into `O(team,cell,t,Δt)`, then thresholded into a percentage. The brief is explicit that "the tool must present allegiance as an inference with confidence, never a hard fact" — but the one artifact every officer will actually read (a one-line status summary) hides that confidence entirely behind a clean-looking percentage. The entry's own §8 risk ("confidence-band rendering must never be skippable/hideable by default, or officers will over-trust a clean-looking line") is about the map's front-line rendering, but the status-line and Teams-tab numbers commit exactly the same sin one layer up, and nothing in §4.1 or §4.4 addresses it.

**4. One visual encoding conflates two different kinds of uncertainty.**
§6 says front-contour confidence is "normalised `|∇(O_A−O_B)|` — steep gradient draws solid, shallow/sparse draws dashed." "Shallow gradient" (genuinely contested ground — a tactical fact) and "sparse" (too few track samples to know — a data gap) are conflated into a single dash pattern. An officer looking at a dashed stretch of front cannot tell "this is real no-man's-land" from "we don't have enough samples here yet (early in the fight, or a coverage gap)." Those two situations call for opposite officer behavior (trust the contested-zone read vs. don't trust the line at all), and the design gives them one indistinguishable glyph.

**5. No raw, filterable event list anywhere in the information architecture — a screen the plan already requires and this entry silently drops.**
The engineering plan's Phase 3.3 specifies an "Events (scroll list, filters by kind/attacker/target/team/type; row select → cursor + inspector)" tab as part of the main floater. This entry's screens (§4) are War Map, Front Timeline, Combat Inspector, Teams & Territory — no Events list. Digging is entirely spatial (click a cell/dot/cross) or wiki-style (follow links). For officer workflows the brief explicitly calls out — "reviewing a promotion candidate," "investigating a reported violation" — an officer typically needs to scan and filter a chronological or attribute-filtered list (e.g., "every hit by weapon X in the last 10 minutes," "every event involving avatar Y") which a map cannot do at all when the relevant events are spatially scattered or the officer doesn't yet know where to look. This is not a stylistic omission; it removes a capability the plan already committed to and that the officer-questions section needs.

**6. Duel-graph node placement ("session-median location") is presented as a settled spatial anchor without checking whether it tracks combat at all.**
§6: "nodes sit at each avatar's session-median location so the graph stays a map, not a force-layout." For an avatar who spends most of the session at a staging area and fights briefly at the front, the median position is the staging area, not the fight — the duel graph would visually place a documented fight far from where it happened, while looking exactly as authoritative as a correctly-placed node. The entry treats this as a design virtue ("keeps the map... instead of a generic network view") without acknowledging the failure mode it introduces. A damage-weighted centroid (median of positions at the *times* the avatar was involved in exchanged damage) would be closer to truthful and is barely more code, but the entry doesn't consider or reject it — it just asserts median-position is fine.

## Minor

**7. DBSCAN constants (ε≈6 m, min-pts≈3) for staging-area detection are asserted with no sensitivity discussion.** The entry's own §8 flags the mislabeling risk (a mid-fight rally point becomes a false "Staging-B"), which is honest, but the parameters themselves are presented as settled numbers rather than as something that will need per-region tuning (open regions vs. cramped combat sims have very different natural clustering radii).

**8. Uncertainty signaling is inconsistent between map and world for the same approximation.** §6 says DAMAGE-position heat draws "desaturated to mark the approximation" (victim's track position substituted for an unknown shot origin) on the map. §5's in-world overlay describes "damage: line from attacker position at t... to target track at t" with no equivalent visual downgrade for the same approximation projected onto the ground. An officer alt-camming in-world sees a normal-looking damage line for data that is, on the map, explicitly marked as approximate.

**9. "Gear-up duration" (median low-velocity dwell before an avatar's first outbound damage event) is undefined for avatars who never deal damage** (support roles, medics, someone who dies before ever attacking). No fallback value or display is specified; the Place page presumably needs to handle N/A gracefully, but the entry doesn't say so.

## What must be kept

- **Front/territory computed from track-based occupancy, not from DAMAGE event positions.** This correctly routes around the brief's stated gap ("DAMAGE has no positions") instead of pretending to solve it — the one place in the analysis model that gets the data-availability constraint exactly right architecturally, even though the front-axis parameter itself (Fatal #1) is unresolved.
- **The Death page's damage build-up list with per-hit LOS verdict and explicit candidate breakdown** ("candidates: t-0.0 BLOCKED · t-0.3 CLEAR · t-0.6 BLOCKED... caveat: travel-time window 1.5s, one clear candidate exists"). This is the honest, non-bare-verdict LOS display the brief demands almost verbatim, and it is the strongest single piece of the entry.
- **Equipment through-wall suspicion always paired with sample size** ("4 of 11 kills, BLOCKED-only, small sample") rather than a bare rate — directly answers the brief's "how confident is that claim per weapon" requirement.
- **The confidence-threshold slider on the Teams tab live-reclustering and greying out flipped avatars on the mini-map.** This is a working, concrete instance of the brief's own named Rung-2 example ("team assignment across confidence thresholds") and should survive any rewrite even if the status-line percentage problem (Serious #3) gets fixed.
- **Manual team overrides recorded as "officer-asserted" and protected from silent re-clustering.** Correct handling of the brief's "let the officer override" requirement — overrides must not evaporate on the next re-cluster.
- **One click-target model across map, list, and world** (same picking contract opens the same Inspector page for a given noun) and **one Inspector panel with a body sub-panel factory keyed by page type** — this is the right XUI-economy move: it turns seven wiki-page types into one `LLPanel` shell plus seven small body renderers, which is squarely inside existing XUI capability (scroll lists, tab containers, custom-drawn sub-panels) rather than inventing new widget machinery.
