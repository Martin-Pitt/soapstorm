# Judge J4 — Buildability and polish

**Lens.** Would this ship in *this* viewer, and would it feel like a high-quality instrument while the officer is parked and alt-camming? I weight **feasibility** (XUI immediate-mode widgets, custom `LLView` drawing, main-thread cost at 2 h / ~100k events / millions of track samples) and **overlay** (in-world UX, encodings, clickability that does not fight alt-cam) most heavily, but every criterion is scored 1–10.

Feasibility notes I checked against the codebase rather than taking on faith: `LLUrlEntry`/`LLUrlRegistry` exist and are the real mechanism for clickable nouns inside any `LLTextBase` (36 `LLUrlEntry` subclasses today); `LLAccordionCtrl` exists; `LLScrollListCtrl` is **flat** — no native tree, so any expand/collapse of instance rows is bespoke row splicing (only entry 07 says this out loud); the `RenderUIBuffer` dirty-rect gotcha in `doc/viewer/ui_system.md` bites animated custom views (only entry 08 names it); the plan's click hook consumes a plain left-click only, which kills any held-click or radial-menu design (only entry 10 reasons this through and drops its own feature over it).

## Per-entry notes

### 01 — Ladder-first (56)

The most implementation-aware entry in the field, and the only one that solves the tool's largest product-level hole: the overlay is gated to stationary + alt-cam, i.e. it is *off* exactly when the officer is walking, re-gearing or briefing. 01 answers with the **Chart renderer** — a 2D top-down `LLView` drawing the overlay's own primitives orthographically, with a stated **parity rule** (anything the overlay draws at rung R has a Chart twin with identical encodings and picking; the world adds only depth, occlusion and a free camera). Its own risk section correctly identifies that parity must be a shared draw-list, not a convention.

Feasibility elsewhere is unusually concrete: a `layout_stack` of six stage panels with one visible, custom views drawing `gl_rect_2d` + `LLFontGL::renderUTF8`, stock scroll lists, an ordinary `LLTextEditor` with a registered `LLUrlEntry` for `secondlife:///app/sscombat/<type>/<id>`. The §6 "Cost and caching" paragraph is the single most important perf statement any entry makes: the hostility graph, per-window damage totals, volleys and coverage are **accumulators updated on event append**, and Matrix/Braid never touch the event vector during a paint tick; raycasting is the only unbounded consumer and it is budgeted at 200/frame filling behind a visible hatch. Degradation is specified rather than hoped for — the Ledger folds past twelve rows with force-promotion of anything consequential, the Matrix collapses to blocks at 41 agents.

Overlay is rung-indexed so pressing `]` changes what the *world* shows; σ drawn as foot-ring radius is a lovely honest encoding (a coarse-located avatar visibly sits in a 4 m disc); the depth-tested/depth-off double draw makes occlusion the through-wall cue. Uncertainty rule "a lone red ray is a lie" is exactly right.

Costs: it is a large build (six stages + article floater + Chart + ribbon + sweep strips), and R−1 needs a plan delta (raw char arena, ~25 MB) which it flags honestly and degrades gracefully without. The anchor/witness invariant is the best answer in the field to "step down must land somewhere named".

### 08 — Allegiance (56)

The best overlay specification here, and the best answer to the officer's actual job. The **LOD-by-camera-distance table** — halo, label, damage lines, trails, LOS fan, ground tint each with ≤40 m / 40–120 m / >120 m behaviour — plus the rule that *each rung owns a distance band* and a one-line HUD caption naming the current regime ("rung 2 — territory; zoom in for bodies") is the only design here that stops three layers fighting for the same pixels during free alt-cam. The **pick-stack list** when >1 marker falls within 12 px is the correct handling of the staging-area heap, and it explicitly routes assertions through it so a click never files against the wrong overlapping avatar.

Analysis depth is extraordinary and, unusually, *itemised*: burst as the evidence unit, trust and `targetIsAgent` admission filters before anything reaches the field, a bounded proximity term with a spatial hash and a hard 20k pair-test/session-second budget plus a `prox-capped` degraded chip, correlation clustering with lineage events and 20 jittered re-solves reporting agreement counts, a structure index against a null model that flips the tool into DEATHMATCH mode, 2.5D front extraction per 4 m vertical level (marching squares + chamfer distance transform) that *refuses to draw* when no contour survives. The Sweep tab's ablation rows with agreement counts, and the "11/20 — inconclusive" outcome, are the honesty engine the brief asks for.

Officer coverage is the field's best: the intra-group case is *discovered, not assumed* (the `STRAINED 41%` header and `(!)` tag-dependence flag arrive unasked, from a second background solve that costs what the first did), and Q9 gets a real artifact — lineage events auto-generate a briefing chapter list where each chapter is a marked View restoring cursor, window and parameters together.

Feasibility is good but this is the biggest build and the most tuning constants in the field, and the entry admits it (`E0`, 3 s gap, `w_type`, 0.08 margin, LOD distances "are guesses until they meet a real raid"). It names the `RenderUIBuffer` dirty-rect gotcha, which nobody else does. Ribbon legibility for a genuinely two-sided 60-agent fight is left unresolved by its own risk section. Ranked below 01 only because the schedule risk is concentrated in analysis machinery that sits off the path the engineering plan already fixes.

### 11 — Comparative (55)

The best *click model* and the most honest self-accounting of drawing cost. Selection resolves on mouse-**up**, only if pointer travel since mouse-down stayed under 4 px and the press was under 350 ms; any drag cancels the pick; any ALT/CTRL/SHIFT-modified press is never consumed. That is the only formulation in the field that provably cannot fight ALT+drag orbit or the pick tool. Ties raise a pick-stack ("3 here: death #47 / Ruen trail / lattice G4") instead of guessing.

It also states its own drawing surface honestly — "four `LLView` subclasses plus **seven paint routines** inside `SSCellView`, roughly 2.5k lines of primitive drawing, not four widgets' worth" — and then bounds it: Grid capped at 60 cells with a never-silently-dropped overflow row, facet membership indexed once per Query change, cell geometry cached and invalidated by cursor/filter change. The text-density note (≈12 chars per cell label at 100 % UI scale; numbers only at 150 %) is exactly the detail that decides whether small multiples are legible in XUI, and no other entry raises it.

Analysis is top-tier. Team clustering swept across θ **and** across time windows separates three things a single whole-session clustering cannot: **drift** (chevron + "changed side at 14:41 ± 1 window"), **noise** (hatch, unclassified), **short sample** (its own badge). The intra-group walkthrough — two cross-line medics hatched as unclassifiable with a 40 % friendly-fire rate whose spread bar covers half the range, versus Tamsin's real defection with stability .84 before and .88 after — is the sharpest demonstration of allegiance drift in the competition. `flip fraction` as the headline instead of a verdict, with `[a clear shot exists]` demoted to a chip because that test is uninformative when true and decisive when false, is the correct statistical framing. The spawn-anchor *test* (φ < 0.5 → no rings, hatched axis) and the equipment key `(creator, root name, attach class, damage signature)` with a permanent UNATTRIBUTED row are both real insights.

Overlay: per-layer caps (≤80 trails, ≤200 damage lines, ≤400 lattice cells, ≤128 fan rays), labels culled past 256 m and thinned to ≥18 px separation, and a corner chip reading `drawing 62 of 214 markers (LOD)` — the officer is told what is hidden. The "wall witness" (1 m normal disc at the blocking surface) is a nice concrete touch.

Cost: the Grid is the most novel widget anyone proposes, and the Query grammar is a lot of scaffolding before the first useful screen exists. Also the densest UI — this one needs a legibility pass at real UI scale.

### 02 — Hypertext (55)

The best information architecture, full stop, and the strongest officer-workflow answer alongside 08. Address grammar `ss:<type>/<id>[@t][?facet]` rendered through a registered `LLUrlEntryBase` subclass so links work in *every* `LLTextBase` — page bodies, tooltips, notifications, chat, notes — is both correct about the mechanism and unusually leveraged. Typed backlinks with **polarity** (`⚑ FLAG`, `✓ CLEAR`, `⇄ OVERRIDE`, `✎ NOTE`) mean a promotion reviewer seeing "3 claims" can tell two are exonerations without opening them; that is a genuinely new capability. Named **trails** as the briefing/dossier artifact, and per-page `(analysis epoch, dep set)` with a visible "recomputed" flash, are both properly thought through.

Overlay contributions I would take regardless of who wins: the **permanent dim session base layer at 25 % alpha** under the focus layer, so an officer who alt-cams somewhere out of curiosity always has geography to click and exploration never requires having navigated first; and **scale classes** (micro <20 m / local 20–120 m / region >120 m) selected each frame from the camera's ground footprint, with pinned layers one class away at 40 % alpha and decimated, two classes away collapsed to a centroid + count, and "drawn 200 of 512" in the figure caption. That is the rule that stops a pinned region-wide shot cloud burying the six-ray LOS fan the officer is leaning in to read. MARGINAL as a first-class LOS verdict — obtained by jittering the deciding candidate by its position error along the two axes perpendicular to the ray — is cheap (4 extra rays) and honest.

Feasibility is decent but this is the entry most exposed to widget cost: long generated `LLTextBase` pages with many URL segments are not free, and it says so, mitigating with lede caps, lazily-built accordions and an LRU of 32 pages. The restraint term (LOS probes as clustering input) is the one analysis choice I would push back on for v1 — bounded at ~10⁴ casts, but its own risk section concedes a CLEAR probe with no damage may mean "did not see", not "chose not to shoot".

### 10 — In-world-first (45)

The strongest *in-world craft* outside the top four, and the entry that reasons best about input precedence: it explicitly drops the click-and-hold radial menu from its own earlier draft because no trigger fix removes the collision with alt-cam drag-to-orbit, the plan's plain-left-click hook, and SL's right-click pie menu — and moves those actions to plain-click chips on the card. That is the correct call and it is verifiable against the codebase.

Billboard dossier cards anchored to a 3D point with leader lines, chip sub-rects held at a fixed minimum screen size regardless of world distance (the same floor nameplate text uses), vertical offset for overlapping cards (the nameplate trick), an 8-card cap by proximity + recency with pinned cards exempt, and a full text mirror in the Inspector so every card action has a camera-independent fallback — this is polished, buildable overlay work. The **evidence triangle** over a ghost's head, carried onto trail heads at rung 1, is a good browse-before-you-suspect affordance that costs one glyph.

Analysis is narrower but honest: reaction time reported as a **window** ("400–600 ms"), floored by the 2 Hz bridge tick, with an explicit note that the number is a product of the track's interpolation model as much as the avatar's behaviour. Events whose attacker has zero track coverage are excluded from the heat field and tallied in a visible "unattributed" counter rather than smeared onto a nearby cell.

Weaknesses: thin ladder (transitions are mostly "click a thing"), no Q9 answer, billboard clutter is the most self-inflicted legibility risk in the field, depth-tested picking precedence unresolved, and heat-field cost at whole-session window is designed but not load-tested.

### 06 — Forensics (45)

Contains the single best cheap idea in the competition: the **occlusion point cloud**. There is no wall-geometry store anywhere in the plan — only a boolean raycast — so instead of inventing a wall silhouette, every BLOCKED raycast deposits its already-returned hit point into a session spatial index, and the micro-replay draws that as a sparse scatter. Chokepoints accrete a recognisable shape for free, untested ground stays honestly sparse. That is zero new cost and strictly more honest than a drawn wall.

Two other fixes are load-bearing and correct. **LOS candidate positions branch by equipment class** — handheld uses the owner's track, a deployable uses the deployable's own track, a mount/vehicle uses the mount's track rather than the seated avatar's offset position — closing the gap where turrets and traps, the paradigm through-wall case, had no computation path at all. And **bucketed clustering** (2-min buckets, matching the front-line buckets) makes team drift and per-bucket friendly-fire real, with flags that state when the neighbouring bucket disagrees. The node graph explicitly does not re-lay-out when the confidence slider drags — colour only — which is a small but genuine polish/perf detail.

Attribution as ranked raw damage totals tagged dominant / contested / long-shot, instead of a manufactured 72/18/10 split, is the right call. Overlay defaults to **Focus mode** (trails only for the selection plus one team-hop) with a "Show all trails" toggle — the correct default for 20–30 avatars.

Weaknesses: overlay spec is comparatively thin (no LOD table, no pick disambiguation, no caps), and the entry openly leaves the Docket/briefing workflow unspecified — a real gap since the brief names Q9 explicitly.

### 04 — Cartographer (45)

Good spatial thinking, mid-tier feasibility. The N-team occupancy generalisation is a strict generalisation of the two-team zero-crossing rather than a rewrite, which is the right way to extend a model. Separating **margin** (contested — a tactical fact, encoded as band width) from **coverage** (data density — encoded as dash pattern) into two independent visual channels is a clean fix to a real conflation, and the PCA-with-explained-variance front axis that auto-splits into per-segment ribbons at `r<2` is honest about when one axis is a lie. Evidence-scaled team confidence (`margin × min(1, edges/EVIDENCE_FLOOR)`, hatched below the floor) is a good, cheap correction. The Staging vs **Arrival point** split is a genuinely useful distinction for mid-raid teleport-ins.

The polish detail I would keep: **"Show in-world" always draws a small always-on-top 2D compass ribbon** with bearing and distance ("↗ 340 m — the ramp") independent of the overlay's gating, so the button never silently does nothing when the officer is 300 m away or walking.

Feasibility is the weakest of the mid-tier: a quadtree over 100k events with dirty-cell tracking is designed but unprofiled, and the ground-level LOD re-tiling (1.5 m leaf within ~30 m of camera, coarsening to 4 m, blended at the transition) adds a *second* cost surface on top of it, which its own risk section admits. The small-multiples panels are flagged as the most XUI-novel piece and are now used for one more thing. No Q9 answer.

### 05 — Timeline (44)

Solid, modest, buildable. The **attacker position tiers** (TRACK / LAST-SEEN / APPROX-VIA-OWNER) are the right shape for a real gap — the fixed per-avatar track store cannot resolve turret/deployable/mount attackers — and the entry is careful that this is an honest approximation, not parity: tier-3 positions are excluded from through-wall tallies by default (opt-in, clearly labelled) so a guessed position never launders a weapon's suspicion score, and the **hollow diamond** marker means an approximate attacker position never looks precise in world. Adding an **Object** noun and a collapsed Objects swimlane band closes a gap nobody else noticed: destroyed turrets and vehicles had no page anywhere.

The single best feasibility catch in the entry: the overlay trail window is `min(current zoom window, SSCombatLogTrailSeconds)`, not the raw zoom, with a caption when the cap bites ("trail: 20 s (capped; timeline window is 6 m)") — the naive 1:1 coupling would ask the overlay to draw hours of trail. `K_space` (cheap flood-fill over the contact-map grid) forking the phase ribbon rather than averaging multiple fronts into one weak-confidence "Front" band is the right refusal.

Weaknesses: swimlane scaling at 40+ avatars is admitted to need tuning against a synthetic session rather than being specified; the contact-map grid resolution is a stated tradeoff the fork mechanism depends on; the friendly-fire "assignment as of time t" dependency is flagged as *blocking* against the fixed plan, which is honest but unresolved. Overlay is competent rather than ambitious. No Q9.

### 12 — Fairness (43)

Narrow scope, high integrity inside it, and one insight worth stealing outright: **re-run every LOS sweep at `t−1 s` and `t+1 s`** and label the verdict ROBUST if it survives, else show both possibilities. The brief states log events can lag the real hit by up to 1 s — a margin comparable to the entire travel window everyone else sweeps — and 12 is the only entry that treats the event's own timestamp as a swept parameter rather than an anchor. Also correct: one Fairness sub-tab per *contributing* DAMAGE event, not just the killing blow.

Removing the summed "Signals" column and turning the threshold into a **filter, not a rank** (rows ordered by name or one clicked column's own count, never a sum) is a discipline other entries would benefit from. The overlay's measured-vs-modelled encoding — eye and broken-wall solid, ear (audible-cue guess) hollow/dashed — is the right instinct. It honestly names that its LOS-ray fan needs **thin-line-segment picking** (distance-to-projected-segment with pixel tolerance), an explicit addition to the plan's rect-per-marker pick list rather than something that falls out for free.

Feasibility is fine and modest: per-avatar per-signal counts maintained incrementally so the slider re-filters a cached table; whole-roster trail mode is an *exclusive* overlay mode replacing per-avatar trails with decimated polylines (one vertex per direction change or 5 s) to stay inside the plan's budget — and it flags that 100 avatars still exceeds the 60-avatar assumption.

Weaknesses: the ladder and IA are bolted to one theme; Q1 is answered adequately but Q2/Q6/Q7/Q9 are thin. Front/phase detection on death-only positions is the sparsest signal here and is used as counter-evidence.

### 07 — Equipment (42)

Two things I want in the synthesis. First, the **solid-vs-dashed honesty rule applied everywhere a position appears**: DEATH events use real `source_pos`/`target_pos` and draw solid at full alpha; every other event — the overwhelming majority of the log — has no ground-truth muzzle or hit position, so the line is reconstructed from `sampleAt` and draws **dashed at 60 % alpha** with a "reconstructed" tag on hover. Second, the **ground-projected shadow decal**: each accumulated Engagement Field point drops a faint dot on the terrain beneath it, so a density field stays readable from an oblique alt-cam angle without fighting foreshortened 3D lines. That is a real in-world rendering insight and cheap.

It is also the only entry that catches that `LLScrollListCtrl` is flat and that family/instance expand-collapse must be bespoke row splicing — verified correct. The classification checklist with **partial credit** (2 of 3 instances bridge-confirmed → 17 of 25) and a **priced, visible** HUD-attach-point override (`−10` penalty, never silent) is good evidence UI. The position-source conflict rule (prefer the moving `source` object's own track over the owner's, tag divergence >5 m as "conflicting tracks, showing source") is a detail others miss.

Weaknesses: the ladder is thin and mostly "views with clicks"; the IA deliberately refuses a Place noun (defended, but it costs digging depth); no Q9; the Engagement Field is a late patch on an equipment-first frame rather than a native rung.

### 09 — Progressive (42)

The most shippable entry and the smallest build — a fixed ~340×120 main floater that is pure transport chrome plus a one-line headline, and an Inspector showing exactly one page. If schedule were the only axis this would rank far higher, and its restraint is a real virtue: three index pages (Event / Equipment / Suspicion) reusing one widget with three column sets close the cold-start gaps for Q3/Q4/Q7/Q8 at almost no cost.

Two ideas worth keeping. The **auto-pinned discarded branch**: popping to a mid-stack breadcrumb segment and pushing somewhere different auto-pins the abandoned forward branch labelled by its leaf noun, so a single linear stack no longer silently eats a sub-investigation — one sentence teaches the whole model. And the **specified colour fallback**: Range mode uses team colour only while the session-wide top-two cluster margin exceeds 15 %, and below that switches to the per-avatar name-tag palette, so the overlay never asserts a split the data does not support. Team clustering is given an actual objective (minimise intra-cluster damage weight, Kernighan-Lin-style local search) that is *aligned with* the intra-group case rather than fighting it, plus time-bounded overrides `(avatar, team, effective_from)`.

Weaknesses under my lens: the overlay is claimed as "the primary display surface" but is specified more thinly than 08/11/02/01 — no LOD table, no per-layer caps beyond decimated polylines, pick disambiguation by repeat-click cycling (a learned interaction, and the weakest of the three disambiguation schemes proposed). The front is a perpendicular-bisector approximation, honestly labelled but a real fidelity loss. No side-by-side comparison without pins, admitted. No Q9.

## Ranking

1. **01** — best feasibility engineering and the only entry that solves the gating hole (Chart parity). Anchor/witness makes step-down deterministic *and* lossless.
2. **08** — best overlay spec (LOD-by-distance, rung-owns-a-band) and best officer coverage; ranked second only because it is the largest and most constant-heavy build.
3. **11** — best pick model and the most honest accounting of its own drawing cost; θ×time clustering separates drift from noise from small sample better than anyone.
4. **02** — best IA and the base-layer/scale-class overlay rules; most exposed to text-widget cost.
5. **10** — strongest in-world craft outside the top four and correct about input precedence; thin ladder, no Q9.
6. **06** — the occlusion point cloud and equipment-aware LOS sourcing are keepers; thin overlay spec, Q9 explicitly punted.
7. **04** — good spatial model, two unprofiled cost surfaces (quadtree + ground LOD re-tiling).
8. **05** — position tiers and the trail-window cap are real fixes; competent but unambitious.
9. **12** — the ±1 s timestamp robustness check is worth stealing; scope too narrow to win.
10. **07** — reconstructed-vs-measured drawing rule and ground shadows are keepers; weak ladder/IA.
11. **09** — most shippable, least ambitious; overlay under-specified for an entry that claims the world as its primary surface.

## Grafts for the synthesis

1. **(01) Chart renderer with a parity rule.** A 2D top-down `LLView` drawing the overlay's own primitives orthographically, with identical encodings and screen-rect picking at every rung. The overlay is gated off exactly when the officer is walking, re-gearing or briefing; "where did the fighting happen" must not become unanswerable for that reason. Enforce parity as a shared draw-list, not a convention.
2. **(08) LOD by camera distance, one rung per distance band, with a caption.** A per-layer table for ≤40 m / 40–120 m / >120 m (halo, label, damage lines, trails, LOS fan, ground tint), so three layers never fight for the same pixels during free alt-cam, plus a one-line HUD caption naming the current regime. A view that silently drops glyphs is a lying view.
3. **(11) The pick model.** Selection resolves on mouse-**up**, only if pointer travel stayed under ~4 px and the press under ~350 ms; any drag cancels; ALT/CTRL/SHIFT-modified presses are never consumed. Ties raise a pick-stack list at the cursor. Pair with **(08)**'s rule that an *assertion* must route through the stack list so it can never be filed against the wrong overlapping avatar.
4. **(01 + 11) One perf contract.** Hostility graph, volleys, per-window damage and coverage are accumulators updated on event append; no paint tick ever touches the event vector. All raycasting shares one budget (~200/frame) with priority (selected > visible rows > background), a visible "computing" hatch, a cancel, and LRU memoisation keyed on positions quantised to 1 m — avatars stand still, so the hit rate is high. Invalidate on track backfill.
5. **(06) Occlusion point cloud.** Persist every BLOCKED raycast's already-returned hit point into a session spatial index and draw it as a sparse scatter in the micro-replay and in world. Free wall geometry from work already done; chokepoints accrete a recognisable shape; untested ground stays honestly sparse. Never draw a wall the tool has no data for.
6. **(11 + 06) Allegiance over time, three states not one.** Cluster per time window and sweep θ; report **drift** (chevron plus a printed change time) separately from **noise** (hatch, unclassified) separately from **short sample** (its own badge). Friendly-fire flags are per-bucket facts that state when the neighbouring bucket disagrees. This is the only formulation that handles the brief's "team membership can change during a session" without blending or discarding it.
7. **(12) Sweep the event timestamp, not just the travel window.** Re-run each LOS verdict at `t−1 s` and `t+1 s` — the log's own stated lag — and label it ROBUST or show both verdicts. Nothing else in the field treats the anchor of the sweep as itself uncertain, and the lag is the same magnitude as the window being swept.
8. **(02) Permanent dim base layer plus scale classes.** Draw the whole-session figure at ~25 % alpha under the focus layer so free alt-camming always has geography to click and bare ground opens its cell. Classify every figure micro / local / region, select the in-scale class from the camera's ground footprint each frame, decimate pinned layers one class away and collapse them two classes away, and print "drawn 200 of 512" in the caption. Combine with **(11)**'s corner LOD chip so the officer is always told what is hidden.
9. **(07 + 10) Measured versus reconstructed, drawn differently, everywhere.** DEATH positions solid at full alpha; DAMAGE lines rebuilt from `sampleAt` dashed at ~60 % with a "reconstructed" tag; heat cells weighted by COARSE/stale samples blurred and desaturated; and any event whose attacker has *no* track coverage goes into a visible "unattributed" tally instead of being smeared onto a nearby cell. Add **(07)**'s ground-projected shadow dot under each field point so density stays readable from an oblique alt-cam angle.
10. **(01) Anchor and witness.** Ascending writes the destination's anchor to the instance you came from, so stepping down returns *that* instance, not a lookalike; arriving cold lands on the aggregate's medoid **witness** with extremes on the arrow keys; and the rail prints the exact landing, labelled `(back)` or `(witness)`, before the key is pressed. Pair with **(08)**'s `[sweep this]` affordance on any uncertainty badge or cell, so climbing to the parameter rung always starts from the specific reading you distrust.
