# Judge J3 — Analysis rigor and honesty under freeform combat

**Lens.** How well does each entry hold up when combat refuses to be tidy: allegiance that drifts or never forms, attribution over a laggy log with no positions on DAMAGE, LOS verdicts poisoned by bullet travel time, damage adjustments, and a behaviour audit that produces evidence rather than a witch hunt. Analysis is weighted most heavily; every criterion is still scored 1–10, total = sum (max 60).

A note that applies to the whole field: the brief asks for ~1500–3000 words. Nine of eleven entries overshoot, several by 2×. I have not scored that differentially because it is near-universal, but the longest entries (01 at 6.4k, 08 at 6.8k) buy their depth partly by ignoring a stated constraint, and a synthesis will have to cut.

## Per-entry notes

### 01 — Ladder-first (55)

The only entry where the ladder is load-bearing rather than described. Six rungs with named operators, and two invariants that actually solve the problems the brief poses: **up-then-down is the identity** (ascending writes an anchor, `[` returns the instance you came from, and a cold entry lands on the aggregate's medoid *witness* with the rail saying which you are about to get), and the **haze rule** (no aggregate drawn without its unknown fraction in the same mark).

On my lens it is the broadest-and-deepest analysis section in the field:

- **Ordering honesty.** Every event carries `t̂ ± u` (0.05 s fd-confirmed, 0.15 s script-stamped, 1.0 s receive-time fallback) and *no ordering claim is made between two events whose intervals overlap*. The killing blow is only named when its margin exceeds combined uncertainty; otherwise the article says "ambiguous between X and Y", both are drawn, and equipment kill credit splits and hatches. Nobody else handles the log-lag-versus-attribution collision this squarely.
- **Non-circular team seeding.** Group-id seeds are admitted or refused *before* clustering, by three stated tests on the graph's vertex set; in the intra-group case test (a) fails, seeding disables itself, and the header names the failed test. Clustering also runs unseeded and the unseeded result wins on >10% disagreement.
- **LOS as a function, not a scalar.** `V(τ)` over the travel window; the 8 px sweep miniature replaces every scalar badge, so flip-flopping is visible before a word is read. COARSE candidates return UNKNOWN by rule. Per-weapon through-wall is a Wilson interval with UNKNOWN counted separately: "67% [21–94%] · n=3", never "kills through walls".
- **Behaviour without the witch hunt.** Awareness lead is yaw-only, must be swept over θ before it may be cited, and gets a free track-only prefilter before it spends a raycast. Reaction interval is reported as a percentile of the session's own distribution, never against an absolute threshold, and greyed where the stamp is weak.
- **Bout placement.** Position-less DAMAGE is placed at the *target's* interpolated position with the reasoning stated (bouts are where people are being hit; midpoints invent locations nobody stood in), and the choice is a labelled toggle inside the segmentation sweep.
- **Segmentation constants are themselves sweepable**, and a bout whose membership shifts under a 20% ε nudge gets a boundary-stability chip. Structure that exists at one parameter value is not quoted as fact.

Two structural wins beyond analysis. The **Chart renderer** — a 2D top-down twin of the overlay with a stated parity rule — exists precisely because the overlay is gated off exactly when an officer is walking or briefing; "where did the fighting happen" never becomes unanswerable for a UI reason. And the R2 **claim list per noun**, with one mapping rule from noun × rung, is what welds the wiki to the ladder without per-noun improvisation.

Deductions: R−1 needs a plan delta (raw byte arena) — admitted, with a stated degradation to "reconstructed, not raw". Two renderers is a real maintenance-drift risk it names but does not fully solve. Cost model is credible (accumulators on append, ~1 MB of hostility cache, 200 casts/frame budget).

### 08 — Allegiance (54)

The deepest treatment of the lens's first-named concern, and it earns it. Combat is a signed time-decaying field; teams are *solved*, never stored.

The standout is the **background tag-free second solve**: every snapshot is solved twice, with and without the group-tag prior, and disagreement lights a `(!)` on the band header next to a **STRAINED nn%** meter (share of the band's internal pair weight that is negative). An officer who has never heard the phrase "intra-group skirmish" is told in the default view that RED is internally hostile and that its shape depends on tags. That is the brief's hardest case discovered *unasked*, at the cost of one more sub-millisecond solve on ≤100 nodes. Nothing else in the field does this.

Other analysis strengths: bursts as the evidence unit (a stray splash tick cannot carry an execution's weight); two admission filters before anything reaches the field (untrusted events weighted **zero** with `+untrusted` as a sweep axis; `targetIsAgent` false never contributes — shooting a wall is not a social act); spawn-cohort affinity **conditional on the raid having a respawn signal**, with an actual detection test and a `no-respawn` chip in a degraded-terms strip when it fails; proximity-under-fire given a stated bounded algorithm, a hard budget, a `prox-capped` chip and first place in the ablation list because it is the weakest term; `S − S_null` against 20 random partitions driving a DEATHMATCH mode where the tool stops naming factions and the Ribbon becomes a hostility heat strip that scales to 80 rows; confidence split into **margin** (conflicted → stripe) and **evidence mass** (ignorant → hatch) because they mean different things; 20 jittered re-solves giving every lineage event an agreement count, with "11/20 — inconclusive, not enough evidence to test this" as a first-class outcome; front extraction per 4 m vertical level via marching squares plus chamfer distance, refusing to draw when no contour survives; intra-side hostility scored by four tests with a leave-one-out aftermath that admits it is *still* correlated with the term it adjudicates.

The overlay is the best in the field on encoding: the **allegiance halo** as posterior wedges (a 60/40 avatar is literally two-tone, a thin-evidence avatar is a dashed grey ring), and an LOD table where **each rung owns a camera-distance band** with a HUD caption naming the current regime, so three layers never fight for the same pixels and a view that drops glyphs says so. The pick **stack list** at ≤12 px ambiguity, and recording which route filed an assertion, is exactly right for the staging-area heap.

Deductions. Feasibility is the field's most ambitious: proximity engine, double solve every 10 s, 20 jittered re-solves, per-level front extraction, null-model structure index. It itemises costs honestly rather than hand-waving, which is why it is 7 and not 5. Analysis coverage tilts hard social: attribution/killing-blow ordering is standard, through-wall per equipment is a Kit-page tally rather than an interval, and adjustments are a per-side fairness fact (good framing, moderate depth). Calibration constants are, as it says, guesses.

### 02 — Hypertext (54)

Best information architecture in the competition and, tied with 01 and 11, the best statistical honesty.

Three ideas I would take unchanged. **Two kinds of number, never mixed**: quantities with a real denominator print as intervals with n; anything from clustering or classification prints as an ordinal band (FIRM/LIKELY/WEAK/UNDETERMINED), and no two-decimal clustering margin ever appears in a lede, because an officer will quote "0.81" in a violation report as if it were a probability. **MARGINAL** as a third LOS outcome: jitter the deciding candidate by its own position error along the two axes perpendicular to the ray; agree → FIRM, disagree → MARGINAL, and through-wall rates are computed over FIRM verdicts only with MARGINAL/UNKNOWN counted beside them and "resolved 128/512" narrowing live. **Flip points in the lede**, not behind a facet — free to compute because the sweep grid *is* the candidate grid — so a skimming officer gets "BLOCKED for travel < 0.9 s; CLEAR at or beyond it (5 of 6)" without opening anything.

Also strong: the hedging rule that hedges *exactly* when >1 owner's damage window (plus log lag) covers the killing blow, with the explicit argument that hedging uncontested attributions trains officers to ignore hedges; restraint probes bounded to co-presence candidate pairs at ≤24 per pair with a 32-ray/frame idle queue, entering clustering only as a refinement after hostility and co-movement have already produced a partition; pre-aim fraction whose domain is *exactly* pairs where a hit later landed, never all-pairs; typed officer claims with polarity (`⚑ FLAG`, `✓ CLEAR`, `⇄ OVERRIDE`, `✎ NOTE`) so a promotion reviewer seeing "3 claims" can tell two are exonerations without opening them. The **band** noun (transient local alliance, damage-edge counting only, never fed to clustering, explicitly a lead) is the right answer to "did those three gang up on him for two minutes" in an FFA.

The risk section is the most self-aware in the field, including the admission that confidence bands hide the problem rather than solve it because the session contains no ground truth to calibrate against.

Deductions: the three-axis rail is defensible and honest (a Death page has two WHO subjects; a Model has a real WHO step-up) but the vertical/horizontal discipline is looser than 01's, and "stepping up = following a link to a coarser noun" makes the ladder more taxonomic than representational. Auto-generated prose reads as authority; the lint-test proposal is the right mitigation but is a proposal. Event addresses do not travel between officers, acknowledged as open.

### 11 — Comparative (53)

One query grammar (`facet × cellView × sweep`) where a breadcrumb chip, a pin, a link and a compare slot are all literally a `Query`. Coherent to an unusual degree, and the analysis is first-rate.

The sharpest single statistical insight in the field: for through-wall claims the headline is **flip fraction and coverage**, and the existential result is demoted to a small `[a clear shot exists]` chip *because that test is uninformative when true and decisive when false*. "No clear line at any τ" is the actual wall-kill claim; everything else is instability. Paired with the τ × 3 aim points × 2 eye heights grid (≤78 casts) this is the most honest LOS treatment written here.

Second: clustering run across **θ and across time windows** yields two diagnostics a single whole-session clustering cannot distinguish — **drift** (modal cluster changes between adjacent windows while stability is high on both sides → chevron and a printed change time) versus **noise** (low stability inside the window → hatch, no chevron) — with sample size as its own `short` badge so a late arrival's low n is never read as ambiguity. Edge weights normalised per avatar by tracked-and-armed seconds, so a 25-minute arrival is judged on rate not volume.

Third: **equipment identity as a security problem.** Names are user-editable and spoofable, so the key is (creator, root name, attach-point class, damage-type signature), collisions get a chip and an officer merge/split, and excluded kills go into a permanent UNATTRIBUTED row with its own interval rather than being hidden or laundered into a named weapon.

Also: front field as an inference over an inference, swept θ×σ with sign-disagreement hatching and stated undefined conditions; spawn anchor **tested** (relocation fraction φ ≥ 0.5 over ≥8 decided deaths) before the axis is offered, otherwise no rings and hatched charts; adjustments as a waterfall per script showing the distribution; behaviour metrics coarse-to-fine (≤26 casts/event) on a 200/frame pool with LRU memoisation on 1 m-quantised positions; flags carrying disposition, initials and timestamp into the debrief text.

Deductions: feasibility. It states honestly that this is four `LLView` subclasses plus seven paint routines ≈ 2.5k lines of primitive drawing, and the Grid's cognitive load is real — a grammar this expressive can produce nonsense queries, and the "12 of 19" overflow row is a patch on a genuine scaling limit. It is also the entry most likely to leave a novice officer on rung 3 forever, which the brief explicitly warns against.

### 06 — Forensics (46)

Hero-screen design done well, with three honesty fixes I would take: **ranked raw damage totals** tagged dominant (>2× runner-up) / contested / long-shot instead of a manufactured 72/18/10 split from an unstated formula (the internal weighting is disclosed and used only to pick the killing-blow highlight); the **occlusion point cloud**, which plots the already-computed hit points from BLOCKED raycasts as a sparse scatter instead of inventing a wall silhouette the tool has no geometry store for — chokepoints accrete for free and untested ground stays honestly sparse; and the rule that a **BLOCKED ray draws red only when at least one dense (VIEWER-quality) sample was tested**, otherwise grey regardless of the raycast result.

Team clustering runs per 2-minute bucket, so an avatar's team history is a sequence and a friendly-fire flag is a bucket-local fact that says when the neighbouring bucket disagrees. LOS candidate positions branch by equipment kind (handheld → owner track; deployable → the object's own track; mount → the mount's track, not the seated owner's offset position) — the turret case, which is the paradigm through-wall case, finally has a computation path. The reaction check distinguishes "checked, nothing anomalous" from "insufficient track density to assess", so a coarse-tracked avatar never silently reads as cleared.

Deductions: through-wall rate has a denominator but no interval; no ordering/log-lag treatment beyond the fd note; FFA degrades to working avatar-by-avatar, which is honest but thin; and the docket/briefing workflow (Q9) is explicitly deferred, which is a real hole in an entry whose whole premise is building a case.

### 12 — Fairness (45)

The entry most directly on the "behaviour audit without witch hunts" half of my lens, and it does that part better than anyone.

Its unique analysis contribution is the **±1 s timestamp robustness check**: the brief says log events can lag the real hit by up to 1 s, which is a margin comparable to the travel window being swept, so every awareness verdict is re-run at `t−1 s` and `t+1 s` and labelled ROBUST or shown with both possible verdicts. Only 12 noticed that the sweep is anchored to a number that is itself uncertain.

The anti-witch-hunt machinery is the best here: no summed "Signals" column and the threshold is a **filter, not a rank**, with rows ordered by name or by one clicked column's own count — never a sum; `thin` badges below n=8 excluded from ranking; Wilson interval printed *before* the point estimate; a **Signal documentation page** per signal type carrying definition, formula, known false-positive causes and the live instance list; a baseline exclusion audit trail that cannot be cleared without a logged reason; minimap-range consistency reclassified from exonerating to symmetric-and-hedged; the ambush case handled explicitly as "no reaction cue available" rather than a fabricated number; per-contributor fairness sweeps so a death is not collapsed to its killing blow. Overlay: measured glyphs (eye, wall) solid, the modelled one (ear) hollow/dashed — ground truth and guess must not look equally certain.

Deductions are scope. Allegiance is delegated to "the plan's clustering module unchanged", so intra-group and FFA get a sentence each and drift is handled only as baseline re-segmentation. Attribution and equipment classification are essentially absent. Front/phase detection runs on DEATH positions only and it admits a 1–2 death "front" sits in the same UI chrome as a 20-death one. Officer coverage is narrow: Q2/Q6/Q7 are thin, Q7 effectively absent.

### 05 — Timeline (45)

Two real contributions. **Attacker position tiers** — TRACK / LAST-SEEN / APPROX-VIA-OWNER — with the tier printed beside every LOS verdict and damage line, a hollow-diamond marker in world for anything below TRACK, and tier-3 positions **excluded from through-wall tallies by default** so a guessed position never launders a weapon's suspicion score. That is the cleanest answer in the field to "who was the attacker when the attacker is a turret". And **object targets** get a noun, a page and a swimlane band — destroyed vehicles and turrets previously had nowhere to live.

`K_space` (flood-fill over the contact-map density) driving a **fork** in the phase ribbon instead of one blended low-confidence "Front" band is good honesty: the fork itself is the model-appropriateness signal, so there is no second "is this the right model" number to misread. The participant-count line under the density sparkline distinguishes an arrivals-driven rise from an escalation among the same people — a small fix for a real ambiguity. The reaction-time signal is demoted and captioned as measuring the *tool's* visibility model, not the suspect's client.

Deductions: LOS mechanics are the plan's default (CLEAR if any candidate clear) with no sweep formalism or interval; through-wall has a denominator but no CI; allegiance drift is handled only by arrival carets, a looseness slider and a flagged dependency on "assignment as of time t" that it correctly identifies as not yet promised by the plan; damage adjustments are one line. Honest about all of it, which is why it scores where it does.

### 10 — In-world (44)

By some distance the best pure overlay UX. Billboard cards anchored in 3D with chip sub-rects at a fixed minimum screen hit size, leader lines to the ghost's feet, an 8-card cap with vertical offsetting borrowed from nameplates, dual-pass depth so a BLOCKED verdict is *seen* as a wall rather than asserted, and a well-argued rejection of its own earlier click-and-hold radial menu because no trigger fix removes the collision with alt-cam drag-to-orbit and SL's right-click pie menu. The Inspector as a full **action mirror** (not just a text mirror) means nothing depends on the camera reaching a pinned card.

Analysis is the weak half. The behaviour chips are excellent — three independent signals never summed, and reaction reported as a *window* ("400–600 ms") floored by the 2 Hz tick with an explicit note that the number is a product of the interpolation model as much as the avatar's behaviour. The heat field excludes events whose attacker had no track and tallies them in a visible "unattributed" counter instead of smearing them onto a nearby cell. But team detection is an assertion that mutual damage will outweigh the shared seed, there is no allegiance-over-time model at all (the intra-group case gets sub-clusters but no time axis), LOS has a fan but no sweep formalism or interval, and adjustments are a mini-bar on a card. Q9 is absent.

### 09 — Progressive (42)

The most buildable entry, and it earns its feasibility 9 honestly: fixed 340×120 controller, one content pane, everything collapsed by default, and a clear-eyed note that windowed reclustering is a recurring background pass that must be scheduled off the render path.

Its best analysis contribution is giving team clustering **an actual objective**: partition to minimise total intra-cluster damage weight, KL-style local search, seeded from group id where present — with the argument stated that this is aligned with the intra-group scenario in a way plain label propagation on signed weights is not. Time-bounded overrides stored as `(avatar, team, effective_from)` treat "she defected at minute 40" as representable instead of forcing a session-wide verdict. The auto-pinned discarded branch ("the breadcrumb is linear, but branching off it leaves a pin behind") is a genuinely teachable one-sentence navigation model. The Range-mode colour fallback has a *numeric* trigger (top-two cluster margin below 15% → per-avatar palette) rather than a vibe.

Deductions: front-unbending is a perpendicular bisector, honestly labelled as an approximation but a real fidelity loss; LOS and through-wall are the plan's defaults; adjustments are one line; Q9 absent; and the single-visible-page discipline costs side-by-side comparison, which it admits.

### 04 — Cartographer (42)

Good spatial thinking with two fixes I would keep: **contestedness and data coverage as two independent visual channels** (band width vs dash pattern), so a wide solid band is a stalemate and a wide dashed band means "don't trust this line yet" — the conflation it fixes is one most entries still carry; and **evidence-scaled team confidence** (`margin × min(1, edgeWeight/FLOOR)`) so an avatar with a clean-looking margin but two recorded exchanges renders hatched rather than solid. The Staging-vs-Arrival-point split (a cluster is only Staging if members dwell and someone's first outbound damage originates nearby) is a nice guard against mislabelling mid-raid teleport-ins. The bearing/distance compass strip so "Show in-world" never silently does nothing is a small, correct UX honesty fix.

Deductions on my lens: LOS is candidate-list-with-caveat, no sweep, no interval, no log-lag treatment; damage adjustments barely appear (Q5 essentially unanswered); no volley/burst concept; friendly-fire semantics are stated but not developed; Q9 absent. The N-team occupancy generalisation is a genuine improvement, but the PCA-plus-corner-split front axis is, as it says, a heuristic — and it is the entry's most load-bearing derived quantity.

### 07 — Equipment (41)

Sound within its frame. Best bits: classification confidence as a weighted checklist computed **once over family-aggregated evidence** with partial credit shown (2 of 3 bridge confirms = 17 of 25) so the lines actually sum, plus a *priced* HUD-attach-point override (`−10 penalty`, visible) rather than a silent one; the **position-source conflict rule** (prefer the `source` object's own track over the wielder's, tag the marker "conflicting tracks, showing source" when they diverge >5 m); the splash-radius estimate with `n=` volleys and an "insufficient data" floor below n=3; and the discipline that only DEATH events have real positions, so everything else draws dashed at 60% alpha with a "reconstructed" tag — applied everywhere a position appears, not just in one panel. The allegiance timeline (rolling 5-min clustering, margin defined, "insufficient data" under 3 interactions, friendly fire keyed to the interval active at the hit's timestamp) is a solid drift model.

It is also the most honest entry about XUI: it flags that `LLScrollListCtrl` is flat and family expand/collapse is bespoke row-splicing, not a native tree.

Deductions: no LOS sweep formalism or interval; team detection is derivative; the Engagement Field is a welcome backdrop but deliberately not a destination, which keeps the IA thin; Q9 absent; and the ground-projected shadow decal, while clever for oblique alt-cam reading, is the one place it adds primitives without a stated cap beyond the global 2,000.

## Ranking

2. **08** — the field's best allegiance model and best overlay encoding; discovers the intra-group case unasked. Costs the most to build.
3. **02** — best IA and the best statistical display discipline; the ladder is taxonomic rather than representational.
4. **11** — the sharpest single insight on through-wall claims and the best drift-vs-noise diagnostic; heaviest custom drawing surface.
5. **06** — excellent attribution and micro-forensics honesty; bucketed teams; Q9 deferred.
6. **12** — best anti-witch-hunt machinery and the only ±1 s timestamp robustness check; narrow officer coverage.

## Grafts for a synthesis

1. **(08)** Run every team solve **twice — with and without the group-tag prior** — in the background, and light a `(!)` tag-dependence flag plus a `STRAINED nn%` internal-hostility meter on the band header. The intra-group skirmish is then discovered by an officer who never suspected it, at the cost of one extra sub-millisecond solve on ≤100 nodes.
2. **(01)** Decide **group-tag seed admission before clustering**, from the graph's vertex set alone, by three explicit tests; when a test fails, disable seeding and *name the failed test* in the header. Run unseeded in parallel and prefer the unseeded result when the partitions disagree by >10%. This removes the circularity that makes the same-tag case silently wrong.
3. **(11)** Make **flip fraction + coverage** the headline for any LOS claim, and demote the existential result to a small `[a clear shot exists]` chip — uninformative when true, decisive when false. "No clear line at any τ" is the only real wall-kill claim.
4. **(12)** Re-run every LOS/awareness verdict at **`t−1 s` and `t+1 s`** (the log's own stated lag, independent of the travel window) and label it ROBUST or show both verdicts. A "robust CLEAR" is a materially stronger claim than one that flips under a 1 s nudge, and the brief guarantees that nudge exists.
5. **(02)** Adopt **MARGINAL** as a first-class LOS outcome by jittering the deciding candidate by its own position error perpendicular to the ray, and compute per-weapon through-wall rates over **FIRM verdicts only**, with MARGINAL/UNKNOWN counted beside them and "resolved n/N" narrowing live as the background queue drains.
6. **(01)** Carry **`t̂ ± u` per event** and make no ordering claim between overlapping intervals: the killing blow is named only when its margin beats combined uncertainty, otherwise "ambiguous between X and Y", both drawn, kill credit split and hatched. Pair with **(06)**'s ranked raw damage totals tagged dominant/contested/long-shot instead of a manufactured percentage split.
7. **(05 + 06 + 10)** Give every attacker a **tiered position**: TRACK / LAST-SEEN(object's own registry position) / APPROX-VIA-OWNER, branching by equipment kind (handheld → owner; deployable → the object; mount → the mount, not the seated avatar's offset). Print the tier beside every verdict, draw sub-TRACK attackers with a distinct glyph, **exclude the guessed tier from through-wall tallies by default**, and tally events with no usable position in a visible *unattributed* counter rather than smearing them onto a nearby cell.
8. **(06)** Build the **occlusion point cloud** for free: every BLOCKED raycast already returns a hit point, so index them and scatter them in the replay panel. Real, incomplete evidence of tested geometry — never an invented wall. Add its companion rule: **BLOCKED draws red only when a dense sample was tested**, grey otherwise, regardless of the raycast result.
9. **(11)** Cluster across **θ and across time windows** and report **drift** (modal cluster changes between windows, stability high on both sides → chevron + printed change time) separately from **noise** (low stability inside a window → hatch, no chevron), with a `short` badge keeping small sample size distinct from ambiguity. Combine with **(09)**'s time-bounded override `(avatar, team, effective_from)` so a correction never overwrites correctly-inferred earlier history.
10. **(02)** Enforce **two kinds of number**: quantities with a real denominator print as intervals with n; anything from clustering or classification prints as an ordinal band, and no clustering margin ever appears as a decimal in a lede. Pair with **(12)**'s per-signal documentation page (definition, formula, known false-positive causes, live instances) as the target of every band chip.
11. **(01)** Ship the overlay's **2D top-down Chart twin under a parity rule** — anything the world draws at rung R has a Chart twin with identical encodings and picking. The overlay is gated to stationary + alt-cam, i.e. unavailable exactly when the officer is walking, re-gearing or briefing; geography must not become unanswerable for a UI reason.
12. **(08)** Give the overlay an **LOD table where each rung owns a camera-distance band**, with a HUD caption naming the current regime, plus a **pick stack list** when candidates fall within ~12 px. Take **(11)**'s "drawing 62 of 214 markers (LOD)" chip with it: a view that silently drops glyphs is a lying view.
13. **(12 + 11)** For behaviour: a **filter, not a rank** — no summed score, one column per signal with its own count, `thin` below n≈8 excluded from ordering, interval before point estimate, and flags carrying a **disposition** (`open` / `checked — plausible cause` / `escalated`) plus a note, initials and timestamp that travel into the exported debrief.
