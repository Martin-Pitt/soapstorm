# Attack on Entry 05 — Time-First: the Combat Timeline (ANALYSIS + FEASIBILITY lens)

## Fatal

**1. "Attacker" position for non-avatar sources (turrets, deployables, mounts) has no data path, but the design draws lines to it anyway.**
The overlay spec (§5, rung 0) says "Damage in flight = a line from attacker to target," and the LOS analysis (§6) says candidates come from "the attacker's track" over the travel window. Both assume the attacker resolves to an avatar with a track sample. The brief and the engineering plan explicitly carve out equipment kinds that don't: deployables (grenades/traps/turrets rezzed from a HUD), mounted deployables (an object that shoots itself while an avatar merely rides it), and vehicles. For these, `owner` is the avatar who rezzed the thing, not the position of the thing doing the shooting — a turret can sit at a fixed point while its owner is elsewhere in mouselook, or a rezzer chain can be several hops removed. The track store in the fixed plan is `unordered_map<LLUUID, vector<TrackSample>>` **per avatar only**; there is no track store for objects. Entry 05 never says how a turret's or deployable's *own* position over time is obtained (it isn't — only DEATH gives a one-shot `source_pos`, and viewer-visibility facts are transient). This silently breaks: the damage-in-flight line (§5 rung 0), all LOS candidate generation for non-handheld weapons (§6, §4.2's candidate table), and therefore the through-wall suspicion score (§6, §7 Q8) for exactly the equipment class — turrets/deployables — the owner's vision statement calls out by name ("what equipment has been identified to kill through walls"). This is presented as if it works uniformly ("attacker to target," no caveat) when for a whole equipment category there is no computation path at all.

**2. Object targets (vehicles, turrets, deployables being destroyed) have no home in this design.**
The data model explicitly carries a `targetIsAgent` flag and the brief states `target` is "victim avatar or object." Entry 05's entire information architecture and swimlane view are avatar-rows-under-team-bands; there is no page, row, or overlay treatment for an event whose target is an object. A vehicle destroyed mid-fight, or a turret killed to stop it shooting through a wall, produces DEATH/DAMAGE events this design has nowhere to put — not a swimlane row (rows are avatars), not an Avatar page, not a Team page. This is a real, common combat-log shape (per the owner's own equipment taxonomy) left as an unaddressed gap, not a stated exclusion.

## Serious

**3. Phase segmentation bakes a raid narrative into named, prominent UI and chains unvalidated inferences without propagating their uncertainty.**
STAGING/PUSH/FRONT/COLLAPSE/RESPAWN are presented as detected facts on the always-visible Phase ribbon (§4.1) with a single confidence number each. But: (a) STAGING's criterion depends on an inferred "spawn point" seeded from "the session's first two minutes," and (b) COLLAPSE's criterion tests "retreat velocity toward spawn" — reusing that same inferred, unverified point. The confidence score is defined only as "normalized margin between the winning rule's signal and its threshold" (§6) — it does not account for the fact that an entire branch of the state machine rests on a guessed location that could itself be wrong (a raid with two staging areas, or a freeform fight that never staged at all, breaks the spawn seed and silently mislabels everything downstream). The brief requires the freeform, neutral-sim, and intra-group cases to be first-class, not degraded cases of the classic-raid template; Entry 05's FFA walkthrough (§7) only argues that *team* clustering degrades gracefully, and never revisits whether slapping "Staging → Push → Front → Collapse" labels on a freeform skirmish is honest. A ribbon showing "Staging (conf 0.82)" reads as a fact about what happened, not as "our raid-shaped curve-fitter found a plausible fit" — which is what it actually is for non-classic sessions.

**4. The through-wall-suspicion companion signal ("time-to-first-hit after target track re-enters view range") measures the wrong viewer's view range and this is not disclosed.**
§7 Q8 offers this as "a second, independent signal alongside LOS," captioned only with "lag, travel time, sim batching." But "view range" here can only be the *tool's* visibility model (viewer render distance / bridge tick presence for the observing officer's tooling), not the *shooter's* client settings, draw distance, LOD, or occlusion culling — which the brief states outright are never knowable ("other viewers' settings ... wireframe use can only be suspected from behaviour, never proven"). Framing a proxy for *our* visibility as "reaction time after re-entering view range" invites the officer to read it as reaction-to-seeing-the-target, which is a claim about the *suspect's* screen, not something this data can support. This needs either removal or a much stronger disclaimer than the current damage-type-style caveat caption.

## Minor

**5. LOD/downsampling for whole-session rendering is asserted, not specified.** §4.1 says swimlane/overview draw lists are "rebuilt only when the zoom window or filter changes," which controls *when* work happens but not *how much* work a rebuild costs at 2 h / 100k events / millions of samples compressed into ~1000 px of overview width. Some column-bucketed min/max/density reduction is almost certainly the intended answer (it's implied by "density sparkline"), but the entry doesn't say so, and get this wrong and the "always-visible overview" safety rail becomes the frame-budget bottleneck it's supposed to protect against.

**6. Denominator wording drifts between the analysis model and the walkthrough.** §6 defines through-wall suspicion as `BLOCKED / all verdicts for that weapon`. §7 Q8 describes it as "3/40 BLOCKED, linked to each [kill]" — suggesting the denominator is kills, not all damage events. Cosmetic, but exactly the kind of inconsistency that matters when the whole feature's pitch is "always show the denominator."

**7. Reassignment-aware friendly fire is flagged as a gap against the fixed plan (good) but is treated as a footnote.** §6 and the Risks section (§8) correctly identify that "assignment as of time t" is not yet a data shape the fixed engineering plan promises; this is a real amendment to a document the brief says not to redesign, and should be surfaced as a blocking dependency to confirm before implementation, not bundled into a bullet list of open risks alongside softer ones like swimlane scaling.

## What must be kept

- **Single shared cursor across timeline, inspector, and overlay.** Cheap (one scalar), and it's what makes the ladder's "step up / step down" claims actually true rather than aspirational — every rung reads from the same coordinate.
- **The wikipedia-style Inspector shell** (breadcrumb + backlinks + pinning, one shell reused per noun type) — implementable entirely in stock XUI (`tab_container`, `scroll_list`, text) with no custom drawing required, and it's the cleanest realization of the brief's "dig and dig" requirement.
- **Uncertainty-as-a-separate-channel rule**: color reserved for team/damage-type, outline style (solid/dashed/dotted) reserved for confidence/uncertainty, never overloaded. This is a good, enforceable convention that prevents the classic mistake of uncertain data looking as confident as verified data.
- **Always-visible whole-session overview strip with a draggable viewport.** Directly implements Bret Victor's "don't get stuck in the zoomed weeds," is cheap to compute incrementally, and doesn't depend on any of the fatal/serious inference chains above.
- **Simultaneous display of all LOS travel-window candidates** (rung 2) rather than stepping through them one at a time — this is a direct, low-risk translation of data the fixed plan already computes (candidate list with individual verdicts) into a parameter-sweep view, and it's the single clearest realization of "abstract over parameter" in the whole entry.
- **Graceful team-clustering degradation to "Unaffiliated / low-confidence"** for FFA sessions (§7) — this part of the design does honestly avoid forcing structure that isn't there, which is the standard the phase-segmentation machinery (finding 3) fails to meet.
- **No screen in this entry requires a web view.** Phase ribbon, swimlanes, and overview are one custom `LLView` with cached draw lists (consistent with the existing storm-debug-overlay pattern cited in the plan); side panels and the Inspector are stock `tab_container`/`scroll_list`/text — all XUI-native.
