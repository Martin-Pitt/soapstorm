# Judge J2 — Information architecture and digging

**Lens.** Page/panel grammar, links, backlinks, history, side-by-side, pinning. Does the officer stay oriented while digging four nouns deep, and can they get back? `dig` and `officer` weighted most heavily; every criterion scored 1–10, total = sum (max 60). Ties broken by `dig + officer`.

The question I asked of every entry: *after four hops — session → phase → death → weapon → suspect — can the officer (a) still name where they are, (b) get back to the framing they started from, and (c) put two things side by side?* Most entries have breadcrumbs. Very few have an answer for (b) that survives the officer changing the cursor, the time window and a threshold on the way down, and only three have a real answer for (c).

## Per-entry notes

### 01 — ladder (56)

The only entry that solves the return problem properly. Two mechanisms, deliberately not merged: the shared back/forward stack retraces *your path*, while the per-`(rung, subject)` **anchor** — written by `]`, read by `[` — returns *this subject's* ground truth. Entering an aggregate cold lands on its **witness** (medoid), and the rail prints `(back)` or `(witness)` plus the exact instance before you press the key. Nobody else states where a step-down will land before it happens.

The vertical/horizontal split (ladder changes altitude and keeps the subject; wiki changes subject and keeps altitude) is drawn into the breadcrumb as tinted operator chips vs plain `→`, so altitude change and subject change never look alike. The Rung Row table (one rule mapping any noun to all six rungs) means the article grammar is derivable rather than improvised per noun — the single best defence against the page-set drifting during implementation.

Article floater keyed by noun id gives real side-by-side. Links are `LLUrlEntry` on `secondlife:///app/sscombat/<type>/<id>`, which is the correct viewer mechanism. Pins → R3 Board is how small multiples get assembled, and Case + clipboard is the officer artifact.

Analysis is the most careful in the field on ordering: `t̂ ± u` per event with *no ordering claim* between overlapping intervals, and a killing blow that refuses to name one attacker when the margin is inside the uncertainty. "Share of damage, not of health" printed in the mark. The pre-clustering seed test (seeds disable themselves and the header names the failed test) is the cleanest intra-group answer submitted.

Costs: six stage panels plus a Chart renderer plus the world renderer is the second-largest custom-drawing bill here, and R−1 needs a plan delta (honestly flagged, with a named degradation if refused). The Chart twin and its parity rule is the entry's best feasibility idea, not its worst: the overlay is gated off exactly when an officer walks or briefs, and geography must not become unanswerable for that reason.

### 08 — allegiance (55)

Best officer entry, and the second-best navigation model. **Two histories, drawn identically and never merged**: `[<][>]` over the noun chain in the Inspector, and a separate `[<][>]` over the *View* chain in the session floater, where a View is `(cursor, window, tab, mode, ParamSet, selection)`. Anything that moves the cursor or the parameters pushes the previous View first — so a four-hop violation dig ends with one click back to the framing it started from, which is precisely the failure the other entries paper over with a breadcrumb. `[mark]` names a View, and marked Views become briefing chapters. That is Q9 solved as a data structure rather than as a promise.

One global **ParamSet** means two rung-2 tabs can never disagree about teams at the same moment; the Sweep is the only screen that shows more than one, explicitly as comparison. The **Ledger** makes officer assertions data — listed, undoable, attributed, reporting what changed and what evidence they overrule.

The intra-group scenario is *discovered, not assumed*: `STRAINED 41%` plus a `(!)` tag-dependence flag (every solve runs twice, with and without the group prior) appear in the default view to an officer who has never heard the phrase. `[sweep this]` on the badge you distrust is the best climb affordance in the field — you ascend from the specific reading you doubt, which becomes the sweep's subject.

Analysis is the deepest submitted: trust and target-kind admission filters before anything enters the field, bursts as the evidence unit, spawn-cohort affinity conditional on a *tested* respawn signal, structure index vs a null model with an explicit DEATHMATCH fallback (the Ribbon becomes a hostility heat strip rather than attempting 80 threads), 2.5D front extraction that refuses to draw, jittered agreement counts instead of crisp topology changes. The overlay LOD table — each rung owns a camera-distance band, with a HUD caption naming the current regime — is the best answer to "the officer alt-cams freely".

Marks off: it is the longest entry and the most to build; the Ribbon's legibility above 40 threads is handled by budget rather than solved; side-by-side is pinned Person pages rather than a first-class compare.

### 11 — comparative (54)

**Query-as-value** is the most elegant IA idea in the competition: `Query = {filter, rowFacet, colFacet, cellView, sweep, baseline, timeWindow}`, and a breadcrumb chip, a pin, a compare slot, a link and a noun page are all literally that struct. One history stack restores Query *and* cursor time; the breadcrumb is editable (click to truncate, drag a chip into slot A/B); backlinks are "appears in these cells", computed by re-running facets against an existing index. The Board keeps Queries, so a pin restores exact state, and "copy as text" carries flags with dispositions and officer initials into a debrief.

Analysis is joint-best. **Flip fraction** demoted above the existential test — "a clear shot exists" is uninformative when true and decisive when false — is the sharpest single claim about LOS anyone made. Clustering swept across θ *and* across time windows separates three things every other entry conflates: **drift** (modal change across windows, stable within), **noise** (unstable within), and **short** (small n). The front field is swept over θ×σ and any cell whose *sign* disagrees is hatched rather than drawn. The respawn test (φ) gates the spawn axis instead of assuming a game rule. Equipment keyed on `(creator, root name, attach class, damage signature)` with a permanent UNATTRIBUTED sink is the right answer to a spoofable name.

Deductions on dig: the noun pages are listed in a sentence each and subordinated to the Grid, so the *page* grammar is thinner than 01/02/08 even though the *query* grammar is stronger. The bill is honest but large (~2.5k lines of paint routines across seven cell renderers).

### 02 — hypertext (53)

The strongest pure-hypertext IA, and the entry I would steal from most. Address grammar `ss:<type>/<id>[@t][?facet]` rendered through a registered `LLUrlEntryBase` so links work inside every `LLTextBase` — page bodies, tooltips, chat, notes. One page skeleton for all twenty page types (title/confidence · lede · infobox · figure · accordion sections · *What links here* · facet footer). Split pane B with its own history. Pin rail as the working set. A padlock that binds a page's `@t` to the global cursor.

Two ideas nobody else had. **Typed claims with polarity** — `⚑ FLAG`, `✓ CLEAR`, `⇄ OVERRIDE`, `✎ NOTE` — rendered *in every backlink list*, so a promotion reviewer seeing "3 claims" can tell two are exonerations without opening them. And **trails**: the visit sequence recorded as a graph, nameable (`ss:trail/promotion-nyx`), dumpable to chat as a linked outline — the briefing artifact falls out of the navigation model for free. It also thinks about address *stability*: aggregate addresses travel between officers, event addresses are labelled session-local, unresolvable links land on a stub page naming what they sought. Nobody else noticed that a link an officer pastes to a colleague may not resolve.

Cache coherency is specified (`(epoch, dep set)`, LRU 32, visible "recomputed" flash), which matters because officer overrides silently invalidate half the wiki otherwise.

The ladder is three axes (WHO/WHEN/WHERE) rather than one rail, which is defensible and mostly right — a Death page really does have two WHO subjects, and a Model has no side — but it costs some of the crispness 01 gets from a single altitude with named operators. The base overlay layer plus scale classes (§5) is the best overlay-IA thinking in the field: a permanent dim session layer means free alt-camming always has something to click, and pinned figures two scale classes away collapse to a centroid with a count rather than burying the six-ray fan the officer is leaning in to read.

### 06 — forensics (46)

The Death Report is a genuinely good hero page and the **Team Epoch** is a good noun (a team's membership within one bucket, so an avatar can belong to different epochs of different teams). Navigation is pragmatic: browser back/forward plus a flat *Recently Visited* list, which is the cheap correct fix for a linear stack dropping a branch, and each of the six pins owns its own breadcrumb stack — stated explicitly, which most entries leave ambiguous.

Two analysis ideas worth taking: **raw ranked damage totals** tagged dominant/contested/long-shot instead of a manufactured 72/18/10 split, and the **occlusion point cloud** — every BLOCKED raycast's already-computed hit point accreted into a spatial index and drawn as a sparse scatter. That is free geometry evidence from casts the tool already paid for, and it correctly refuses to invent a wall silhouette the plan has no data for.

Held back by Q9: the docket workflow (flag → review → briefing export) is *explicitly left unspecified*. That is one of the three officer jobs, deferred by name.

### 05 — timeline (45)

Best gap-closing revision in the field. **Attacker position tiers** (TRACK / LAST-SEEN / APPROX-VIA-OWNER) printed beside every LOS verdict and drawn as a hollow diamond, with tier-3 excluded from through-wall tallies by default, is the only clean answer submitted to "a turret is the paradigm through-wall case and has no avatar track". **Object** as a first-class noun (destroyed vehicles/turrets) closes a hole every other entry has. `K_space` flood-fill forking the phase ribbon per spatial cluster is an honest refusal to narrate one front.

IA is competent rather than deep: breadcrumb explicitly reconciled with the history stack (Back pops one segment, so the two can never disagree — good rigor), backlinks, pin-to-strip, second Inspector instance for side-by-side. But the noun graph is thin on links, there is no case/dossier artifact, and Q9 goes unanswered.

### 12 — fairness (45)

Best anti-abuse discipline, and one analysis idea nobody else had: **sweep the anchor, not just the parameter**. Every LOS verdict is re-run at `t±1 s` — the log's own stated lag, which is comparable to the travel window being swept — and labelled ROBUST or shown with both verdicts. The **Signal** documentation page (definition, formula, known false-positive causes, live instances) is the strongest honesty mechanism submitted, and **Session Baseline** as an inspectable noun with a non-clearable exclusion audit trail is a good idea about a subtle failure mode. Removing the summed "Signals" column and making the slider a filter rather than a rank is the right call.

Under my lens it is narrow: the noun graph serves the audit, not the raid. Q2/Q4/Q5/Q6/Q7 are comparatively underserved, intra-group and FFA are two sentences each, and there is no composition artifact beyond pins and notes.

### 04 — cartographer (44)

One genuine contribution to digging: the map keeps **its own back/forward stack of view snapshots** (mode, layers, window, crop) plus a Home button and a breadcrumb dot trail of the last ~12 dig locations, distinct from the 3-slot comparison pins. Spatial memory separated from page memory is right, and it is the same insight 08 formalises as View history.

The N-team occupancy generalisation with `margin` and `coverage` as two independent visual channels (band width = contested, dash = data coverage) fixes a real conflation. Staging vs Arrival point, and PCA explained-variance driving an auto-split of a bent front, are both honest.

But the page set is thinner than the top four, side-by-side caps at 3 pins, and Q5/Q9 are effectively unaddressed. The Events tab was reinstated because the map alone cannot serve a promotion scan — correct, and a tell that map-first is a lens rather than a spine.

### 10 — in-world (44)

Best overlay entry, weakest reading surface. The card is the universal unit and every in-world billboard is mirrored as an Inspector text row, so no action depends on aiming a camera — good discipline. Breadcrumb chips as **view snapshots** (layers, trail window, slider, front toggle) with a pinned Overview home chip and forward history kept rather than popped is a real navigation idea. The rung table with per-rung click targets and per-rung uncertainty cues is the tightest overlay specification here, and dropping the click-and-hold radial menu on input-precedence grounds (alt-cam drag, the plan's plain-left-click hook, SL's right-click pie) is exactly the right kind of rejection.

The heat field's **unattributed tally** — an event whose attacker has no track coverage is excluded and counted visibly, never smeared onto a nearby cell — is a small idea worth copying everywhere.

Marks off: the noun set is card-shaped and shallow, there is no history list or case artifact, and Q5/Q9 are absent.

### 07 — equipment (42)

Sharp on its own axis. The Event Dossier generalised to *any* DAMAGE closes the brief's own worked example (a bare damage event must open a page), breadcrumbs carry the session time active at each visit (a nice touch — you never lose which moment motivated the dig), and the classification checklist with partial credit that actually sums is more honest than most confidence numbers here. Solid-vs-dashed for ground-truth `source_pos` vs reconstructed positions, applied everywhere rather than at one screen, is good. The ground-projected shadow decal so a density field reads from an oblique alt-cam angle is clever.

Under my lens the entry's own rejected critique is the problem: "where" is a toggleable layer with a summary panel and *deliberately not a page*, so geography has no destination, no backlinks and no history. That is a hole in a wiki, not a saving. Q9 absent.

### 09 — progressive (41)

The cheapest to build and the most honest about what that costs. One clever mechanism: popping to a mid-stack breadcrumb segment and then pushing somewhere *different* **auto-pins the discarded forward branch**, labelled by its leaf noun — a one-sentence teachable model that recovers the classic linear-stack loss. The three index pages (Event / Equipment / Suspicion) are the right cold-start fix, and the clustering objective is actually defined (minimise intra-cluster damage, KL-style local search) with a stated reason it separates two same-tag sub-teams.

But one visible page at a time means no side-by-side without pins that restore a leaf rather than a stack — the entry names this cost itself. Q9 unaddressed, Q5 minimal. It is a good floor, not a winner.

## Grafts worth taking into a synthesis

1. **Anchor vs witness, and print the landing (01).** `]` writes the anchor; `[` reads it, so up-then-down is the identity. Cold entry into an aggregate lands on its medoid **witness**, with `[`+Left/Right to the extremes. The rail names the exact destination and whether it is `(back)` or `(witness)` *before* the key is pressed. This is the single most important idea in the competition for keeping digging coherent.
2. **Two histories, never merged (08).** A noun chain for "what I was reading about" and a View chain for "how I was looking at it" — `(cursor, window, tab, params, selection)` — pushed by anything that moves the cursor or a parameter, with `[mark]` naming a View. Marked Views become the briefing chapter list, which turns Q9 into a data structure instead of a promise.
3. **Typed claim polarity in backlinks (02).** `⚑ flag / ✓ cleared / ⇄ override / ✎ note` rendered in every *What links here* list, so a promotion reviewer distinguishes an exoneration from an accusation without opening it. Cheap, and it is the difference between a backlink list and a reputation.
4. **Query-as-value (11).** One struct that a breadcrumb chip, a pin, a compare slot, a link and a noun page all *are*. Back/forward restores query and cursor exactly; pinning is a copy; backlinks are membership tests against an index that already exists.
5. **Permanent dim base layer + scale classes (02).** The session figure always drawn at ~25 % so free alt-camming always has geography to click (bare ground opens its spot page), plus micro/local/region scale classes: the focus layer full strength, pinned layers one class away decimated to 40 %, two classes away collapsed to a labelled centroid — with "drawn 200 of 512" in the caption. Fixes both "nothing to click until you navigate" and "a pinned region-wide cloud buries the fan you are reading".
6. **[sweep this] from the badge you distrust, plus flags that volunteer themselves (08).** Every uncertainty badge, strain chip and matrix cell is a climb affordance whose subject is the reading you doubted. Pair it with the strain meter (`STRAINED 41%`) and the tag-dependence `(!)` flag from the always-run second, tag-free solve, so the intra-group case is *discovered* by an officer who never suspected it.
7. **Flip point in the headline, and sweep the anchor too (02 + 12).** The nearest parameter value at which a verdict changes is free once the sweep grid exists, so print it in the lede, not behind a facet — and additionally re-run the verdict at `t ± 1 s` of log lag, labelling it ROBUST or showing both outcomes. The log's lag is the same order as the travel window being swept; only 12 noticed.
8. **Occlusion point cloud (06).** Every BLOCKED raycast's hit point (already returned by `lineSegmentIntersectWorldGeometry`) accretes into a session spatial index and draws as a sparse scatter. Chokepoints become recognisable for free, untested ground stays honestly sparse, and nothing invents wall geometry the tool does not have.
9. **Drift vs noise vs short, badged separately (11).** Cluster stability swept across θ *within* a window and modal change *across* adjacent windows separates a real defection (chevron + printed change time) from an unstable classification (hatch) from a late arrival (`short`). Three failure modes that every single-threshold team model prints as the same low confidence.
10. **Attacker position tiers (05) + Chart twin parity (01).** Tier every attacker position TRACK / LAST-SEEN / APPROX-VIA-OWNER, print it beside every LOS verdict, draw tier-2/3 as a hollow diamond, and exclude tier-3 from through-wall tallies by default — the only submitted answer for turrets and mounts. And keep the rule that anything the overlay draws at rung R has a 2D top-down twin with identical encodings and picking, since the overlay is gated off exactly when the officer walks, briefs or re-gears.

Honourable mention, not in the ten: the heat field's visible **unattributed tally** (10) — events with no track coverage are excluded and *counted*, never smeared into a neighbouring cell; and the **Signal documentation page** (12), one page per signal type carrying its formula and its known false-positive modes.
