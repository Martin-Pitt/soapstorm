# Combat Log tool — design competition brief

You are designing the user experience, user interface and analysis model of a combat-review tool inside Soapstorm, a Firestorm-derived Second Life viewer. Rendering/engineering plumbing is already planned (see "What is already decided"); the competition is about how an officer *comprehends a combat session* through this tool. Read this whole brief before designing.

## The owner's vision (verbatim intent)

- "I basically want a high quality tool you can enable in the soapstorm settings for analysing how combat is flowing based on people are dying and running around, detecting distinct groups/teams (although mind friendly fire), allowing OIC (officers in command) to inspect and review combat history for various purpose such as promotions or violations, how damage adjustments are coming through the combat logs, types of equipment being used by each team derived from their damage and kill logs, being able to see how much damage or deaths each avatar or equipment is doing, etc. There may be need for different sub floaters for inspecting different information. There should be good UX for showing logs overlayed in-world instead of just through a 2D interface. Particularly highlighting lines of fire and lines of sight, so we can check if a death happens where people were particularly tracking what equipment has been identified to kill through walls (taking into account false positives due to bullet travel time or such). The tool could be used to identify that people are behaving well, such as not wireframing (although they might be using minimap which is considered fair) and using fair equipment."
- "Clicking on a death or damage event could show in the floater allowing you to inspect events and related events together. E.g. death would try to associate related damage events that led up to it. You could also inspect the equipment related to the events, to figure out what caused the damage and what equipment was used, such as bullets, weapons (handheld typically), HUDs, deployables (rezzed from HUD such as a grenade, trap, or a turret that may rez bullets), vehicles (agent(s) sitting on it and has moved (e.g. not a chair), mounted deployables (agent sits on it, can shoot itself or agent uses their own weapon on it), etc."
- "I'm envisioning something similar like being able to dig and dig, like wikipedia articles where you dig into articles."
- "Great UX Bret Victor style, like Ladder of Abstraction, in being able to fully comprehend how combat session went multi-dimensionally."
- "The tool will be primarily used having to stand still and alt-cam around. Moving and being in first or third person all negate the use of the tool." The in-world overlay only draws while stationary + alt-camming and 5 s after leaving mouselook/OTS.
- Session scale: a 1–2 hour raid. Data is in-memory for the current session (no files in v1).

## How combat actually happens (design for all of these)

- **Classic raid**: a group teleports into a sim, gathers and gears up at a spawn/staging area, then pushes out and fights from there. Fronts form, collapse, re-form. Respawns return to the spawn area.
- **Freeform**: SL combat is truly freeform; the above is typical, not guaranteed. Fights start anywhere, people arrive mid-fight, leave, come back.
- **Neutral combat sims** (e.g. Bad Space / Outer Space regions): somewhat free-for-all; group allegiances may exist, may drift, or it may become a deathmatch where everyone fights everyone.
- **Intra-group skirmishes**: two teams from the *same* group (e.g. two Ashguard teams, one defending, one attacking). Active group tag is then useless for team detection; only who-shoots-whom over time reveals sides, and friendly fire must not be misread as "different team".
- Team membership can change during a session. The tool must present allegiance as an inference with confidence, never a hard fact, and let the officer override.

## Data the tool has (facts, verified)

Combat 2.0 combat log (via LSL bridge script, region-wide):
- `DAMAGE`: `damage` (final), `initial` (before adjustment), `type` (damage type int; -1 impact, 0 generic, 1 acid … 14 emotional, community 100 medical, 101 repair, 102 explosive, 103 crushing, 104 anti-armor, 105 suffocation, 106 redeploy), `source` (object that did the damage — bullet, weapon, trap…), `rezzer` (object that rezzed the source — gun, HUD, turret), `owner` (avatar owning the source), `target` (victim avatar or object), optional `modifications[{task_id, events[{script, new_damage}]}]` (damage adjustment scripts, e.g. armour HUDs). **No positions.** Log events may lag the real hit by up to 1 s.
- `DEATH`: `source`, `source_pos [x,y,z]`, `target`, `target_pos`, `owner`, `rezzer`, `type` (grafted from the last DAMAGE).
- Script-generated custom events on the same channel arrive tagged untrusted with the sender's object key and owner key.
- The wearer's own incoming hits also arrive from `final_damage` in the bridge attachment: precise time, `source_pos`, and a hit list — only for the user's own avatar; de-duplicated against the log.

Avatar tracks (region-wide):
- Viewer object updates for avatars in view range (position, velocity, rotation, sitting/parent) at roughly 2–10 Hz when moving; coarse locations for far avatars (~1 Hz, z quantised to 4 m).
- Bridge script ticks (default 2 Hz, delta-encoded, up to 100 agents): position, yaw, velocity and `llGetAgentInfo` flags: MOUSELOOK, SITTING, ON_OBJECT, FLYING, WALKING, IN_AIR, CROUCHING. These are ground truth and correct viewer drift.
- Own avatar included.

Objects / equipment:
- For `source`/`rezzer` keys: name, creator, owner, group, attach point (0 = not attached; 31–38 = HUD points), rezzer chain (rezzer of the rezzer), root; from the viewer when visible, or from the bridge script within ~0.5 s of first sight (bullets de-rez fast). Sometimes unknown.
- Classification is inferred: handheld weapon, projectile of weapon X, HUD, deployable (grenade/trap/turret), vehicle (object with seated avatars that moved), mount (seated and firing). Evidence is partial; show it.

Names: avatar display names resolve asynchronously. Team colours can seed from contact sets / group colours / netmap marks the officer already uses.

Line of sight: the viewer can ray-cast against world geometry (not avatars). For a hit at time t, candidate shooter positions over the preceding bullet-travel window can each be tested; a verdict is CLEAR / BLOCKED / UNKNOWN with the caveat that travel time, lag, and sim batching create false positives.

Not available: health values, HUD internals, the region's own game rules, other viewers' settings (wireframe use can only be suspected from behaviour, never proven).

## Constraints of the medium

- UI is Second Life XUI: immediate-mode widgets (floaters, tab containers, scroll lists, text, sliders, buttons, line editors, custom LLView subclasses that draw with lines/rects/text each frame). No HTML, no web views. Multiple floaters can be open; floaters can be resized and docked. Custom-drawn panels (timelines, charts, small multiples, node graphs) are fine if drawn from primitives.
- In-world overlay: lines, rings, arrows, crosses, filled quads, 3D-anchored text labels, per-vertex colour/alpha, optional depth test (occlusion visible or x-ray). Clickable markers via screen-rect hit tests. It draws only while the officer is stationary and alt-camming; the officer alt-cams freely around the region.
- Timeline: the officer can scrub/play the session; the world shows tracks and events at the cursor time. Live mode follows now.
- Performance: main thread, immediate mode; a 2 h session may hold ~100k events and millions of track samples. Views must be computed incrementally or on demand.
- Everything is uncertain: lag, missing positions, unknown objects, inferred teams. Honest uncertainty display is a first-class requirement.

## Bret Victor, Ladder of Abstraction (the UX north star)

- Rung 0, concrete: a real-time view "for getting a direct, visceral sense of a system's behavior". Here: the live 3D overlay at the cursor time.
- Rung 1, abstract over time: show all moments at once ("from a helicopter above the city … every street at once"). Trails instead of moving points; the whole session as one picture.
- Rung 2, abstract over parameters: show behaviour across a range of a variable (e.g. LOS verdict across the whole travel-time window; team assignment across confidence thresholds; damage across adjustment scripts).
- Higher rungs: several dimensions at once, colour-coded outcomes, small multiples, coordinate transforms that "unbend" space (e.g. distance-to-front instead of x/y).
- Interaction: scrubbing; "quickly skim over it, or stop at interesting events and examine them carefully"; pointing at a location on an abstraction to step back down to the concrete instance.
- Principle: "the deepest insights emerge not at any one level of abstraction, but in the transitions between them"; every abstraction must offer both a step up and a step back down; never get stuck on the ground or in the clouds; stay grounded by checking concrete instances.

Wikipedia-style digging: every noun the officer meets (an avatar, a death, a weapon, a team, a place, a minute, a verdict) is a destination with its own page/panel and links to related nouns; navigation keeps a history/breadcrumb so digging never loses the thread; backlinks show what refers to the thing you are looking at.

## Officer questions the design must answer well

1. How did this raid go, overall? Where did the fighting happen, when, between whom?
2. Who were the teams? How sure are we? Where did friendly fire happen?
3. Why did X die at 14:32? Who hit them, with what, from where, and could they see them?
4. Which equipment kills through walls? How often, and how confident is that claim per weapon?
5. How do damage adjustments (armour scripts etc.) change incoming damage per team/avatar?
6. What did avatar Y do all session — movement, mouselook use, kills, deaths, weapons, time seated on vehicles?
7. Which equipment did each team use, and which items are suspect/unfair?
8. Did anyone behave suspiciously (implausible reaction, speed, awareness through walls)? Show evidence and the honest uncertainty, not verdicts.
9. Officer workflows: reviewing a promotion candidate, investigating a reported violation, briefing the group after the raid.

## Deliverable format for a design entry

Write your entry as Markdown to the path you are given, ~1500–3000 words, with these sections:
1. **Thesis** (one paragraph): the organising idea.
2. **Ladder**: the concrete-to-abstract rungs your design offers and, for each, the step up and the step down (how the user moves between rungs).
3. **Information architecture**: the nouns (pages/panels), what each shows, how they link, navigation model (history, breadcrumbs, backlinks, pinning).
4. **Screens**: for each floater/panel, an ASCII wireframe plus a description of every region of it. Keep XUI feasibility in mind.
5. **In-world overlay**: what is drawn at each rung, encodings (colour, width, alpha, glyphs), what is clickable and what clicking does, how uncertainty is drawn.
6. **Analysis model**: the derived quantities your UI needs, how each is computed from the available data, and how its uncertainty is represented.
7. **Walkthroughs**: answer officer questions 1, 3, and 8 above step by step through your UI, plus one intra-group skirmish scenario and one FFA scenario.
8. **Risks and open questions**.

Be concrete and opinionated. Prefer few, deep, composable views over many shallow ones.

## SLMC domain facts (from the owner, 2026-09-08; later stages must honour these)

Combat systems
- All avatar damage is Linden Combat 2.0 (LLCS) and appears in the combat log. Vehicles and deployables often still use legacy **LBA** (Listen Based Armour, chat-channel health for scripted objects), which is invisible to the combat log. Anti-armor damage type 104 is the Combat 2.0 replacement but adoption is slow and political. => object health/death is mostly unobservable unless a region system emits custom combat-channel events; the tool must show "unknown" rather than infer object deaths.
- Delivery methods coexist in one raid: (a) classic prim-thrower bullets (`llSetDamage`, rezzed, scripted die-on-collision); (b) scriptless bullets via `llRezObjectWithParams` with pre-set damage; (c) hitscan: `llCastRay` + `llDamage` from the weapon attachment with a visual effect; (d) explosive grenades rezzed from a HUD using `llDamage` (area damage, several targets from one source); legacy remote kills used rezzed kill objects forced to collide. => classify per event: `source` attached (or `source == rezzer`) means hitscan/direct, so no travel-time window and LOS verdicts are strong; `source` unattached and short-lived means projectile, so the travel-time window applies; one `source` hitting several targets within a second means area damage.
- Damage types: generic (0) dominates because of older weapons; newer bullets use piercing (8); creative weapons use other types (lightning staff = electric, ice staff = cold); 102 explosive; 104 anti-armor for vehicles; 100 medical with negative damage = healing; 106 redeploy = respawn signal, not a real hit. The tool should name these and treat negative damage and 106 specially.
- Damage adjustments (`modifications[]`): the important cases are complete or temporary **immunity** (new_damage 0) and reduction. Some are legitimate: the region owner's experience temp-attachment controls where and when people can be killed (safe zones at spawn, non-combatant bystanders). Others are what officers need to see. Today this is invisible. Requirement: per combatant, surface which scripts/objects adjusted their incoming damage (`task_id` resolves to an object; its owner/creator distinguishes the region's experience attachment from the combatant's own HUD), and the *patterns* under which immunity/reduction occurred: immune at the spawn hub vs in the middle of the battlefield while dealing damage; immune bystander who never dealt or took damage (fine) vs immune while killing (suspect).
- Death handling depends on the region system (EBCS, FLECS, others): typically an in-region teleport to the team's spawn hub plus health reset, no respawn delay, tied to a DEATH event. Heuristic: a position jump of roughly 8–24 m in one frame with no velocity to explain it is a teleport (or a sit-hack). Custom systems are emerging: a Battlefield-style DOWNED state written to the combat log where the simulator's DEATH is not followed by a teleport until the downed combatant fails to be revived or redeploys; and redeploy-related death events (type 106) for someone who redeploys mid-zone. => DEATH and teleport are separate signals to record, link, and show; neither implies the other.
- Region combat platforms broadcast structured state on region chat channels. FLECS (Ashguard's Resdayn) publishes team configuration `{name, color}[]` and per-agent team assignments on a teams channel, spawn points per team on a respawns channel, tracked agents on a tracker channel, and gamemode/battlefield bounds on a meta channel. EBCS uses experience permissions for on-death teleport and safe zones. => an optional "region system" allegiance/spawn source the bridge could listen to; when present it seeds teams and spawn zones with high confidence, but it must remain optional and the inferred model must work without it.
- Allegiance signals are messy: a military group may use a land-holding group separate from its membership group, and militia (foreign individuals) fight for a group without wearing its tag. Active group tag is a weak hint.

Scale and behaviour
- Raid sizes vary from 1v1 to 3–20 raiders per side; length 15 min to 2 h; deaths from one per second at peak to one per minute or slower.
- Loadout switching mid-fight is normal. The **Layer system**: combatants wear several weapons at once (Layer 1, 2, 3) and switch between them without re-attaching, or swap to a whole new layer set. => equipment attribution must be per hit, and a per-avatar "loadout over time" view is expected; "the weapon" of an avatar is a timeline, not a fact.
- Public reference for damage-type usage statistics: combathud.app (per region, past 24 h, by type, includes healing).

Sides, roles, rules (owner, continued)
- Formal sides: group tags; uniforms typical but inconsistent (every SLMC group has its own rules and discipline varies). Attachments often share an identifiable text prefix (e.g. "[Ash]"), sometimes odd ones. => allegiance can be analysed from shared groups plus attachment-name prefixes across a side, as weak-to-moderate evidence. Objectives are rare and region-custom; most fights are team deathmatch or free-for-all.
- Violations officers chase, in practice: wireframing, unapproved weapons, armour/immunity beyond limits, spawn camping, firing from outside the battlefield, alt-cam aiming. Evidence today is eyewitness plus an OIC HUD that helps compile the raid report. The Ashguard Discord "raid-reports" channel holds the reports and discussion threads (the owner may grant browser access; treat any excerpts as private).
- Promotion reviews want all of: kills, survival, positioning, moving with the squad, covering, discipline; post-combat analysis of teamwork, tactics and strategy at individual, squad and team level would be "super cool". => the design should offer squad-level views (co-movement clusters, mutual support, cover/advance patterns) as an explicit rung, not just per-avatar tallies.
- Users: OICs who stay behind, OICs who also fight, individual combatants reviewing themselves, curious spectators. Both live (short stationary peeks between engagements) and after the raid. OICs compile a report per raid rating individuals. => the design must support a "peek" mode (fast orientation in seconds) and a "study" mode, and should produce material an OIC can lift into a report.
- Regions: raids can involve neighbouring regions (a new combat continent around Bad Space, with Outer Space and Good Space adjacent) but combat and death teleports stay within one region; treat the region as the session boundary.
- Places: the officer defines named zones by hand (spawn, gate, courtyard, roof); region-system respawn points and battlefield bounds can pre-populate them.
- Kills through walls: legitimate cases are landmines, tripwires, ballistically thrown grenades, mortar-style lobbed slow shots over buildings, minimal splash through a thin wall. Not legitimate: bullets through relatively thick walls or barricades; explosives inflicting direct death without a raycast beyond thin-wall splash. Nothing is set in stone and no weapon list of suspects exists. => the tool must classify the delivery (hitscan / projectile trajectory / lobbed arc / area) and show wall thickness or occluder count along the line, and let officers judge; it should surface weapons whose kills are repeatedly BLOCKED with high geometry confidence as candidates, never verdicts.
- Fair awareness: minimap used as a radar is fair. Wireframing (viewer wireframe mode) shows behaviourally as locking onto a specific person across the region through several walls and buildings, with the give-away being elevation tracking rather than precise bearing; the longer they track, walk or run into walls and shoot into walls, the more egregious. => a "tracking-through-walls" analysis: facing/yaw and movement heading versus the nearest enemy that is occluded from them, sustained over time, with shots fired into occluders. Evidence strength grows with duration and with the number of occluders.
- Projectile tracking exists in the viewer: the "ghost projectile" mechanism tags in-view objects that look like bullets (long thin shapes, roughly 4 m or longer and 0.2 m or thinner) and tracks them, so the tool can record each visible bullet's flight (spawn point, velocity, lifetime, where it stopped) and infer its shooter from where it appeared relative to the avatar tracks. => bullets-into-walls, fired-without-aiming, and shot origin are observable for projectile weapons even when the combat log has no positions.
- Mouselook is not required by rule, but most attachment weapons need it to aim. A deployed automated turret firing without mouselook is fine; a fake weapon firing bullets straight at someone while the avatar is not properly aiming is an obvious cheat. Avatar rotation is only reliably available from the bridge poll (delayed, ~2 Hz), so aim-versus-shot-direction comparisons carry that uncertainty.
- Today: eyes plus an OIC HUD for the report. Biggest gap: the combat log's historical and analytic potential is unused.

## How officers actually report (from a scan of ~130 Ashguard raid reports and 8 discussion threads, 2026-01 to 2026-09; names withheld)

- **The artefact.** One forum post per event, tagged Attack / Defense / Raid / Skirmish / Operation / Expedition / FFA / Interception / Paid. Body is generated by the group's OIC HUD and then hand-edited: duration; Personnel (members, then a "(Militia)" list of non-members who fought alongside); Report (free text, usually 1–3 sentences); Incidents (free text); "OIC HUD Output" = one line per participant `agent-uuid,minutes-active` (activity is minutes in which the avatar's rotation changed within 5-minute windows, from the OIC protocol). Replies in the thread carry pasted IM logs with the opposing OIC, memes, and occasional substantive follow-up (equipment testing, commendations, rules to change).
- **Scale observed.** Duration median 90 min (quartiles 60–90, max ~145). Personnel median 8 per report (quartiles 7–10, max 16) plus militia median 1 (max 10). Opponents typically 3–12. Fights are "team deathmatch" by default; objectives (payload, capture/trains, flags) are rare and region-specific.
- **Vocabulary the tool should speak.** *Escalation* / *auth* / *tier*: the agreed gear level that rises through the fight ("low escalation", "high escalation", "max auth", "went to tier 3"); OICs negotiate it by IM with the other side's OIC and expect the log to show *when* the fight escalated (tanks, mechs, drones, vehicles appearing). *Last life* / *called it*: the OIC declares the final life at ~90 min; people leaving before the call is itself an incident. *Combat cuck / randoms*: uninvited third parties joining; side switching mid-fight is common on neutral sims. *Motes*: pay per raid, with bonuses for specific acts (capturing an objective near the enemy spawn, holding out while outnumbered). *Commendation*: recommended in the report for valour with a narrative ("fought 3 v 7 for 20 minutes until reinforcements came home").
- **What Incidents actually contain** (categories, most to least common): equipment disputes and tests (an enemy drone not taking LBA damage, a mech instantly regenerating after death, a mech hitbox too small, mines too small, flak without a height check, a sensor shotgun banned on the home sim, a burst raycast weapon with no visual effects so misses are invisible, "caseless rounds" that are a prim bullet reporting to a stats server then setting damage 100, an ice staff whose walls scale HP with size, a tranquiliser sleep effect that follows the victim to spawn, resistance armour with no visible display); rules of the sim (no explosive rounds or lingering AoE near spawn, no shooting past the red line, respawn timer rules, sit in spawn for the expected duration, flashbang etiquette); conduct (spawn killing "ALLEGEDLY", players leaving the experience or detaching the HUD mid-combat, rez-queuing the sim by changing outfits, movement enhancers "yeeting across the sim", people shooting each other in the back on the same side, "shooting through walls but that's the usual"); system faults (respawn system failure cancelling a raid, someone not teleported to spawn on death with the experience apparently accepted, kill feed not coming through, IMs bugged so messages were relayed by hand); and social friction. Wireframing accusations are frequent and contested: one thread notes that long flashbangs "are literally bait to make wireframing allegations because people blindfire in the direction they are flashed from".
- **Implications for the design.**
  1. The tool must produce, or at least feed, this report: duration, personnel with militia split, per-participant activity minutes (or better), escalation timeline, incidents with evidence links, commendation-worthy episodes (outnumbered holds, objective captures) as ready-made text.
  2. "Was this equipment doing what it should" is the most common officer question in practice: per-object evidence pages need hit/miss visibility (a raycast weapon with no effects), damage taken vs expected (LBA and immunity anomalies, regen after death), area and height behaviour (flak, AoE near spawn), and side effects that persist through respawn.
  3. Escalation is a first-class timeline lane: when vehicles, mechs, drones, staffs and explosives first appear, per side.
  4. Spawn-area rules make the spawn zone a named place with its own rules; deaths and AoE near it are a standing query.
  5. Experience/HUD compliance (in the experience, HUD attached) and death-without-teleport anomalies are things officers already investigate by hand; surface them.
  6. Blindfire-after-flash is a documented false positive for the through-wall tracking analysis; the tool must show the preceding flash/effects context before a wireframing claim.

## How one SLMC group is organised and what its channels show (Ashguard Discord sample, 2026-07 to 2026-09; names withheld)

- **Structure.** A council ("Temple") of officers approves applications and votes on membership in an officers' channel; recruits ("Nammu") go through a short training (HUD familiarisation, handbook, a raid requirement) before full membership; "Militia" are non-members who fight alongside and are listed separately in reports; an OIC is designated per event and can be "taken" by another officer mid-raid through the OIC HUD. Training material, a personnel handbook, a roster/activity tracker spreadsheet and a documentation site exist outside Discord. Officers also OIC for the *other* side on occasion (one officer ran both sides of an inter-group event).
- **Raid lifecycle as seen in chat.** A bot posts "ALERT: <avatar> [<group>] wants to raid <region> with a party of N or more" when an attacker registers at the sim; officers muster members and militia by role ping with a map link and a Discord event; the fight runs; "GG" closes it; the OIC HUD generates the report skeleton. Attack targets depend on which groups currently have open sims; raids get delayed or fail for lack of a target. Multi-day "operations" (contest flags on a neutral sim for daily pay) also happen.
- **A Discord kill feed already exists**, fed from the region system: one line per death, `<killer> <verb> <victim> with <weapon name> from <distance> meters away`. Weapon names carry a maker prefix and version, e.g. `[Ash] - Kagrenac bolt-caster v1.1.0`, `[Ex] PP19 Bizon v 1.04`, `[AI] AKSZU CS 6.2.4`, `[Chaos] Melta Burn - 25 DMG`, plus unbranded effects (`Electrical Blast`, `[UGL Blast]`). Some lines have no weapon (bare "erased X from 27 m away"), some have no killer name (a vehicle or object kill shows as `**`), and self-kills occur (a flask of blinding). Distances range from 1.5 m (melee katar, knife) to 230+ m (mortars, bolt-casters). => Equipment *families* keyed on the name prefix and stem (strip version numbers) are real and stable across instances; distance-at-kill per weapon is an expected statistic; object/vehicle kills and self-kills are ordinary rows, not anomalies.
- **Officers' current concern about viewer tools (same week):** a proposal that Soapstorm *broadcast* when developer-view features (hitboxes, wireframe, bounding boxes) are toggled, as a combat-channel log entry rather than anything transmitted in a way LL disallows, so that "the OIC HUD can look for it on the combat log and ping them as opposed to letting everyone know"; framed explicitly as "a tool for OICs to use to investigate potential issues", not active policing, because "people like to panic". => (a) a Soapstorm-side custom combat-channel event for dev-view toggles is a plausible companion feature and the tool should be ready to ingest such an untrusted-but-self-reported event type; (b) the tool's behaviour-audit surfaces must be private to the reviewer by default and phrased as investigation aids.
- **Equipment culture.** Groups build their own arsenals ("artificery" channel: sensor ranges of explosives, dual-wield animations); balance disputes are settled OIC-to-OIC by IM during the fight ("please ask that basket grenades not be used"; "the sensor shotgun isn't used on Resdayn due to our rules"; flashbang duration and directionality; ice-wall HP scaling). Escalation is usually a negotiated ladder: prim throwers → explosives/crowd control → vehicles, mechs, drones, tanks, with "tier 2 / tier 3" armory auth systems in some groups. => Per-equipment pages should show first-use time per side (escalation lane) and let an officer attach the negotiated rule ("asked not to use at 17:26") as a claim so later hits by that item are flagged as rule-breaking rather than merely suspicious.

## Written sim rules (Ashguard Combat Rules, docs.ashguard.net, public, fetched 2026-09-08) — the checkable rule set

These are one group's rules for its home region; other groups differ, so the tool treats rules as *configurable claims per region* with these as the worked example. Each rule maps to something the tool can measure, partially measure, or only annotate.

- General: do not enter or shoot into red spawn zones unless returning fire (measurable: zone + damage/track); vehicles and armour must be LBA compatible (not observable via combat log); vehicles must have respawn timers (annotate); LBA damage capped at 2000/min per weapon, +750/min per extra crew, fleet ships exempt up to LBA's own 4500/min blacklist (partly measurable if anti-armor 104 hits are logged); avatars ≥1.5 m tall (not observable); prejump enabled (not observable); no obnoxious sounds.
- Weapons: no infinite ammo (partly: sustained fire without reload gaps); **no weapons designed to kill through obstacles that would typically stop them** (the LOS analysis); no fully automatic raycast weapons; burst raycast allowed only without "haze" (a cluster of rays around a central ray); no sensor or agent-list weapons except flamethrowers, explosives and melee, or for lock-on.
- Explosives and AoE: 5 m kill / 10 m wound radius unless highly directional (measurable: distance from source to each target hit by the same source within a second, using tracks); larger radius only with a visible and audible wind-up; explosives with kill radius over 2 m must be interceptable; no non-physical or interceptor-defeating projectiles; **explosives must not kill through walls or solid objects** (LOS from blast point); mines at least 0.25 × 0.25 × 0.04 m (viewer sees object scale when visible); AoE must not be group-safe (measurable: same-group targets within radius take damage or not).
- Movement: jetpacks no in-air recharge, ≥3 s recharge, reasonable distance; sprints ≤5 s with ≥6 s recharge; dive-rolls and dashes ≤15 m, not in air, ≥4 s recharge (all measurable from tracks: burst speed segments, distance per burst, in-air flag, recharge intervals).
- Avatar equipment: riot shields ≤50 HP and directional; blinding or movement-limiting effects must recharge longer than they last; **360° body armour cannot exceed 25% damage reduction** (measurable directly from `modifications`: `damage/initial` per victim per script); healing limited per life or by recharge (measurable: negative-damage medical events per life).
- Deployables: explosives with ≤1 HP destroyable by projectiles; 100 HP at deployment, repairable to ≤400; auto-turrets one per person, must be constructed (not instant), ≤180° field of fire, explosive ammo only with lock-on time (partly measurable: turret fire directions relative to its facing, time from rez to first shot).
- Vehicles: no avatar-hitbox aircraft; hitbox must represent visual scale; hover or jump ≤15 m above ground (measurable from seated-avatar tracks vs ground height); self-repair cooldown ≥1 min; respawn timer formula 1 s per 10 HP (full LBA) or per 20 HP (light); HP caps: tanks/heavy 400, gunships/VTOL/heavy mechs 200, light vehicles/mechs 150, fighters/UFO 10.

## Cross-group rule variance (public rule sets of Chaos Indivisvm 2026-07, Epsilon Security Consulting 2020, Grand Federation 2020, compared with Ashguard 2026)

The rule catalogue must be per-region and editable; the same behaviour is legal on one sim and banned on another. Shared ground and the spread, as observed:

- **Universal**: no client-side assistance (wireframe, hitboxes, collision skeletons, "ARC", derendering foliage, disabling prejump or hard-fall; one group claims "we have ways to detect if you have hitboxes showing"); no shooting or killing through walls by phantom bullets, non-raycast explosions, rez offsets or agent-list weapons; the OIC is the sim's authority during combat and complaints go OIC to OIC; safe zones / spawn hubs / "courtesy lines" around spawns that must not be fired into or fought from; LBA-compatible armour only, with HP classes for vehicles (tanks ~400, gunships ~200, light ~150, fighters ~10) and "you should always be killable" (no invincible or mutilated armour); AOs that move the visual body away from the hitbox are banned; doxxing, GPU crashing, mass abuse-reporting and content theft are the "big four" everywhere.
- **Equipment enforcement mechanisms in use**: a scripted "banned equipment enforcer" that keys on object names (renaming gear to evade it is a ban offence), auto-returners or auto-killers applied to non-compliant users, seeker missiles fired at avatar flyers, admin tools. => equipment identity by name is already the community's enforcement key, so the tool's equipment-family keying on maker prefix and name stem aligns with practice, and a rename mid-session (same creator, same behaviour, new name) is itself a signal worth listing.
- **Numeric limits that differ by sim** (all measurable from tracks or events): dodge/dash distance 10 m (Chaos) vs 15 m (Ashguard); sprint cooldown 5 s (Epsilon) vs 6 s (Ashguard); flak must not detonate on targets under 15 m (Chaos) or 20 m (Grand Fed) above a surface, and flak or seekers may not be used on grounded infantry; hand-grenade throw speed ≤30 m/s; flashbangs ≤~10 s and counting down regardless of mouselook; persistent effects ≤30 s; melee ≤5 m unless two-handed; magazine ≤100 with reload enforced; explosive projectiles ≥0.06 m and interceptable, bullets must die on collision, projectile density ≤1000; seekers need an audible lock tone, a raycast line-of-sight check and proximity detonation only within 5 m of an airborne target; one "superweapon" per side per hour of battle after 60 minutes of attacking (Grand Fed); riot shields ≤50 HP and must drop when firing; deployables 2 per type per person, interceptors 1 per person and never at bullet speed (≥149 m/s), mines ≤5 per person and bullet-destroyable, turrets need a lock time >1 s, a destroyable power source and specialisation.
- **Rules the tool can only annotate**: mono vs LSO bullet scripts, shotgun methodology being public, gear being the latest version, visible trails on raycast weapons (partly observable: a hit with no projectile and no effect), repair-tool tradeoffs, "rule of cool" exceptions granted by an OIC.
- **Consequence for the design**: the rule catalogue is data (per region, per event, with an OIC-granted exception list), each rule carries a measurability class (measurable / partial / annotate), and rule checks produce *findings for the OIC to weigh* against the rule text, never automatic verdicts. Sim owners should be able to export and share their catalogue.

## How physical bullets actually behave (community reference "Physical Bullets in Second Life", Hadet Sonnenkern et al.)

- The simulator uses discrete collision detection per physics frame; a collision event is usually generated on the *next* frame after the intersection, and under sim lag a fast bullet can pass between frames and never register. LL mitigates this by treating projectiles as arrows with an invisible trailing tail longer than the per-frame gap. => (a) hit timestamps lag the geometric impact by one or more sim frames on top of the combat-log batching; (b) a fired bullet that produces no DAMAGE is *not* evidence of a miss under lag, so "shots fired vs hits" per weapon must be shown with the region's time dilation alongside it; (c) the viewer's projectile heuristic (long thin box) is consistent with best practice: bullets are box prims, rotation-locked, dying on collision.
- Reference bullet script: `llSetDamage(100)`, rotation locked on all axes, collision filter excluding its own name, `llDie()` on any collision or land collision. => bullets from the same weapon share an object name (the filter depends on it), which is what the equipment-family key relies on; a bullet that survives collision or bounces is unusual and worth listing on the equipment page.

## How raycast weapons deliver damage in practice (open-source MRCG kit and Chaos public armory, read 2026-09-08)

- **Ray origin is the camera, not the avatar.** Both kits cast from `llGetCameraPos()` along `llGetCameraRot()` with spread: `pos = llGetCameraPos() + llRot2Fwd(llGetCameraRot())`. In mouselook the camera sits at the eye; out of mouselook the camera is behind and above the avatar, so a raycast weapon fired from third person or an alt-cam sees around corners the avatar cannot. => the tool's LOS test for hitscan hits must be run from *two* origins, the avatar's eye and a plausible camera position, and the "alt-cam aiming" violation is precisely the case where the camera origin has line of sight and the eye does not; the bridge's `AGENT_MOUSELOOK` flag at the time of the shot is the decisive context.
- **Pre-`llDamage` raycast kits deliver damage with a rezzed "damage prim."** MRCG rezzes `[MRCG] damage` (renamed `<gun description> dmg`, damage set from the gun's description) near the gun, then moves it with `llSetRegionPos` onto the target avatar's position and briefly makes it physical so it collides; it self-deletes after 6 s or when leaving a damage-enabled parcel. Chaos's node rezzes a visual trail from the camera and an `AT Emitter` for anti-armor. => in the combat log such a hit has `source` = a tiny (0.15 m) short-lived prim that *teleported* to the victim and `rezzer` = the gun; the viewer sees an object appear at the shooter and jump to the victim. Classify as hitscan when the source's first and last observed positions are far apart with no plausible flight, and never draw its "trajectory" as a bullet path.
- **Weapons broadcast their own hit reports on region channels** (MRCG: `llRegionSay(-777, "<target>,<dmg>,<gun type>,<damage>,<falloff damage>,<LBA damage>,<falloff range>,<max range>,<firing rate>,<spread>,<magazine>,<reload>")` and combat-meter style `llRegionSayTo(target, -500, "<id>,damage,<dmg>")` for LBA). => optional extra listeners for the bridge: hit reports carry weapon parameters the combat log lacks (falloff, spread, magazine), but they are unauthenticated and per-kit, so they can only enrich, never confirm.
- **Damage falloff and spread are standard**: full damage inside a falloff range (e.g. 70 m), reduced damage beyond it up to a maximum range (e.g. 200 m), spread growing with sustained fire. => expected damage-vs-distance curves per weapon family are a natural equipment-page chart, and a full-damage hit far beyond the family's usual falloff distance is an anomaly worth listing.

## The owner's existing trackers (Ashguard-FLECS misc boards and slmc-data, read 2026-09-08)

- **"Active combatant" as the community already defines it** (active-combatants-board/tracker.lsl, board subtitle "Recent damage or in mouselook in a combat area"): an agent becomes a combatant when they are the `owner` of a DAMAGE, DEATH or custom OBJECT_DEATH event, or when `llGetAgentInfo` shows MOUSELOOK or ON_OBJECT while standing on a parcel with `PARCEL_FLAG_ALLOW_DAMAGE`; they stay a combatant while in mouselook or seated on an object, and otherwise expire 2 minutes after their last damage activity; they drop when they leave the region. => the tool's Combatant/Civilian split uses the same test, and "combat area" means a damage-enabled parcel.
- **Vehicles and crews**: a seated combatant's `OBJECT_ROOT` is the vehicle; crew membership is maintained per vehicle and the vehicle is dropped when its last crew member leaves. => script 2's tick can carry `root` for seated agents at no extra cost beyond `llGetObjectDetails(agent,[OBJECT_ROOT])`; the tool treats a root that moves as a vehicle and a static root as a mount or seat.
- **Group from attachments**: the tracker reads the agent's affiliation as `llGetObjectDetails(first attachment, [OBJECT_GROUP])`, because attachments carry the group they were worn under. => a second allegiance hint besides the active group tag, obtainable per agent in one call.
- **Group registry schema** (slmc-data.lsl, stored in experience key-value): `groups` = map of group UUID → name; `armies` = list of `{g: group, m?: militia group, c?: civilians group, l?: land group, n: name, s: shorthand}`; `regions` = list of `{n: region name, t: BASE | CONTINENT | NEUTRAL, g?: owning army group, l?: land group}`. About 30 groups and 12 regions today, including for Ashguard: main group, Hireling Militia, Civilians, Land. => the viewer's curated group list should use this exact schema so the owner's data file drops in unchanged; region type and owning group give the session a "home side" prior (defence vs raid) and tell neutral sims apart.
- **Region activity board**: activity is counted from DEATH events on the combat channel; each region panel also reads `llGetEnv` (`agent_limit`, `allow_damage_adjust`, `restrict_combat_log`, `damage_throttle`, `damage_limit`), `llGetSimStats` agent counts, and parcel flags at the landing point (no-damage parcel, access list) to colour the panel (safe land, no entry, neutral, continent). => the same region-settings block the tool's Session page shows; parcel damage flag at a position is a usable "combat area" predicate.

## LBA (Listen Based Armour) protocol facts (github Krutchen/SLMCLBA, LBA v2.32, read 2026-09-08)

- Each LBA object listens on a private channel derived from its own key (`0x` + first four hex chars of the MD5 of the key); damage arrives as chat on that channel with the damage amount as the last CSV field. Variants: LBA, LBA-Light, LBA-Slim, LBA-Deployable, plus respawn-timer helpers. => LBA damage traffic is per-object and cannot be captured region-wide by the bridge; anti-armor damage type 104 in the combat log is the only log-visible form of object damage.
- **LBA health is published in two places the viewer already receives for any in-view object**: the object description is rewritten to `LBA.v.<rev>,<hp>,<maxhp>` on every change, and the hover text shows `[LBA] [hp/maxhp] [██████----]` coloured red-to-green. Hover text travels with object updates and the description with object properties (which the ROC facts cache already fetches). => **object health is observable viewer-side for LBA objects the viewer receives updates for** (vehicles, deployables, turrets, mechs in view), sampled whenever the text changes; the earlier "object health is unobservable" statement must be narrowed to "unobservable for objects out of view or without LBA text, and object damage sources are not attributable except via type 104 log events". Health-over-time per vehicle, time-to-kill against the sim's HP class table, and "regenerated instantly after death" anomalies become measurable from text changes.
- Built-in caps and anti-grief: single-hit anti-armor damage clamped at 300; blacklisting at 150 AT per 4 s (infantry) or 300 AT per 4 s (seated); rezzers must carry a valid LBA description and more than one script to be trusted; landmines are recognised by inventory counts; a `die()` hook resets hp to max (respawn) or deletes the object, with respawn timers computed from timestamps. => the sim rule "LBA damage cap 2000 per minute" is enforced by the armour itself, and a blacklisting event is visible as an owner-say on the vehicle owner's side only; the tool can only observe its consequence (damage stops registering in the hover text).
