# Attack on Entry 09 — Minimal Progressive Disclosure (ANALYSIS + FEASIBILITY lens)

## Verdict up front

The screens and overlay layers are, almost without exception, drawable in XUI/LLView primitives at the stated scale — this entry is disciplined about staying inside the medium and its own §8 risk list already catches the two biggest perf traps (rung-1 trail decimation, marker pick radius). The damage is in the analysis model: three derived quantities are asserted with no computation path that survives contact with the brief's own data constraints, one honesty rule is applied inconsistently (the exact screen the brief cares most about — a single death — is the one place the LOS caveat is dropped), and one noun (Zone) is defined two incompatible ways in the same document.

## 1. Computability of derived quantities

**Team clustering: signed edge weight is incompatible with the stated algorithm.** §6 defines the graph edge weight as "signed net damage exchanged (negative = mostly hurting each other)" and says label propagation over this graph yields "a probability vector over inferred clusters." Standard (and soft/fuzzy) label propagation only knows how to do one thing with an edge weight: pull the two endpoints' label distributions *together*, proportional to the weight. It has no defined behavior for a *negative* weight — pulling two nodes' distributions apart is a different problem (correlation clustering / signed graph partitioning), not label propagation, and the entry never says which algorithm is actually meant, nor how it handles a node with one strongly negative edge and no positive edges (the common case: two enemies who have only ever damaged each other, no positive-affinity edges to anyone). As written this is not an algorithm, it's an algorithm-shaped placeholder. This is exactly the intra-group-skirmish case the brief singles out (two same-tag teams, "only who-shoots-whom... reveals sides") — the one scenario where getting this right matters most, and it's the one where the spec is vaguest.

**Time-indexed friendly-fire confidence has no underlying time-varying model.** §6 says team assignment "can drift" so friendly-fire confidence must be "time-indexed, not session-wide," using "the minimum of both avatars' confidence at that instant." But the clustering computation described is a single graph built from session-wide accumulated net damage — there is no sliding window, no re-run schedule, no decay. A static clustering run once over the whole session cannot produce a genuinely time-varying confidence at instant *t* without a second, unspecified mechanism (e.g., windowed graphs recomputed per bucket). The design states the *requirement* (drift-aware) and the *desired output* (per-instant confidence) but never bridges them — this is inference dressed as a solved problem, not a described one. It also undermines the walkthrough claim that "team membership can change during a session" is actually handled: nothing in the analysis model changes an avatar's cluster mid-session, only its confidence number (drawn from a static computation) that the text asserts, without mechanism, varies with time.

**"Front unbending" has no tractable definition of the boundary it claims to project onto.** §6: "project every position onto signed distance to the instantaneous boundary between the two teams' kernel density estimates." A KDE difference's zero-contour is an implicit curve with no closed form; "signed distance to it" requires either (a) sampling a dense grid and running a distance transform per time bucket, for every bucket in a 1–2 h session, and re-deriving it any time the officer changes the chosen team pair, or (b) some other approximation the entry doesn't name. This is not a "draw a line" operation, it's a nontrivial 2D geometry computation repeated over the whole timeline, sitting behind a UI action ("pick two teams") that the entry implies is instant. At minimum this needs a stated approximation (e.g., 1D projection onto the line joining team centroids, updated per coarse time bucket) — as written it's unbuildable at interactive latency and the entry doesn't flag it as a risk the way it flags rung-1 trail rendering.

**Reaction-time and fire-while-occluded signals silently assume a solved LOS/view-cone check.** "Time from entering an attacker's plausible view cone to returning accurate fire" requires computing, for every candidate suspect, when the target entered a cone *and* was not occluded — i.e., it needs the same raycast machinery as the death LOS verdict, applied continuously rather than at one instant, which is a much higher raycast volume (one cast per track sample pair per candidate window, not one cast per death). The entry never says this reuses the LOS module, never budgets the extra raycasts, and never states where the "human baseline" reaction time or "normal human range" yaw-rate constant comes from (a hardcoded guess is fine, but it should be named as one, not left implicit). "Fire-while-occluded" additionally *compounds* the LOS module's own known false-positive rate (travel time, lag, sim batching) into a suspicion signal about a person, which is a stronger claim needing a stronger caveat than the entry gives it — Q8's evidence-only framing is stated as policy, but this specific signal is more exposed to LOS error than a plain through-wall equipment claim and gets the same generic caveat.

**Unknown equipment/objects have no screen.** The brief is explicit that source/rezzer identity is "sometimes unknown." Every example screen (Avatar's "Weapons: SMG-X," Claim's "SMG-X — kills through walls") shows fully-resolved names; nothing in the design shows what an Equipment or Claim page looks like keyed on an object the viewer and bridge both failed to identify. This isn't cosmetic — a claim like "kills through walls" is exactly the kind of accusation an officer will point at an unnamed bullet key, and the entry has no rendering rule for that case (contrast with the LOS UNKNOWN handling, which does get an explicit hollow+`?` glyph rule).

## 2. Where inference is presented as fact

The entry is generally good about attaching confidence numbers to inferred quantities (team %, friendly-fire conf, Wilson intervals on claims), but there is one clear lapse exactly where the brief cares most:

**Per-event LOS verdicts outside the Claim page drop the caveat.** §6 says explicitly: "The travel-time/lag caveat text is permanently attached to the Claim page, not a one-time tooltip." That sentence *scopes the caveat to the aggregate Claim page only*. The Death/Event page wireframe (§4) shows `LOS: CLEAR (4/4 candidates)` as a bare line with no caveat, and the Q3 walkthrough — "why did X die at 14:32" — is the brief's own headline scenario for this exact failure mode ("checking if a death happens where people were particularly tracking... taking into account false positives due to bullet travel time"). By the entry's own design, the single-death page an officer actually opens to answer that question shows a flat CLEAR/BLOCKED with only a candidate count, and the false-positive caveat lives one hop away on a page about equipment aggregates, not about this death. This is inference (a probabilistic ray-cast verdict with known false-positive modes) rendered as if it were a settled fact, in the one place the brief asked hardest for it not to be.

**"Killing blow" is fine, worth noting as the good case.** The plan confirms `DEATH.type` is server-grafted from the last DAMAGE event, so "killing blow = last event in window" is actually recovering server-authoritative fact, not inferring it — the entry doesn't overclaim here.

## 3. Internal inconsistency: what is a "Zone"?

§3 defines Zone as an auto-computed noun ("auto-clustered patch of ground") with a fixed identity (density clustering over the whole session, named by compass offset from spawn — see §6). §2's rung-1 ladder step describes a completely different mechanism producing the same noun: "drag-select a dense knot of trails → pushes a Zone page." A manual marquee selection over an arbitrary, unclustered region of the map is not the same object as a precomputed density-cluster centroid with a stable name like "NW Wall" — one has a persistent identity or it doesn't. The document's own stated principle in §3 is "one class of object, one page shape each"; Zone as specified violates that with two different construction paths that aren't reconciled (does drag-select snap to the nearest precomputed cluster? spawn an ad hoc unnamed zone? the entry doesn't say, and the Session page implies only three fixed, named zones exist).

## 4. XUI / overlay buildability — the good news

Checked against the brief's stated primitives (immediate-mode widgets, custom LLView drawing, lines/rings/arrows/crosses/quads/labels/per-vertex alpha/depth test/screen-rect picking):

- Every 2D screen (Session, Avatar, Death, Team, Claim, Zone) is scroll lists, text, sliders, buttons, and small custom-drawn sparkline/bar strips — no table needs anything beyond what a custom LLView bar-chart widget already does elsewhere in this codebase (the scrub strip in the main floater is explicitly the model). **No screen requires a web view.**
- The single-stack-of-pages, breadcrumb, and pin-rail navigation model is ordinary floater content-swapping; nothing here needs more than what one resizable floater with a swappable content panel already provides.
- Overlay layers (head ring/arrow/label, eye glyph, damage line with pulse, death cross+ring, trail polylines with alpha ramp, translucent heat disc, LOS candidate dots+ray, hollow+`?` UNKNOWN glyph, white selection outline) are all literally the primitives the brief lists, and the entry correctly reuses the depth-test-twice trick (implied, matches the plan's occlusion-readability convention) rather than inventing new GL state.
- The entry's own §8 already flags the two real perf risks correctly: rung-1 full-session trail rendering needs decimated/cached polylines (right diagnosis, right fix — recompute-on-demand, not per-frame), and small-marker picking at distance needs a minimum screen-space pick radius decoupled from world distance (also correct, and consistent with the plan's existing screen-rect pick precedent).
- One perf risk the entry does *not* flag, despite flagging the adjacent one: the front-unbending KDE-boundary computation (see above) has no stated decimation/caching strategy at all, unlike the trail-rendering case it explicitly budgets for.

## What must be kept

- The floater-is-chrome / overlay-is-primary-display split, and "if it can be drawn on the world, draw it on the world" as the organizing discipline — this is the single strongest idea in the entry and should survive into any merged design.
- The single navigation stack + breadcrumb + capped pin rail: teachable in one sentence, and correctly identified by the entry itself (§8) as trading away side-by-side comparison — an honest, bounded trade-off rather than a hidden one.
- Collapsed-by-default lists inside pages ("show all", "Evidence [expand]") as the in-window expression of the same minimalism, rather than only applying it floater-to-floater.
- The rung-1 decimated/cached-polyline requirement and the pick-radius-independent-of-distance requirement — both correctly diagnosed perf constraints that any competing entry will also need.
- Wilson-interval framing for equipment claims (avoids the "2/2 reads as 100%" trap) and the evidence-only, non-accusatory framing for Q8 suspicion signals — both correctly matched to the brief's honesty requirements.
- The observation that FFA sessions should degrade to "unassigned is the largest group" rather than forcing a false two-team split — correct and should be a hard requirement on whatever clustering algorithm actually ships.
