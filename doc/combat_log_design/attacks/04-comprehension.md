# Attack on Entry 04 — The Cartographer (MAP-FIRST)

Lens: COMPREHENSION. Question: does an officer actually move up and down the ladder of abstraction and dig from whole-raid to a single hit and back without losing the thread, does the design survive freeform combat, is uncertainty honest, is the 3D overlay usable while alt-camming.

## Walkthrough 1 — "How did this raid go, overall?"

Following §7: open the War Map, read "A holds 58% (14:40)," switch to Territory, scrub. This works cleanly *if* the session resolved into two teams. Two problems surface immediately:

- The status line's single glance number is defined only for the two-team case. The brief explicitly lists neutral sims where "group allegiances may exist, may drift, or it may become a deathmatch where everyone fights everyone" — i.e. a spectrum, not a binary. The entry's own analysis model computes the front as the zero-crossing of `O(A) − O(B)`, which is structurally two-sided. For three loose factions (a very ordinary state for a neutral sim before it collapses into a scrum), there is no rung between "two-team front" and "FFA duel graph." The thesis promises "everything is secretly a spatial question," but the moment there are 3+ factions, the tool has nothing spatial to say about the *shape* of the fight — it drops straight to a node-and-edge duel graph, which is exactly the "pile of views" failure mode the thesis was supposed to avoid.
- After scrubbing to inspect the collapse (clicking the biggest heat cell, opening its Place page, confirming at Rung 0), the officer wants to return to "how did the raid go overall." But nothing in the design restores the War Map's prior view. "Show on map" recentres and crops; clicking a Front Timeline point "sets the War Map's time window centred there and crops it to that stretch." These are the *only* documented map-state transitions, and they are all one-directional zooms-in. There is no map-view history/undo paired with the Inspector's page history. The Inspector remembers where you've been in noun-space; the map — the hub the whole thesis is built on — does not remember where you've been in view-space. After three or four digs, reconstructing "the whole-session, whole-map, Territory-mode view I started from" requires manually resetting Δt, the spatial crop, and the layer toggles. That is losing the thread, in the one place (the hub) where the thesis said losing the thread couldn't happen.

## Walkthrough 2 — "Why did X die at 14:32?"

This one is genuinely good. Click the death cross (map, list, or in-world — one target model, three surfaces) → Death page → build-up list with per-hit LOS verdicts → the killing blow rendered as `BLOCKED(!)` with the travel-time caveat and candidate list spelled out inline, not summarized into a verdict → "Show in-world" for the sightline fan. This is the strongest part of the entry: it genuinely walks down from a summary claim to raw candidate geometry, and the caveat text is structurally unavoidable (§8 explicitly rules out hiding it).

One real gap: "Show in-world" is documented as *not* moving the camera ("moves the timeline cursor and, if the officer is already stationary+alt-cam, highlights the marker"). Given the engineering constraint that the overlay only draws while stationary and alt-camming, and that no scripted camera jump is proposed, this is probably the honest answer — but the entry doesn't say what the officer sees if they are not already standing near "the ramp." Nothing (no marker, no overlay draws that far away without the camera near it, no compass/bearing hint on the map toward the officer's own position). An officer who clicks "Show in-world" from their desk chair with the camera parked at spawn gets nothing and no explanation of why. This needs either a bearing indicator or an explicit statement that the officer must alt-cam to the map location first.

## Walkthrough 3 — "Did anyone behave suspiciously?"

The entry's own walkthrough for Q8 only enters through the **Equipment** page ("Open Equipment for the weapon in question... through-wall suspicion rate... kill-location mini-map clusters near one wall corner"). That answers a *weapon* suspicion question well. But Q8 as phrased in the brief is about a *person*: "implausible reaction, speed, awareness through walls." The Avatar dossier page (§3) lists "movement summary, mouselook time, kills/deaths, weapons used, team + confidence, mini trail-map" — there is no reaction-time metric, no "faced/tracked a target with no LOS and no visible cue" signal, nothing that would let an officer start from a *person* (a promotion candidate, a reported player) and ask "is Kessa's aim or awareness suspicious" without already having a weapon in mind. The design has built excellent per-weapon suspicion tooling and left the symmetric per-avatar case as an unaddressed gap, even though the brief's Q8 is written in avatar-first language and Q9 explicitly names "investigating a reported violation" (against a person, not an item) as a target workflow.

## Freeform combat survival

- **Classic raid**: well served — the front/territory rungs are built for exactly this case.
- **Intra-group skirmish**: handled thoughtfully. Seeding purely from who-shoots-whom, hatched A/B pattern under one group colour, and "officer-asserted" overrides that survive re-clustering are all correct, specific answers to a brief requirement that's easy to hand-wave. Keep this.
- **Neutral sim with drifting allegiance / 3+ factions**: not really handled, per above — the design jumps from "two clean teams" straight to "no teams, use duels," with nothing for the messy middle the brief explicitly calls out.
- **People arriving mid-fight**: two specific mechanisms break under this, both under-examined relative to how central the scenario is:
  - Staging/spawn detection clusters "each avatar's first track sample of the session." A handful of people teleporting into the sim mid-raid and landing at the same regional TP-in point (common in SL) will DBSCAN into a spurious "Staging-C" Place page that has nothing to do with a real staging area. §8 only flags the milder version of this risk ("may mislabel a mid-fight rally point as a spawn"), not the TP-landing-point artifact, which is a distinct and more likely failure mode for exactly the scenario named in the brief.
  - The FFA duel graph anchors each node at the avatar's "session-median location... so the graph stays a map, not a force-layout." For someone who arrived mid-fight, moved across the sim, and fought in two unrelated spots, the median of those positions can land somewhere neither fight happened (including inside a wall or off the fightable area), actively misleading the one visual the design offers for this scenario. The design's stated goal here — "space is the axis, even when teams don't exist" — is undermined by its own placement rule for exactly the population (transient/mobile combatants) the scenario is meant to stress.

## Uncertainty: honest or decorative?

Mostly honest, and this is a real strength: the entry's own risk section bans hiding the confidence band by default, the through-wall rate is always shown with a sample size rather than a bare percentage, the LOS caveat text is structurally attached to the verdict rather than summarized away, and manual team overrides are explicitly protected from silent re-clustering. That is uncertainty treated as a first-class citizen, not a tooltip.

One place it isn't fully honest: the team-clustering **margin** (assigned-cluster edge weight minus best alternative, normalised) drives both the confidence slider and the visual solidity of team colour. For an avatar with very few recorded interactions (arrived late, barely fought), a near-empty edge set can produce a deceptively confident-looking margin purely because there's little competing evidence to be "the best alternative" against — the same small-sample problem the design is careful to caveat for through-wall suspicion ("small sample" is called out explicitly there) is not addressed for team confidence, where it matters just as much and is rendered as a hard visual (solid vs. hatched) rather than a percentage with a sample-size footnote.

## 3D overlay while alt-camming

The design is coherent here: same fields, same colour language as the map, gated exactly per the already-decided rule, sightline fan drawn live on click, dash-gap-width standing in for the map's grey-band-width so confidence reads the same way in both spaces. That consistency is worth keeping.

Two unresolved practicalities: the adaptive grid's ~4 m leaf size is tuned for the map's zoomed-out canvas; the entry doesn't say whether the ground-projected heat/territory quads get re-tiled or LOD'd for close-up viewing while alt-cammed near ground level, where a 4 m grid over real terrain clutter could be visually busier than the clean 2D canvas suggests. And the map-based "you-are-here wedge" implies the officer is expected to watch the 2D map and the 3D world at once — reasonable for a wide floater layout, but the entry never addresses screen real estate or update cadence for that split-attention mode, which is exactly the mode the owner said is the tool's primary use ("primarily used having to stand still and alt-cam around").

## What must be kept

- The single shared field set (trails/heat/territory/sightline) rendered with identical semantics on the 2D map and the 3D overlay — this is the one piece of the entry that actually earns the Bret Victor citation, because the officer never has to relearn an encoding when moving between rungs.
- The wiki-style Inspector: one page skeleton (header/breadcrumb/body/related/backlinks) reused across all noun types, real Back/Forward history plus a pin strip for comparison. This is a correct, XUI-feasible answer to "digging like Wikipedia articles."
- The policy that the confidence/uncertainty rendering can never be silently hidden or collapsed into a bare verdict (LOS caveats, through-wall rate with sample size, grey contested band).
- Manual team overrides recorded as "officer-asserted" and immune to being overwritten by re-clustering — a precise answer to the brief's requirement that allegiance stay an overridable inference.
- The intra-group-skirmish handling (who-shoots-whom seeding, hatched same-colour split, friendly-fire semantics that don't misfire during the resolving period) — this is the hardest scenario in the brief and the entry gets the mechanics right.
- The Death page's build-up list with a per-hit LOS verdict and an inline candidate breakdown for the killing blow, rather than a single yes/no — the strongest single artifact in the entry and a direct, well-executed answer to Q3.
