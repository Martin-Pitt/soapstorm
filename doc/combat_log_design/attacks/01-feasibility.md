# Attack on Entry 01 — LADDER-FIRST: the rung is the app
## Lens: analysis rigor + XUI/engine buildability

## Verdict up front

The rung/wiki split is a genuinely strong organizing idea and mostly honest about uncertainty. But several derived quantities either have no realistic computation path within the decided plan's cost budget, or are described with an algorithmic order that is circular as written, and the design's own headline invariant ("up-then-down is the identity") contradicts its own stated navigation rule. None of the eight screens need a web view — everything is drawable in XUI/LLView/renderDebug primitives — but two screens (R3 Field's Matrix/Braid and the Exposure/Reaction metrics feeding R1's Life strip) are described at toy scale (6 avatars) and do not survive scaling to the plan's stated ~41 agents / 100k events without a caching story the entry never commits to.

## Fatal / serious findings

**1. Exposure and Reaction-interval metrics (analysis model #13, #14) have no affordable computation path.**
Exposure is defined as "fraction of a life with clear LOS to ≥ 1 hostile, sampled at 2 Hz." That requires, for every 2 Hz sample across every avatar's life, a world-geometry raycast against *every other hostile avatar's position at that instant*. For a 5-minute life against, say, 20 potential hostiles: 600 samples × 20 = 12,000 raycasts, and the plan's own decided LOS primitive (`lineSegmentIntersectWorldGeometry`, main-thread, immediate-mode) is the same one the Risks section admits costs "16 casts per event" for a *single* death sweep, capped at "200 casts per frame ... filling in behind a visible computing hatch." A 1–2 h raid with dozens of lives implies hundreds of thousands to low millions of raycasts to keep Exposure current — this is not "on demand" in any sense compatible with 60 fps main-thread immediate mode, and the entry's only budget language (the 200-cast/frame cap) is proposed for LOS *sweeps on click*, not for a background statistic that the R1 Life strip apparently wants precomputed and always visible. This is presented in the Analysis Model as a settled, computable quantity with no caveat about cost; it should either be re-scoped (compute only around damage events, not continuously) or dropped from the always-visible Life strip.

**2. Team-assignment seeding is described circularly.**
"#8 Team assignment": "seeded by shared active group id *only when* group ids differ across the graph's blocks — in an intra-group skirmish the seeds disable themselves." But *blocks* are the output of the clustering that the seed is supposed to bootstrap; you cannot know whether group ids "differ across the graph's blocks" before blocks exist. The mechanism as written requires the answer to decide the algorithm's input. A rescue is easy (check group-id diversity among the avatars in the graph directly, before clustering, not "across blocks") but the entry doesn't state that — it states the circular version as settled fact, which is exactly the kind of inference-presented-as-mechanism the brief warns against.

**3. The "up-then-down is the identity" invariant contradicts the entry's own descent rule.**
Section 1 states the design's central guarantee: "ascend a death into a bout, descend, you get that death back." But R1's down-rule (§2, R1) says: "click any mark → its moment; **bare `[` → the witness moment**" — i.e. the medoid death, not necessarily the one you ascended from. Unless there is hidden per-subject "last concrete instance" state that silently overrides the witness default (never mentioned, and would itself need to be reconciled with the "witness is deterministic" claim in §6.18), the stated invariant is false for the mechanism as specified. This is the design's own north-star claim, undermined by its own operator definitions — worth flagging precisely because it's advertised as a rigor guarantee.

**4. R−1 "Record" rung assumes a raw-log store the plan does not provide.**
The plan's `CombatEvent` struct (fixed fields, ~intentionally compact for the 100k-event / 2h memory budget) does not retain the original JSON bytes, per-event salvage state, or a persisted trust/lag byte trail beyond what's folded into typed fields. R−1 wants "raw JSON as it arrived... the `fd` dedup partner, and the salvage state if the batch was truncated" per event. This is buildable (raw text is small, ~100k × ~200-300 B ≈ 20-30 MB, within the plan's stated ~100-150 MB envelope) but it is an *undeclared new store* on top of the decided ones, and "salvage state" (which fields survived a truncated-array parse) is not tracked anywhere in the plan's ingest logic (Phase 2.1 says "salvage complete `{…}` elements" but doesn't say it records *which* fields were dropped per partial element). The entry should flag this as a required plan addition, not assume it exists.

## Non-fatal but real gaps

**5. Bout clustering position for DAMAGE events is underspecified.**
DBSCAN over `(x, y, t/κ)` needs a 2D point per event; DAMAGE carries no position (per brief, confirmed). The entry's fix — "position-less events sit at the participants' interpolated positions" — doesn't say *whose* position (attacker's? target's? midpoint?), and attacker/target can be many meters apart (a sniper shot). Midpoint biases the cluster location; attacker-only or target-only biases differently depending on which side moved. This materially affects bout boundaries and is left to the reader to guess.

**6. R3 Field (Matrix, Braid) is shown at toy scale (6 avatars) and does not state a scaling strategy.**
The plan's own worked status line says "41 agents"; a hostility matrix at N=41 is 1,681 cells, each with a mini sparkline/exponent glyph, redrawn from scratch as ASCII-density text — the entry gives no caching commitment for these panels (unlike the LOS sweep, which explicitly gets a cache-per-(event,τ-grid) budget in Risks). Redrawing a 40×40 grid of custom-glyph cells every frame while scrubbing is very likely fine at 60 fps for *static* content once the underlying numbers are cached, but the entry never says the Matrix/Braid values are cached versus recomputed on every paint tick — an omission that matters exactly because the brief calls out "incremental or on-demand" as the hard constraint.

**7. Killing-blow ordering for death attribution assumes DAMAGE arrives time-ordered.**
"#4 Death attribution... killing blow = last event" — the brief states combat log events "may lag the real hit by up to 1 s," and batches can be delivered out of strict order relative to the LSL `t` stamp versus receipt order. The plan sorts the event store by `t`, so "last event" by `t` is well-defined *if* every DAMAGE has a reliable `t`; but the wire contract (plan §Wire contract) says `t` is *optional* per event ("optionally add `t`... to each event"), with events lacking `t` falling back to receive time. A late-arriving, receive-time-stamped event near a death boundary could be mis-ordered relative to `t`-stamped siblings, silently changing which contributor is "the killing blow." The entry doesn't caveat this despite otherwise being careful about lag everywhere else.

## What must be kept

- **The rung/wiki orthogonality** (vertical = abstraction, horizontal = subject) is a clean, buildable navigation model; the Rung Row on every article and the shared back/forward stack are simple, feasible XUI constructs (scroll-list rows + a stack of `(rung, subject, face, cursor, window)` tuples).
- **The haze rule** (no aggregate without its unknown fraction drawn in the same mark) is the single best piece of discipline in the entry and should survive any rewrite — it's exactly what the brief demands ("honest uncertainty display is a first-class requirement") and it's cheap to enforce mechanically (every aggregate-drawing function takes a coverage/confidence argument it must render).
- **R2 Sweep as the LOS answer** (verdict-as-a-function-of-τ, never a scalar) directly and correctly neutralizes the brief's named false-positive trap (bullet travel time) instead of hiding it behind a badge — this is the strongest single idea in the entry and is fully drawable as a strip chart plus world ray-fan with existing primitives (lines, per-vertex alpha, screen-rect picking).
- **The intra-group and FFA walkthroughs** correctly refuse to assert team structure the data doesn't support (Braid shows `?`, Matrix declines to color blocks) — this matches the brief's explicit warning about active-group tags being useless in intra-group skirmishes, and should be kept regardless of what else changes.
- **The occlusion-as-through-wall-cue rendering trick** (draw every line twice, depth-tested + depth-off at 30%) is a real, cheap, already-precedented XUI/renderDebug technique (the plan cites the FS OTS debug block as template) and correctly turns "kills through walls" into something the officer can *see* rather than take on faith.
- **Volley as the load-bearing noun** is a good, cheaply-computable primitive (owner/rezzer/target run with ≤2 s gaps) that the raw log genuinely lacks and that death attribution, stats, and the Bout Ledger all need — worth keeping even if other analyses are rescoped.

## Screens needing a web view

None. Every screen (Ladder chrome, Moment strip, Bout Ledger, Sweep strip, Field's four faces, Session Board, Article) is describable as scroll lists, text, and custom `LLView` primitive drawing (lines/rects/text), consistent with the plan's `layout_stack`-of-panels approach. The scaling concern in finding #6 is a performance/caching gap, not a "needs HTML" gap.
