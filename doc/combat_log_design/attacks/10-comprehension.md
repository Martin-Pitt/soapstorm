# Attack on Entry 10 (In-World-First) — COMPREHENSION lens

## Verdict up front

The rung *concept* is good and the uncertainty encoding is the strongest part of the entry. But the navigation model has a hole at the exact place the brief cares about most: the breadcrumb/history tracks **nouns** (Session ▸ Avatar ▸ Death ▸ Weapon), while the ladder rungs are **floater checkbox state** (Trails/Heat/LOS fan/Front ribbon, trail length, confidence threshold). Those are two different state machines that the entry never reconciles. That produces a real "lose the thread" failure the brief explicitly warns against, plus a Q8-specific gap (no rung ever surfaces *candidate* suspicious avatars, only confirmed ones), plus a genuine SL-camera feasibility problem in the flagship "pin two dossiers and fly between them" move. None of these are fatal to the concept, but as written the design does not survive its own thesis test ("dig and dig... never losing the thread").

## Walking the officer questions

### Q1 — How did the raid go overall?

The entry's own walkthrough works reasonably well: Live → jump to start → Heat + whole-session trails → click brightest patch → cursor + trail window snap to 14:31–14:36 → toggle Front ribbon → check Overview small multiples. That's a genuine rung-1→rung-2→rung-3 traversal and it's the best passage in the entry.

The gap: after drilling into that hot patch, the officer has implicitly *lost* the whole-session Heat view — trail length silently narrowed to the clicked window (per §2's own text: "the trail collapses back to a single rung-0 ghost," and elsewhere "trails collapse to that window"). To ask "and what about the OTHER hot patch at the north gate," the officer must manually re-widen trail length and re-enable Heat from scratch. There is no "step back up without losing where you were" — no history entry was created for "whole-session heat, before I clicked." The breadcrumb strip only records noun visits (Session ▸ Avatar ▸ Death), never layer/window state, so undo-by-breadcrumb doesn't restore it either. For a 1–2 h raid with several hot patches (explicitly "2–3 bright patches" in the officer's own worked example) this is exactly the "pile of views" failure mode the brief is testing for, not a ladder the officer can freely walk up and down without cost.

### Q3 — Why did X die at 14:32?

This walkthrough is genuinely solid: heat patch → death cross → Death card → damage chain → killing-blow line → LOS fan (3 candidates, all red) → click one ray → rung 0 shows Owlblade's ghost behind the wall. The step-down is concrete, the uncertainty ("BLOCKED with the standard false-positive caveat") is attached at the point of use, and depth-tested double-draw is a nice touch (you *see* the wall causing BLOCKED, not just a red line). This is the one place the design clears its own bar. No serious complaint here beyond the general breadcrumb-doesn't-restore-layers issue: returning to Q1's raid-level view from here pays the same "lost my heat view" tax noted above.

### Q8 — Suspicious behaviour

This is where the design breaks down as a *ladder*. The walkthrough starts with "Officer opens the **suspected avatar's** dossier" — it presupposes the officer already knows who to investigate. Fine for the "reported violation" workflow (brief's Q9), but the brief's Q8 is phrased session-wide ("did **anyone** behave suspiciously"), and nothing in the design offers a rung-1/2 view that surfaces candidates. The Equipment card has a "through-wall suspicion tally," which is an aggregate over weapons, not avatars — it can tell you "AK-Prime looks wall-hacky" but not "these 3 avatars have the reaction-time or pre-aim pattern worth a look." Behaviour-evidence chips (reaction time, speed-vs-norm, turn-toward-unseen) live *only* on rung 0 (the individual avatar dossier) with no aggregate rung above them, so an officer who does not already have a suspect has to open every avatar's dossier one at a time across a 30–60 person raid to browse for anomalies. That's not "digging," that's linear search — the opposite of Ladder of Abstraction, and the entry's own architecture (§2) explicitly claims every rung has a step up; behaviour evidence never gets one. This should be named as a real gap rather than assumed solved by "the brief says never sum to a verdict" — refusing to *rank* is correct (matches the brief), but refusing to *aggregate for browsing at all* is a different, uncalled-for omission.

The walkthrough text itself is otherwise fine (evidence stays honest, sample sizes and caveats are shown, no verdict rendered — this matches the brief's explicit Q8 instruction well).

## Freeform combat, allegiance drift, intra-group skirmish

- **Two teams, same declared group.** The design seeds label propagation "by active group id" (§6) and *also* claims the same seeding auto-splits an intra-group skirmish into "Ashguard-A/Ashguard-B" (§7). Those two claims are in tension: if both true subgroups get the *same* seed (same active group tag), the seed pulls them toward one cluster rather than splitting them, and the split has to come from edge weights alone overpowering an identical seed. The entry never explains how the seed is prevented from smothering the split, and this is exactly the scenario the brief calls out by name (§"Data" of the brief, "friendly fire must not be misread as different team"). Worth a design answer, not left as an implementation detail — because getting it wrong means the tool actively *hides* the split it was asked to detect.
- **Three or more factions.** The front-line model is explicitly binary: "vector connecting the two largest team centroids" (§6). A neutral sim with three roughly-equal factions (plausible per the brief's "Neutral combat sims... deathmatch" case) has no coherent two-centroid axis, and the design's only stated fallback is the *two*-team-residual-blowout path ("wide/flat ribbon") or the *zero*-team duel-pair fallback (§7 FFA scenario). A genuine three-team fight is neither "one front" nor "no team" — it would likely present as a merely-wide ribbon that reads as "noisy FFA" when it's actually three coherent groups the tool has simply failed to represent. This is a real blind spot for a design whose central instrument (rung 3) is a single ribbon.
- **Mid-fight arrivals / departures.** Nothing addresses what a freshly-arrived avatar's dossier or ghost looks like before it has any damage-graph edges (no team, no confidence — is it "UNKNOWN" grey, or does it inherit whatever a stale/absent value defaults to?), nor what happens to a ghost when an avatar leaves the sim/logs off mid-review (does the trail just stop, fade, or freeze?). Track-quality staleness dashing (§5) covers *missing samples*, not *avatar no longer exists*; these are different situations an officer needs to tell apart and the entry doesn't distinguish them.

## Is uncertainty honest or decorative?

Mostly honest — this is the entry's strength. Concrete good bits: LOS fan renders *all* candidates including grey UNKNOWN rather than collapsing to one verdict; through-wall suspicion is always shown with its denominator ("3/4, small sample"); front-ribbon width literally *is* fit-residual, not a separate caption bolted on (though the Risks section itself flags officers might misread ribbon height as fact — a fair, self-aware catch); track quality (VIEWER/COARSE/BRIDGE + staleness) drives ghost alpha/dash directly instead of silently interpolating. The one place uncertainty tips toward decorative: the "confidence-tinted ghosts" driven by a live slider (§4A, §6) let the officer *choose* how much uncertainty to see. That's a legitimate abstraction-over-parameter (rung 2) move for exploring the confidence landscape, but the default resting state (wherever the slider happens to sit when the officer isn't touching it) determines what a *new* officer sees by default, and the entry never states a sane default position — a slider left at 0.0 makes every avatar "confidently" team-coloured regardless of actual confidence, silently converting honest uncertainty into a false-confidence display until someone thinks to touch the slider.

## Usable while alt-camming?

Two concrete feasibility problems, not just polish notes:

1. **"Pin two dossiers... physically fly between them to compare" (§3).** The tool's own governing constraint (brief, verbatim) is that movement negates the tool and the overlay only draws stationary + alt-cam + 5s after leaving mouselook/OTS. Flying to the opposite side of a sim to compare two pinned cards means: overlay goes dark the moment the avatar starts moving, and does not come back until 5 s after the avatar stops again at the new spot. The entry presents this as a smooth "spatial substitute for a browser tab," but it is actually a lossy, repeatedly-gated action — each comparison hop costs a movement window with *no* overlay at all, then a 5 s blackout. If "fly" instead means alt-cam-only repositioning without moving the avatar body, that avoids the gating problem, but then camera draw/orbit distance in SL is typically tens of meters, not comfortably "opposite sides of the sim" (up to 256 m) — the entry doesn't say which one it means, and neither reading is actually as effortless as advertised.
2. **Click-and-hold radial vs. alt-cam orbit drag (§4D).** Alt-cam navigation in SL is itself driven by click-and-drag on the world to orbit the camera. The entry's radial menu trigger is "click-and-hold... on any marker" — a held click on a marker while the officer is mid-orbit is a very plausible accidental trigger (the officer starts an orbit drag that happens to begin on top of a ghost or trail line), which would interrupt the exact camera-manipulation the tool depends on. Risk #3 in the entry only worries about the radial fighting SL's *object* pie menu; it never considers the radial fighting the *overlay's own* primary input method (drag-to-orbit). This is a bigger risk than the one actually written down.

Smaller alt-cam concern: link-chips on a billboard card are described as "sub-rects" for click-through (§4C), which at typical alt-cam viewing distances (tens of meters, angled, often partially occluded) are a fine-motor target; the entry never states a minimum on-screen size or a fallback (e.g., always-clickable via the Inspector's text mirror) for when the in-world chip is too small or foreshortened to hit reliably. The Inspector floater does mirror the *selected* card, which somewhat covers this — but the entry should say explicitly that link-chip clicking is guaranteed to also work from the 2D Inspector, not just imply it.

## What must be kept

- The Q3 walkthrough end-to-end: heat → death cross → damage chain → LOS fan → click one candidate → rung-0 confirmation with the wall visibly in shot. This is the design doing exactly what the brief asks.
- LOS candidate fan rendering *all* candidates (including UNKNOWN, dashed grey) instead of collapsing to a single verdict — directly answers the brief's "false positives due to bullet travel time" concern honestly.
- Front-ribbon width as literal fit-residual (uncertainty *is* the visual encoding, not a caption next to a confident-looking line) — and the Risks section's own honesty about officers misreading it.
- Refusing to sum behaviour-evidence chips into a suspicion score for Q8 — matches the brief's explicit instruction and resists the obvious, wrong shortcut.
- Through-wall suspicion always shown with its denominator ("3/4, small sample — treat as a lead").
- Track-quality-driven ghost alpha/dash (VIEWER/COARSE/BRIDGE + staleness) as a first-class, always-visible signal rather than silent interpolation.
- Depth-tested double-draw (solid depth-tested + faint x-ray pass) so occlusion is itself the evidence for a BLOCKED verdict.
- Reusing SL's existing pie-menu interaction language for the radial menu (learnable for free) — the *trigger condition* needs fixing, not the concept.
