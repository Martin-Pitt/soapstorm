# Attack on Entry 10 — In-World-First — ANALYSIS + FEASIBILITY lens

## Verdict

The thesis is coherent and the overlay/click/gating mechanics respect the fixed engineering plan closely (gating, plain-left-click-only, depth-tested double-draw, immediate-mode primitives, per-vertex alpha for staleness). But the design leans on two derived instruments — **battle heat** and, less severely, **damage flash lines** — that quietly assume DAMAGE events carry positions, which the brief states they do not. It also proposes a right-click/hold radial menu that collides with a constraint the fixed plan already nailed down (plain-left-click only, ALT/CTRL and drag untouched, to avoid fighting SL's pie menu). The front-line model is only defined for exactly two teams. One quantitative claim (a 180 ms reaction time) implies sampling precision the data cannot support. None of these are HTML/web-view violations — everything is drawable in XUI/LLView — but several are "inference presented as fact" and one is scope not covered by the fixed plan.

## Fatal

None. Every gap below degrades to "draw less" or "needs one more caveat line," not to a screen that cannot be built at all.

## Serious

1. **Battle heat (Rung 2) has no stated position source, and DAMAGE has no positions.** §4A/§5 describe "a ground-projected translucent field... coloured by damage-event density" as if density-over-space is a first-class fact. It is not: DAMAGE carries no `source_pos`/`target_pos` at all (brief, Data section; plan's "Verified facts"). Every point that feeds the heat field must be reconstructed from `sampleAt(owner_or_target, t)` on the avatar track store — itself uncertain (VIEWER/COARSE/BRIDGE quality, gaps >5 s marked unknown, COARSE z quantised to 4 m, COARSE update rate ~1 Hz). The entry's uncertainty table (§5) gives trails and the LOS fan an explicit uncertainty encoding but gives heat none — no dashing, no alpha reduction, no "n samples, m at COARSE quality" caption. A commander reading "the courtyard glows brightest" is reading a density map built on interpolated guesses for a fraction of its inputs, and the design presents it with the same visual confidence as the LOS fan, which does carry a caveat. This is the entry's clearest instance of "presents inference as fact." It also has no stated algorithm (grid resolution? spatial hash? recompute cadence as the cursor scrubs a "whole session" window over 100k events?) — "computed incrementally or on demand" is asserted, not designed.

2. **No fallback for damage events whose attacker has zero track coverage.** The damage line (Rung 0) and, transitively, the heat field both need a position for `owner`/`rezzer`/`source` at time `t`. The brief explicitly allows "sometimes unknown" for source/rezzer identity, and the engineering plan's own track-quality gap rule (`sampleAt` returns unknown past a 5 s gap) means some fraction of DAMAGE events — a bullet from an object nobody ever rendered, or an owner who was off-track at that instant — will have **no resolvable line and no resolvable heat contribution at all**. The design never states what happens then: does the flash simply not draw (silently undercounting the fight, contradicting the "haunted replay" thesis that every fact is walkable), or does it fall back to a generic "damage occurred, position unknown" glyph at the target only? Given this is foundational to Rung 0/1's core selling point, the silence is a real gap, not a stylistic one.

3. **Front-line model (Rung 3) is defined for exactly two teams via "vector connecting the two largest team centroids."** The brief explicitly requires supporting neutral/FFA sims where "group allegiances may exist, may drift" and multi-faction free-for-alls are called out as a design target (not just "no team at all," which the entry does handle via ribbon-residual blowout). A genuine three-or-more coherent faction fight (three guilds all fighting each other, each internally cohesive) produces two well-defined clusters by construction of "top two centroids," silently discarding the third faction's coherence and mis-plotting its members' distance-to-a-front that doesn't include them. The entry's FFA fallback (wide/flat ribbon) only fires when clustering confidence is uniformly low; it does not fire when confidence is high but the topology is N-way, so the ribbon will confidently draw a wrong 2-sided front for a real 3-sided fight. This is an unhandled case the brief asks to be designed for.

4. **Radial context menu is new interaction infrastructure, not covered by (and in tension with) the fixed plan.** The plan's Phase 4.4 click hook is deliberately narrow: "consume plain left-click (no ALT/CTRL, so alt-cam is untouched)... llviewerwindow.cpp hook next to SSAtmoMagic::handleInfoClick." There is no pick-driven radial/pie menu in the fixed architecture, and right-click is already claimed by SL's own object pie menu system-wide. The entry's own §8 risk list admits this ("the combat radial must trigger only when... or it will fight the base viewer's context menu") — which is an honest flag, but it means Screen D and the "Pin/Track/Compare/Assign team" affordances it depends on are unbuilt engineering outside the "already decided" scope, requiring a new hit-precedence system against the existing pie-menu dispatch (`llviewerwindow.cpp` mouse-down chain: mouse captor → root view → tool → pie-menu fallthrough) rather than a UI-layout addition. This should be counted as new scope in any cost estimate, not "reuses SL's existing pie-menu interaction language for free."

## Minor

5. **Behaviour-evidence chip states false precision.** §7's worked example — "reaction time 180 ms before first shot lands (n=1, could be lag)" — implies sub-200 ms timing resolution. Bridge ticks are 2 Hz nominal (500 ms) with Hermite interpolation filling gaps only when both neighbouring samples are within 2 s; a 180 ms figure is a product of interpolation smoothing, not measurement, for any avatar not sampled at VIEWER-update rates (2–10 Hz, itself only "roughly" and only "when moving"). The "n=1, could be lag" caveat covers sample-size and network lag but not the deeper problem that the number itself is manufactured by the interpolation model. A reader could reasonably treat 180 ms as measured fact.

6. **Heat/front recompute cost is unspecified.** Section 6 and the floater description assert "computed incrementally or on demand" (the brief's own performance requirement, restated) but never gives the algorithm: what spatial structure supports "click a hot patch → jump to density peak" across a 100k-event, multi-hour session without rescanning on every scrub frame? Contrast with the plan's own event/track stores, which specify concrete structures (sorted vector + binary search, per-avatar sample vectors with decimation). The design entry inherits the plan's stores but adds two new derived structures (density field, front-fit) without giving them the same rigor.

7. **Card sub-rect picking on billboards at scale is asserted, not budgeted.** §4C extends "screen-rect picking already planned for markers" to each link-chip's sub-rect on every visible card. With auto-decluttering promised only in §8 as an open risk ("needs an auto-declutter... needs a cap"), the base design as written has no cap, so worst case (60 avatars, several pinned cards, several chips each) is a few hundred picking rects recomputed every frame — almost certainly fine at that count, but the entry never states the number, so "fine at 60 fps" is not demonstrated, only assumed.

## What must be kept

- The gating discipline: plain-left-click-only pick, ALT/CTRL and camera drag untouched, no camera-mode forcing (`Focus in world` recentres the pivot but never switches modes) — this correctly matches the fixed plan and the brief's explicit "moving negates the tool" constraint.
- Honest-uncertainty mechanics that *are* specified: dashed/faded trail gaps for stale samples, LOS fan showing every candidate (including grey UNKNOWN) rather than one collapsed verdict, through-wall suspicion always shown with its denominator, behaviour evidence kept as separate uncorrelated chips rather than summed into a suspicion score (this directly satisfies the brief's explicit instruction for question 8).
- Depth-tested double-draw (full alpha depth-tested + 30% depth-off) as the occlusion cue — cheap, matches the plan's existing style rules, and doubles as the honest rendering of a BLOCKED verdict.
- Team-card treatment of intra-group splits as inferred labels ("Ashguard-A/B — inferred, same declared group") with an explicit manual-override path that becomes session ground truth — correctly matches the brief's requirement that allegiance is never presented as hard fact until an officer says so.
- The 2D small-multiples "Front Overview" panel as an explicit, self-justified fallback ("a floating in-world ribbon is hard to read from an arbitrary alt-cam angle") rather than pretending everything must live in-world — this is good judgement about where rung-3 abstraction genuinely needs a flat, comparable layout, and it stays inside XUI custom-drawn-panel feasibility.
- Card/breadcrumb/backlink navigation model reusing the plan's existing data stores (events, tracks, equipment registry) without inventing new persistence.
