# Attack on Entry 12 — Behaviour-Audit-First — ANALYSIS+FEASIBILITY lens

## Verdict up front

Entry 12 is unusually disciplined about hedging its *headline* risk (turning the tool into a witch-hunt), and every screen it proposes is drawable in XUI/LLView primitives with no web view anywhere. But under the hedging language sit three real computability holes and one recurring pattern: the entry treats **uncertainty sources it names in the Risks section as solved everywhere above that section**. The Risks section is honest; the screens above it are not always as honest as the Risks section promises they'll be.

## 1. Computability audit — can each derived quantity actually be built from the stated data?

### 1a. Awareness class (CLEAR/SOUND/BLOCKED/UNKNOWN) — computable, but conflates two different lag sources
The brief states two independent uncertainties: (1) DAMAGE/DEATH log events "may lag the real hit by up to 1 s" (a *timestamp* error), and (2) LOS verdicts have false positives from "travel time, lag, and sim batching" (a *shooter-position* error, handled by sweeping candidate positions over the travel window). Entry 12's LOS sweep (Engagement Card, §4.3) only sweeps candidate *shooter positions* backward from the event's own timestamp `t`. It never sweeps `t` itself. If the log event's timestamp is off by up to 1 s (independent of bullet travel time), the entire candidate window is shifted and every CLEAR/BLOCKED verdict in the sweep is anchored to the wrong reference instant. The design presents "flip at k=5, 0.75 s before hit" as if `t` (the "hit" instant) is ground truth; the brief says it isn't. This is the single largest "inference presented as fact" in the entry: the whole Awareness Ledger, the north-wall cluster story, and the Q3 walkthrough's "officer confirms visually" all rest on an event time that can itself be wrong by up to 1 s — a magnitude comparable to the entire travel-time window being swept. The fix is cheap (widen the sweep, or show a second, orthogonal timestamp-uncertainty band) but as written it is missing, not just under-hedged.

### 1b. Reaction latency — computable, but "target enters shooter's view cone" is not bounded, so it isn't incremental
"Became knowable" is defined as the earliest of three cues, one of which is "target enters shooter's last known view cone (yaw ± FOV from track)." To find that instant you must scan backward through the shooter's yaw-track and the target's position-track together until you find first entry — with no stated lower bound on how far back to look. In a session where a target was always visible-in-principle since spawn, this scan is effectively O(session) per death, computed at click-time, in a store sized for "100k events and millions of track samples." The entry's own analysis-model section for other quantities (baselines, through-wall rate) is careful to describe incremental/bounded computation; this one quantity has no bound stated. That's a genuine gap against the brief's explicit "must be incremental or on-demand" performance constraint — not fatal (a bound like "look back min(session start, 30 s before first damage from this pair)" fixes it) but it's absent from the text, and a reviewer implementing this cold would build the unbounded version.

### 1c. Minimap-fair shading — asserts a fact the brief says is unknowable
The brief explicitly lists "other viewers' settings" as not available, calling out wireframe as the paradigm case that "can only be suspected... never proven." Entry 12's minimap-fair ring assumes "a very faint ring at true minimap radar range around the shooter... fixed sim-wide radius" and uses it to *positively exonerate* ("minimap-plausible") a hit. Radar/minimap range in SL is not a server-enforced sim-wide constant that the viewer or bridge can read — it is influenced by client-side settings (some viewers extend or fake radar range, some restrict it) which is exactly the category of thing the brief says cannot be verified. This is the most consequential instance of inference-as-fact in the entry, because it is used to actively defuse suspicion rather than raise it — a false "minimap-plausible" tag could clear someone who wasn't. It needs the same treatment as the wireframe heuristic (hedged, sourced, never presented as a green checkmark) and currently gets none.

### 1d. Place page (chokepoint inference) — computable only from the sparse subset of events that carry positions
DAMAGE carries no positions; only DEATH carries `source_pos`/`target_pos`. The Place page ("where signals cluster geographically... inferred from death density") is therefore built from deaths only — a small fraction of the ~100k-event session (a 1–2 h raid might have dozens to a few hundred deaths against tens of thousands of damage events). This is computable, but the entry uses Place-page findings as load-bearing exculpatory evidence in two separate walkthroughs ("a known chokepoint... two other avatars unrelated to her show the same elevated BLOCKED rate there") without ever noting that the geographic signal it's built on is an order of magnitude sparser than the damage/LOS signal it's being used to explain away. A wall with three deaths near it is a much weaker "known chokepoint" than the confident tone implies. Flag: computable, but its statistical power is systematically overstated relative to the DAMAGE-derived signals it's used to countermand.

### 1e. Awareness/reaction "audible cue" model — the entry itself flags this (good), but it is load-bearing at rung 0
Already listed in the entry's own Risks section as "a guess... not verified." Correctly hedged in prose. But the SOUND-ONLY glyph at rung 0 (the "ear" icon drawn directly on the shooter's head in the overlay) presents this guess with the same visual authority as the CLEAR/BLOCKED glyphs, which *are* grounded in real raycasts. An officer skimming the overlay sees three equally solid-looking glyphs, not "two measured, one guessed." This is a rung-0 honesty regression relative to the entry's own stated design principle (documentation pages disclaim, but the concrete view doesn't visually distinguish measured vs. modelled).

### 1f. Everything else checks out
Through-wall rate (Wilson CI), damage adjustment ratio, speed flag (with the sensible COARSE-exclusion and ≥2-sample sustain rule), population baseline (median/IQR with minimum-N gating), team-conditioned baselines (reusing the plan's clustering unchanged), and the wireframe-suspect heuristic's three-way AND-gate (threshold + no-geometry-explanation + multi-target recurrence) are all built from fields the brief confirms exist, with sensible incremental/on-demand computation and no unstated position dependency. The wireframe heuristic in particular is a model example of hedging: never named "wallhacking" anywhere in the UI, requires corroboration across independent evidence, links to its own confounders. Nothing here needs positions DAMAGE doesn't have, and nothing here needs an object identity that could be UNKNOWN — the design correctly treats equipment identity as optional ("Unknown HUD-31" is a first-class row, not a crash).

## 2. Rendering feasibility — XUI immediate-mode + LLView, 60 fps, 100k events / millions of samples

Every screen in §4 is a scroll list, a tab container, or a custom `LLView` grid of rects+text (Fairness Matrix, Watchlist, Equipment Ledger) — all standard XUI/immediate-mode idioms already used elsewhere in the viewer (timeline strips, storm-debug overlays). None require anything resembling a web view. The threshold-slider live-reorder (Watchlist re-sorting as the slider drags) is a re-sort of at most a few dozen avatar rows per frame — trivial at 60 fps regardless of the 100k-event backing store, *provided* the per-avatar signal counts are precomputed/cached rather than recomputed by scanning the event log on every slider tick. The entry doesn't say whether the Watchlist counts are cached per-avatar-per-signal-type (they should be, and can be, since signals are discrete countable events) — this is an implementation note, not a design flaw, but it's the one place a naive read of "sweep the threshold live" could accidentally mean "rescan the log 60 times a second." Should be called out explicitly as "precomputed per-avatar signal counts, threshold only refilters/resorts the small avatar list," not left implicit.

The one genuinely new rendering/picking demand this entry adds beyond the fixed plan: the plan's picking model (`LLRect` per marker, pushed during `render()`) is designed for point-like markers. Entry 12's "clicking a ray jumps the cursor to that candidate's exact time" (LOS candidate fan, §5) requires picking a *thin line segment* in screen space, not a rect around a point — a materially different hit-test (distance-from-point-to-projected-segment with a pixel tolerance) that the plan does not budget for. It's a small, buildable extension of the existing pick-list mechanism, but the entry presents it as if it falls out of the plan's picking for free; it doesn't, and should be listed as a scope addition.

## 3. In-world overlay — drawable with lines/rings/text/rect picking?

Yes, entirely. Awareness glyphs (eye/ear/wall — good: geometry-coded, not colour-only, satisfying the plan's colour-blind-safe convention), view cone (a translucent wedge — a filled triangle fan, standard immediate-mode primitive), LOS candidate fan (N rays, cheap), engagement trail (polylines, same cost class as the plan's own avatar trails), minimap-fair ring (a flat ring primitive identical to existing ring-drawing helpers). All are within the plan's stated per-frame budget class (60 avatars × 20 s trails under 0.5 ms) since the fairness layers are toggled per-selected-avatar, not always-on for the whole roster — the entry says this explicitly ("toggled per-avatar from the Auditor, not always-on, to avoid clutter"), which is the right call and the one that keeps this inside budget.

## 4. Quantities with no computation path

None found that are *entirely* uncomputable. The three flagged above (1a timestamp-lag conflation, 1c minimap-fair ring, 1e audible-cue rung-0 display) are not "cannot be computed" — they are "computed from an assumption the brief says is unverifiable or incomplete, then displayed with more confidence than the assumption warrants." 1b (reaction latency's view-cone backscan) is computable but not bounded/incremental as specified, which is a performance defect against the brief's explicit constraint, not a correctness-impossibility.

## 5. Screens needing a web view

None. Every table, sparkline, heat-swatch grid, and breadcrumb bar is buildable from XUI scroll lists, tab containers, and custom-drawn `LLView` grids.

## What must be kept

- The organising discipline itself: never a composite score, always instance counts + a visible, adjustable threshold; sort by nothing that isn't a raw count.
- The Signal documentation page as a first-class noun — every number links to its own formula and its own known false-positive causes. This is the single best structural idea in the entry and should survive into whatever design wins.
- Wilson-interval-first display for small-n equipment stats (interval before point estimate) — directly prevents the "one lucky hit through a wall" gotcha reading.
- Counter-evidence requirement on the Avatar Page (every clustered signal must show whether the Place page independently explains it) — even though Place-page evidence is sparser than the entry admits, the *requirement to check* is correct and should be kept; just tighten the confidence language on Place itself.
- Per-cluster (not per-group-tag) baselines for intra-group skirmishes, and explicit whole-population fallback with a stated reason for FFA sims — both are correct, minimal, and directly answer the brief's stated failure modes (active-group-tag uselessness, low-confidence clustering).
- The audit trail requirement on baseline-outlier-exclusion (Risks section) — necessary to prevent "recompute without Kaela" from becoming a laundering tool; should be a hard requirement in any surviving design, not an open question.
