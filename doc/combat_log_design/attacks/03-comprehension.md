# Attack: Comprehension — Entry 03 (Officer Workflows)

Lens: does an officer actually move up and down the ladder of abstraction, dig from whole-raid to a single hit and back without losing the thread, survive freeform combat, get honest (not decorative) uncertainty, and get a usable 3D overlay while alt-camming?

## Walking Q1 — "how did this raid go, overall?"

Per the entry's own walkthrough: Overview tab → read heat trail/front list → click "Open Briefing" → Case Binder (Post-Raid Briefing) opens with a pre-filled Digest → scrub timeline for the two front collapses → pin the heat-trail snapshot and Team Ledger grid → edit narrative → export.

That is Rung 1 (heat trail) → Rung 3 (Team Ledger) → Rung 4 (Case), one direction, no return trip. The Overview wireframe itself lists "Top event: 41 dmg kill @ 14:32:07" — an obvious invitation to step down and verify a concrete instance before writing it into a briefing — but the walkthrough never clicks it. The entry's own thesis (§1) says "the deepest insights emerge... in the transitions between [rungs]," and the brief asks explicitly whether the officer can dig to a hit and back. For the one question the brief poses as the "whole raid" question, the answer given is a downhill pipeline: skim → summarize → export. No step down, so no step back up either. This is exactly the "pile of views" failure mode the lens is checking for, on the one walkthrough where the design had every opportunity to show otherwise.

## Walking Q3 — "why did X die at 14:32?"

This is the strongest part of the entry. Roster → Mara Lindqvist Dossier → click the death tick on her timeline ribbon → Inspector opens on Death 14:32:07 → three related damage lines + LOS sweep → scrub the sweep slider → pin the reading and the full damage cluster → "Track killer" to Owen Vance's Dossier → his weapon's Dossier for the wall-kill suspicion score → pin that too. That is a genuine vertical dig with a return path (breadcrumb "Cases > Kestrel Voss > Death 14:32:07") and it ends in a verdict field the tool explicitly refuses to fill in. Good.

Two things break even this best case:
- It never reconnects to the whole-raid picture from Q1. Was 14:32:07 during one of the "two front collapses" the Overview flagged, or was it in a quiet rear area — which is exactly the context that would make a "no-LOS kill" complaint look more or less suspicious? The walkthrough climbs Owen Vance's weapon stats but never climbs back to the Overview/fronts to ask "was this victim where she should have been?" The ladder has a missing rung connection between the death-instance investigation and the session-shape view that would contextualize it.
- The Inspector's own worked example is internally inconsistent (see "LOS fraction" finding below), which matters most here because this is the walkthrough the brief cares most about getting right.

## Walking Q8 — "did anyone behave suspiciously?"

Reaction-time percentile (48th, unremarkable), no occluded-awareness flags, both pinned as counter-evidence, completeness checklist forces a prior-history check before a verdict. This is disciplined: it produces evidence cards with raw numbers and counter-explanations, never a "cheating: yes/no" flag, which is exactly what the brief demands. Good.

But notice what's missing from the walkthrough: the in-world overlay is never opened. The owner's stated primary use case is "stand still and alt-cam around" — the overlay is not a nice-to-have, it's supposed to be the main organ of comprehension — yet across all three walkthroughs (Q1, Q3, Q8) the officer never narrates looking at it. "Show in world" buttons exist on every screen, but no walkthrough exercises one. A design whose flagship rung is present in the wireframes but absent from every worked example of actually answering an officer's question is a warning sign that the overlay was designed but not actually load-bearing for comprehension.

## Specific breaks

**LOS fraction contradicts the plan's own verdict rule.** The Inspector wireframe shows:
```
-1.1s  18 dmg  bullet   LOS BLOCKED (conf low, 1/5 candidates)
-0.0s  41 dmg  bullet   LOS BLOCKED (conf med, 2/5 candidates)
```
The engineering plan's LOS rule (Phase 5 §5, which this entry explicitly does not redesign) is: "CLEAR if any candidate clear, BLOCKED if all blocked, UNKNOWN if the track has no samples in the window." There is no "partial/mixed" verdict — if even one candidate is clear, the verdict is CLEAR by definition. So a displayed "BLOCKED (2/5 candidates)" is ambiguous and, on the most natural reading (2 of 5 candidates clear), is flatly wrong under the stated rule — it should read CLEAR. If "2/5" instead means "2 of 5 candidates had track data at all" (the rest UNKNOWN due to gaps), that's a different and legitimate reading, but the entry never says which, and conflating "no data" with "blocked" would itself violate the rule (a gap is UNKNOWN, not BLOCKED). This is the single feature the brief spends the most words worrying about false positives on, and the entry's own worked example for the walkthrough that matters most (Q3) can't be read as self-consistent. This isn't cosmetic — an officer reading this Inspector cannot tell whether the killing blow verdict would flip to CLEAR if they understood the fraction correctly, which is the entire point of showing uncertainty honestly.

**Intra-group clustering has no seed to grow from, and the entry doesn't notice.** The base plan's team-clustering algorithm seeds from "shared active group id." The brief spends a full bullet stressing that in an intra-group skirmish "active group tag is then useless for team detection; only who-shoots-whom over time reveals sides." In that scenario the seed step degenerates — both sub-teams share the same active-group id, so the seeding heuristic that boots the label-propagation can't distinguish them at all. The entry's Team Ledger and its intra-group walkthrough ("clustering has split 'Ashguard' into two posterior clusters at threshold 0.55... drags the slider down to 0.35 to see if the boundary is stable") simply assumes the clustering already exists and is well-behaved, without ever addressing how a damage-graph-only signal produces two clusters when the seed signal that the base algorithm depends on is uninformative by construction. This is the flagship scenario the brief calls out, and it's also the flagship "digging" scenario in this entry's own Ladder section (rung 3 → override); it deserved at minimum a note that the seed strategy must fall back to something else (e.g., pure modularity on the damage graph with no group-id prior) when group ids collapse to one value. Section 8 (Risks) lists four risks and none of them is this one — a real oversight, not a flagged and accepted one.

**In-world click semantics are overloaded in a way that fights the "stationary + alt-cam" use case.** Section 5 layers three different behaviors onto the same physical action: plain click on an untacked marker → open Inspector; plain click on a tacked marker → reopen (and scroll) the Case Binder; Ctrl-click on any marker → pin-to-case menu. An officer alt-camming through a cluttered fight (the brief's own scenario — dozens of avatars, many deaths, many damage lines) cannot visually tell at a glance which markers are tacked before clicking, so "I want to peek at this death" can unpredictably yank a completely different floater (the Case Binder) into view and steal focus from the careful camera position the officer just set up. That's a direct tax on the one workflow the owner said matters most. A hover preview or a dedicated modifier for "open the pinned card" (rather than overloading plain click by tacked-state) would avoid this.

**Ambiguous "currently open Case" when several are open.** The Cases tab explicitly supports multiple simultaneous Case Binders (Promotion Review + Violation Investigation + Post-Raid Briefing after the same raid is a completely normal officer day). The overlay's tack glyphs and Ctrl-click "pin to case" both refer to "the currently open Case" as if there is exactly one. With several Case Binder floaters open at once — which the IA explicitly allows — which one receives a Ctrl-click pin, and whose accent colour do tacks use? Undefined.

**Breadcrumb/navigation-stack scope is unclear across simultaneous floaters.** The nav stack is described as a single mechanism ("Cases > Kestrel Voss > Death 14:32:07") shown identically in both the Dossier and the Inspector wireframes. If it's a single global stack, opening a killer's Dossier and a victim's Dossier side by side (which Q3-style investigations obviously want) will have them clobber each other's back/forward history. If it's per-floater-instance, the entry never says so, and the shared breadcrumb notation implies otherwise. "Digging without losing the thread" is precisely the property this ambiguity threatens.

**FFA per-encounter evidence has no described interaction.** The FFA walkthrough's payoff move is pinning "short-lived local clusters ('cluster around the north platform, 22:40–22:52') as separate evidence cards." Nothing in the Screens section describes how an officer selects a spatiotemporal region of the Overview heat trail (no brush, lasso, or time-range picker is drawn or mentioned) to produce that evidence card. This is asserted in prose in the walkthrough but has no home in the actual screen designs.

## What survives

- The Q3 dig (Roster → Dossier → tick → Inspector → LOS sweep → pin → killer Dossier → weapon Dossier → pin) is a real, working vertical traversal with a return path via breadcrumb. It should be the template every other walkthrough is checked against — the entry should redo Q1 and Q8 to match this pattern, including at least one genuine step-down-then-up.
- The friendly-fire "disputed if the flag flips across the plausible threshold range" mechanism is a genuinely honest treatment of uncertainty — it doesn't hide behind a single number, it shows exactly the instability that matters for a call the officer has to make.
- The wall-kill suspicion score's confidence-from-sample-count, "never suppressed" rule is the right instinct and matches the brief's demand that an under-sampled claim be visibly weak rather than hidden or falsely confident.
- The Q8 evidence-cards-not-verdicts pattern (percentile framing + explicit counter-explanations, no yes/no flag) is exactly what the brief asked for and should be kept unchanged.
- Dashed-for-unknown-position as a hard visual rule (never solid = "nothing happened") is a small but load-bearing honesty rule; keep it.
- The Case Binder as the terminal "digging ends in something" destination, with pinning available from every view including in-world, is a sound synthesis of the brief's wiki-digging and Bret Victor asks — the concept survives even though several of its mechanics (click overload, multi-case ambiguity) need fixing.
