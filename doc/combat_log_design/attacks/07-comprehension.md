# Attack on Entry 07 (Equipment-first Combat Log) — Comprehension lens

## Verdict up front

There is a real ladder mechanism here — one shared Inspector that re-skins per noun, a breadcrumb, pinning, backlinks — and that is more than most "pile of floaters" designs manage. But the thesis (equipment as the mandatory front door) quietly breaks the ladder at exactly the rungs the brief cares about most: the whole-raid, whole-session, "where did this happen" view. You can climb *down* from any noun into equipment smoothly. You cannot climb *up* from equipment into "the raid" without already knowing which equipment mattered. That's a one-way ladder dressed as a two-way one.

## Walking Q1: "How did this raid go, where, when, between whom?"

The entry's own walkthrough answers this by: read the Overview timeline (two humps), read the Equipment Leaderboard sparklines (same two-hump shape, "confirming" it's session-wide), read the team-damage bar (61/39), then "turn on Fire Fans for the top two weapons" and eyeball where their lines cluster to answer "where."

That last step is the tell. "Where did the fighting happen" is being answered by "where did the top-8-by-damage weapons fire," not by an engagement-density view of the session. Those are not the same question. A raid where damage is spread across a dozen different sidearms at one contested doorway, and a second raid where one overpowered rifle racks huge numbers off in a corner nobody else fought at, would look *identical* on this leaderboard-and-fan path if the corner fight's single weapon out-damages the doorway's dozen. The officer would point at the wrong place. There is no session-wide "every hit, every location, weapon-agnostic" rung-1 view — the design has an avatar-trail equivalent (per the engineering plan's overlay layers) but 07 never surfaces it as an entry point; its own §5 overlay section only describes per-shot, per-fan, per-LOS-sweep layers, all of which require a prior selection. Put the officer at rung 0 with nothing selected and alt-camming: what do they see? The entry doesn't say, because rung 0 is defined as "the exact firing position to the exact hit position" of *a* shot, not "the live state of the raid at time t" as the brief's own ladder text requires ("Rung 0 ... the live 3D overlay at the cursor time"). This is a scope mismatch with the brief's own definition of rung 0, not just a stylistic choice.

## Walking Q3: "Why did X die at 14:32?"

This one works. Click the death tick → Death Dossier → damage ladder → LOS sweep with an honest CLEAR-among-4-BLOCKED read-out → "Open SA-90 >" to the equipment-first exit. Digging down to a killing blow and back up via breadcrumb is clean and the thread isn't lost. This is the design's strongest path because a *death* is a first-class noun with its own dossier.

But the brief is explicit that a bare *damage event* (not just a death) must be inspectable: "Clicking on a death or damage event could show in the floater allowing you to inspect events and related events together." Entry 07 has no dossier for a non-lethal hit. §3's noun list is Equipment / Equipment family / Death / Avatar / Team / Unknown object — a single DAMAGE event that never killed anyone is not a noun. In-world, clicking a rung-0 marker for a non-lethal shot, or a line inside a Fire Fan, only "jumps the cursor to that shot's time" (§2, rung 1 step-down) — it re-enters the concrete view, it does not open a page that says who fired, from where, LOS verdict, for *that specific hit*. An officer investigating a reported near-miss, a suspicious shot that didn't connect, or a hit that mattered for a promotion review but wasn't a kill, has nowhere to dig to. This is a direct gap against the brief's own worked example, not a nice-to-have.

## Walking Q8: "Did anyone behave suspiciously?"

The suspicion-handling is genuinely good: sort the Codex by suspicion, `n=1` renders grey "insufficient data" rather than a scary 88%, `n=9` renders amber and invites a look at the individual LOS breakdowns, and the payoff is "raw evidence rows with timestamps, no verdict" — that's honest uncertainty done right, and it should survive review.

Two problems inside the same walkthrough, though:

1. **Invented evidence.** The walkthrough cites "97% mouselook time and sub-150ms first-shot latency across three separate re-engagements" as what the officer reads off the Loadout Sheet. Neither "first-shot latency" nor "reaction time relative to target visibility" appears anywhere in §6's analysis model, and the Loadout Sheet's own definition in §3 lists only "mouselook %, movement, seat time" for behaviour. A quantity this specific — cross-referencing mouselook-enter events, target-visibility, and shot timing — needs its own derivation (what track samples, what window, what counts as "re-engagement") or it's decoration dressed as evidence, which is exactly the sin the design otherwise avoids.
2. **The confidence-score worked example doesn't check its own arithmetic.** §6 defines classification confidence as attach-point (+30) + source==rezzer (+30) + bridge confirm (+25) + behavioural corroboration (+15), summed. The SA-90 Mk2 dossier mock-up (§4.3) shows attach-point checked and source==rezzer checked, bridge confirm *unchecked* (2 of 3 instances timed out), and no behavioural-corroboration line at all — by the design's own formula that's 60%, not the displayed "conf: 92%". This is the one screen the entry uses to prove "never a black-box number, every contributing check is listed" (§6), and in its own worked example the listed checks don't add up to the shown number. That undermines exactly the trust claim the design is built to earn — an officer who does the addition once will stop believing the percentage.

## Freeform combat, allegiance drift, mid-fight arrivals

This is handled better than the ladder issue. The damage-graph fallback for intra-group skirmishes, the explicit suppression of the equipment-signature hint when group tags already agree (correctly reasoned: same-faction gear makes the signal noise, not signal), and the FFA "no coherent teams — treat as deathmatch" honest failure state are all good, specific, non-generic answers to the brief's hardest scenarios. Keep all three.

One real gap: **team assignment is modeled as a single number per avatar for the whole session** ("Doe(53%->Ashguard)"), even though the brief states flatly "Team membership can change during a session." The brief's own ladder examples list "team assignment across confidence thresholds" as a suggested rung-2 instance; the entry implements rung 2 twice (LOS sweep, adjustment-ratio sweep) but never for team assignment over time. A captured vehicle, a defector, or a player who joins the neutral-sim free-for-all after fighting for one side earlier has no way to be represented — the Armory Board and avatar pages assume a fixed allegiance. For a tool whose stated purpose includes reviewing violations and promotions across a whole session, "which side was this avatar on at minute 40 versus minute 90" is not a decoration, it's core to answering Q2 and Q9 honestly. As written, the design would silently misattribute damage direction (friendly-fire vs. not) for any avatar whose side actually changed mid-session, because the friendly-fire ledger's classification presumably keys off a single label, not a per-interval one — the entry never says.

## 3D overlay while alt-camming

The gating and clickability model (plain left-click, alt-cam drag untouched, depth-tested + 30%-alpha-under dual draw for occlusion) is sound and matches the decided engineering plan's picking model — no complaint there. But the Fire Fan's "capped at 3 concurrent" items, drawn with "no time fade... a static density picture," gives no guidance on legibility at raid-scale distances: a sim-wide raid with lines fanning from a spawn area to fronts hundreds of meters away, viewed via alt-cam from an arbitrary angle, will foreshorten and overlap in ways a top-down "helicopter" view (the brief's literal metaphor for rung 1) would not. The entry never proposes a top-down or map-projected mode, only whatever angle the officer happens to be alt-cammed to. Given the tool's whole premise is "stand still and alt-cam around" rather than use a 2D map, a session-wide spatial rung that's only ever viewed from ground-level oblique angles is a legibility risk the design doesn't address.

## What must be kept

- The single shared Inspector floater that re-skins per noun, with a breadcrumb + pin/compare mechanism — this is real ladder infrastructure, not a pile of tabs, and it's the one thing that makes "dig and dig, Wikipedia-style" actually work here.
- The uncertainty-rendering discipline: grey "insufficient data" below `n=5`, dashed/hatched marks for low-confidence samples, dual depth-tested/depth-off draw for occlusion, `n=` shown next to every percentage. This is the strongest part of the entry and directly answers the brief's "uncertainty must be honest, not decorative."
- The FFA and intra-group-skirmish handling: damage-graph-only fallback, explicit suppression of the equipment-signature hint when it would be noise, and the "no coherent teams" honest failure state instead of forcing two boxes.
- The Death Dossier's LOS candidate sweep as a genuine, literal implementation of the brief's rung-2 "vary the parameter" idea — this is the one place the design matches Bret Victor's actual technique rather than just gesturing at it.

## What must change before this is a comprehension tool rather than an equipment browser

- Give the officer a weapon-agnostic, session-wide "where and when did fighting happen" view as the true rung-0/rung-1 entry point (a raw hit/death density field, not a leaderboard-selected Fire Fan), or the design will systematically mislead about geography whenever the highest-damage weapon isn't where the interesting fighting was.
- Add a bare-DAMAGE-event dossier (or at minimum, extend Death Dossier's machinery to any hit, lethal or not) so the brief's explicit "clicking on a death or damage event" requirement is actually met, not just half-met via aggregated fire fans and death ladders.
- Fix or cut the classification-confidence worked example so the displayed percentage matches the listed checklist, and either define "first-shot latency"/reaction-time as a real derived quantity in §6 or remove it from the Q8 walkthrough.
- Model team assignment as an interval-based signal (allegiance over time, per the brief), not a single per-session number, or explicitly scope the limitation and its consequences for friendly-fire attribution.
