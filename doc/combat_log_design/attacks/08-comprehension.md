# Attack on Entry 08 — Allegiance — COMPREHENSION lens

## Verdict up front

The field model (`Aff − H`, signed correlation clustering, warm-started, decayed) is the strongest single idea in the competition so far: it gives every other view — Matrix, Ribbon, halos, Kit fairness numbers — one shared source of truth instead of five independent guesses, and the three-state uncertainty vocabulary (solid / grey-hatch / two-tone) is disciplined about not inventing a fourth encoding anywhere. That is real ladder discipline, not decoration. But walking the officer's own questions through the design surfaces three genuine holes: the ladder has an unplaced fifth rung (Matrix) that the walkthroughs depend on constantly, the design's flagship intra-group scenario secretly requires the officer to already know the answer before the tool will show it to them, and the in-world overlay has no answer for the one thing the brief says the tool is *for* — standing still and alt-camming to see the whole raid at once, at a distance where a 0.6 m halo is a sub-pixel dot.

## 1. Is this a ladder, or five views wearing one paint job?

Section 2 names four rungs: Moment, Engagement (burst), Session (Ribbon/Front), Inference (Sweep). Section 4 then delivers seven tabs: Ribbon, Front, Matrix, Sweep, Roster, Events, Kit, Ledger. Matrix is not placed on the ladder at all — it is introduced in the information architecture as if it were self-evidently part of the Session floater, and it never appears in section 2's rung list. But the Matrix is not a minor tab: the intra-group-skirmish walkthrough and the Q2 story both lean on it as the primary evidence for team detection ("the block structure *is* the team detection, shown raw"). A reader trying to place Matrix on the ladder has to guess. Is it rung 1.5 (an alternate rendering of the Session, at a fixed window)? Is it rung 3 (since it's parametrized by tau and half-life like the Sweep)? The design never says, and the two candidate answers aren't equivalent — one implies "click a cell to descend to a burst" (which the design does support, "click cell = assert"), the other implies "sweep this like the Sweep tab" (which it explicitly does not — Matrix has no ablation controls). This is exactly the "pile of views" failure the lens is checking for: a genuinely useful screen that was designed as a screen, then bolted onto the ladder narrative after the fact rather than being derived from it.

A second, quieter version of the same problem: does adjusting tau/half-life on the Ribbon change what the Matrix shows for the same time window? The Ribbon's parameter strip and the Matrix's header both show a half-life value, and section 4.4 says adopting a Sweep parameter set applies "to every other view" — which implies yes, there is one global parameter state. But this is inferred, not stated. If it's true, say so explicitly and show the shared control (both headers should visibly be the same slider, not two sliders that happen to agree). If it's false — if Matrix computes its own window/half-life independently, as its own header block ("window [14:20-14:40] half-life 90s") suggests it might — then an officer could genuinely see two different team pictures in two tabs at the "same" moment with no way to tell they're out of sync. Either way this needs one sentence the design doesn't currently have.

**What to keep:** the field as the single computational backbone is correct and should not be diluted. The fix here is not to add complexity — it's to either fold Matrix explicitly into the rung-2 description as "the same session, cross-tabulated instead of braided" with an explicit shared-parameter statement, or to admit it's a rung-3-adjacent diagnostic and move it next to the Sweep in the prose.

## 2. Digging without losing the thread — does it actually hold?

The Inspector's breadcrumb + `[<]`/`[>]` history + pin strip is a good, correctly-scoped answer to "does digging into a *noun* lose the thread" — Person → Faction → Burst → Death chains are recoverable and backlinked, which is the Wikipedia promise fulfilled.

But there is a second kind of thread that the design never protects: the *viewport* thread on the Session floater itself. "Selection is global: whatever page is front sets the overlay highlight," and clicking things (a death tick, a Matrix cell, a burst) moves the global cursor. Walk this concretely: an officer is looking at the Ribbon zoomed to the 14:20–14:40 contested window, opens a death, drills from Death → killer's Person page → killer's Kit → the weapon's Equipment page → back to a different burst that weapon was involved in at 14:52, and now wants to return to "where I was" on the Ribbon. The Inspector's breadcrumb tracks the *noun* chain they walked, correctly. Nothing tracks the *Ribbon's own state* (cursor, window width, tau, half-life, Threads-vs-Front mode) from before the dig started. The cursor has since been dragged to 14:52 by the last "Show in world" click; the original 14:20–14:40 framing is gone unless the officer remembers to manually re-set it. For a 1-2 hour session this is exactly the kind of "helicopter, then back to the street, then where was I" loss the ladder is supposed to prevent, and the design has solved it for one axis of navigation (nouns) and not the other (viewport/parameters).

This is fixable cheaply — a viewport history stack alongside the noun breadcrumb, or simply "the Session floater's cursor/window is itself a bookmarkable noun with its own back-stack" — but as written it is not there, and the walkthroughs (which always narrate a clean single dig-and-return) never expose the gap because none of them do a *multi-hop* dig of the kind an actual violation investigation requires.

## 3. Walking Q1, Q3, Q8 myself

**Q1 (how did the raid go, where, when, between whom).** The Ribbon-then-Front walkthrough answers "when" and "between whom" convincingly and fast — the push/collapse-at-zero-crossing read is a genuinely good rung-1→rung-2 payoff. It does **not** answer "where." Front mode's y-axis is deliberately unbent away from geography ("signed distance to the contested surface"), which is the right move for seeing pushes and collapses, but it means the walkthrough as written never tells the officer *where on the sim* the fight happened, despite Q1 explicitly asking. The design has the answer available — Place pages, the rung-2 ground overlay's tinted terrain — but the walkthrough doesn't visit either, so as delivered, Q1 is only two-thirds answered. This should be one more sentence and one more click ("Place page for the contested band shows it's the bridge, named from the kill-density cluster") — cheap to fix, but currently missing, and it's the specific question the brief asks first.

**Q3 (why did X die, who/what/where/could-they-see-them).** This one holds up well. Death tick → Death page → killing blow, attribution window grouped by owner/rezzer, weapon card, six-candidate LOS sweep with an honest "CLEAR at the earliest candidate; travel-time false positives are possible" verdict rather than a flat accusation. This is the design working as advertised: concrete instance, full evidence, honest hedge, links both up (burst) and sideways (killer). No notes.

**Q8 (suspicious behaviour, evidence not verdict).** The narrated version is good — percentile, peer distribution, per-engagement LOS sweep collapsing two of three flagged cases, the Matrix "Spared" observation framed as a conversation not a charge. But check it against section 4's own screen inventory: the walkthrough opens with "Roster sorted by the behaviour flag column," and Roster's own spec, two paragraphs earlier, lists its columns as "faction, confidence badge, kills/deaths, FF count" — there is no behaviour-flag column in the spec the walkthrough is standing on. This is a small thing individually, but it's exactly the kind of gap the comprehension lens exists to catch: the entry point for the one officer question about suspicious behaviour was never actually specified, only assumed to exist when the walkthrough needed it. Given Q8 is one of the three mandatory walkthroughs, its launch screen deserved at least a one-line wireframe note, not a silent column that shows up only in prose.

## 4. Does it survive freeform combat?

This is the section that mostly works, and mostly by design rather than luck: the structure index / DEATHMATCH fallback explicitly refuses to invent teams when the fight has no team-shape, the FFA walkthrough narrates a band re-forming and dissolving without incident, and the affinity model's shared-target term is explicitly justified as "the strongest ally signal in FFA and needs no group tag." The mid-fight-arrival case is handled structurally (new arrivals sit in an "unaligned" band until evidence accumulates) but is never walked through as its own scenario despite being named explicitly in the brief's "how combat actually happens" list — a one-paragraph mini-walkthrough here (officer watches someone open fire moments after arriving, tool keeps them grey-hatched for two bursts, then assigns) would have been cheap insurance and is conspicuously absent given every other freeform mode gets a dedicated walkthrough.

The intra-group skirmish is where the design overclaims. Read the walkthrough's own sentence order: "Open the Sweep, ablation row 'drop group tags': the structure survives at 3 sides... Adopt that parameter set; the Ribbon now forks at 14:24." That says, in the design's own words, that the *default* Ribbon (with the group-tag prior active) does not fork — the officer only sees three sides after they've already gone to the Sweep tab and specifically ablated group tags. But nothing in the default view — no elevated intra-band `!`-density indicator, no "internal hostility unusually high" flag on the band header, no automatic ablation suggestion — tells the officer to go do that. The walkthrough works only because the reader (and the officer, apparently) already knows out-of-band that this is "two Ashguard teams" and goes looking for the trick. This is precisely the scenario the brief calls out as the hard case ("active group tag is then useless for team detection; only who-shoots-whom... reveals sides") and the one place a design most needs to show its detection mechanism triggering *itself*, without the officer supplying the hypothesis. As written, an officer who doesn't already suspect an intra-group split has no path in this UI to discover one — they'd see one confident RED blob, a scatter of `!` cells in the Matrix if they happened to open it and look closely, and nothing pointing them at the Sweep. That is a real ladder failure: rung 2 should surface the anomaly that motivates climbing to rung 3, and here it doesn't.

## 5. Uncertainty: honest or decorative?

Mostly honest. The margin/evidence split (`M` for conflicted vs `E` for ignorant, kept as two separate glyphs rather than collapsed into one number) is the right call and is followed through consistently into the halo, the Matrix legend, and the Person page. The Sweep's ablation rows are the standout honesty feature — "drop splash dmg → 2 sides (the GREEN split was partly explosive FF)" is the kind of sentence that actually changes an officer's decision, not just their confidence in a decision already made.

One place it slips toward decorative: the structure band's inline readout, `S=0.71 (n .42)`. Nowhere in the document is "n" defined at the point it's used — section 6 defines `S_null` as the mean over 20 random partitions, but the wireframe abbreviates it to a bare "n" with no legend key next to it, in a strip that is supposed to be the tool's honesty gauge. An abbreviation an officer has to reverse-engineer from a different section of a design doc is not meaningfully more honest than a bare percentage; it just makes the dishonesty a documentation bug instead of a UI one. Cheap fix, but worth calling out because this exact strip is billed as the load-bearing "the tool stops naming teams rather than inventing them" moment.

## 6. Would the in-world overlay actually be usable while alt-camming?

This is the sharpest gap in the entry, because the brief is unambiguous that alt-camming while stationary is the *primary* use mode, and the ladder's own framing borrows Bret Victor's helicopter-over-the-city image directly for rung 1. The design's overlay answer to "the whole raid, from above" is the rung-2 ground overlay (tinted terrain quads) plus halos plus trails. The terrain tinting will read fine at a distance — good, it was clearly designed with zoom-out in mind. But halos are specified as a fixed **0.6 m** world-space ring radius, yaw arrows and SansSerifSmall name labels at presumably similar fixed world scale, and trails "decimate to one point per pixel column" only in the 2D Ribbon view, not the 3D overlay. None of this has any stated behavior as the officer alt-cams out to see "the whole raid" — the literal helicopter shot the ladder metaphor promises. At helicopter distance over a 256 m sim, a 0.6 m ring and a small drop-shadowed name label are far under a pixel and will not read; at that same distance the LOS sweep's individual rays and the per-avatar damage lines will visually collapse into noise long before the terrain tinting does. The design has clearly thought about decluttering the 2D Ribbon (explicit decimation, explicit "unresolved for 60-avatar fights" caveat in Risks) but has not extended that same thinking to the one view the brief says is primary. This needs an explicit LOD statement: screen-space-clamped minimum halo/label size, trail width and ray count that thin with distance, and probably an explicit rung boundary in the overlay itself (e.g., past some camera distance, halos disappear and only the rung-2 terrain tint remains) rather than leaving three visual layers all fighting for the same few pixels.

A second, smaller alt-cam concern: shift-click-to-assert on two halos assumes the officer can cleanly pick two individual halos apart on screen. At a staging area with 30+ agents clustered before a push — exactly the moment an officer would want to sanity-check allegiance calls — halos will overlap in screen space. The design doesn't address occlusion/declutter for click targets the way it does for the Ribbon's thread decimation.

## What must be kept

- The single `Aff − H` field as the one computation every view reads from — this is what stops the tool from being five disagreeing opinions about who's on what team, and it should not be watered down to fix the above.
- The three-state uncertainty vocabulary (solid / grey-hatch / two-tone), applied identically in 2D and 3D — genuinely disciplined, keep it exactly as specified and resist adding a fourth state anywhere.
- Bursts, not raw events, as the evidence unit, specifically to stop one stray splash tick from reading as an execution.
- The DEATHMATCH / structure-index fallback that refuses to invent teams — this is the single best answer to the FFA requirement in the whole entry.
- The Ledger as append-only, attributed, undoable, and required to state what it overruled — "the officer is never allowed to silently disagree with the evidence" is the right rule and is actually enforced by the mechanism described, not just asserted.
- The Q3 walkthrough's LOS phrasing ("CLEAR at the earliest candidate; travel-time false positives are possible") as the house style for every probabilistic claim the tool makes — this is what honest uncertainty display looks like when it's done right, and every other verdict in the tool (through-wall claims, suspicious-behaviour flags) should be held to this exact sentence shape.
