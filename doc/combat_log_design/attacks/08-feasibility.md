# Attack on Entry 08 — Allegiance (ANALYSIS + FEASIBILITY lens)

## Fatal

1. **The cost model is asserted, not derived, and is wrong for two of the five affinity terms.** Section 6 states "Cost: Bursts and affinity accumulate streaming, O(events)." That is true for shared-target, co-ride, heal/repair, and spawn-cohort, which are keyed off DAMAGE/DEATH events. It is false for **proximity-under-fire** ("+0.02/s while within 12 m, at least one engaging a third party, neither engaging the other"), which by construction requires evaluating a pairwise spatial predicate over *track samples*, not events. With up to 100 tracked agents and 2–10 Hz samples over 1–2 h, that is up to ~100×99/2 pairs × millions of timesteps if computed naively — nowhere near O(events), and nowhere near the brief's "computed incrementally or on demand" budget on a single main thread. The entry never specifies the actual algorithm (e.g. spatial hashing per burst window, or restricting the pairwise scan to the handful of avatars near an active burst) that would make this tractable. As written it is a footgun the entry itself doesn't notice, sitting directly underneath the Ribbon and Matrix — the two flagship views.

2. **Exposure/Spared is presented as a computable "observation" but has no bounded computation path.** "`Exposure(a,b)` = seconds both alive, within a's 95th-percentile hit distance, with a CLEAR LOS" and the walkthrough example ("Rue S. had 96 s of clear exposure to Pim L. and never fired") implies this is *discovered*, i.e. swept across many candidate pairs, not computed for one pair the officer already picked. LOS verdicts require `lineSegmentIntersectWorldGeometry` raycasts (already the most expensive primitive in the plan, reserved by the fixed design for a handful of candidate positions around a single death). Continuously raycasting between every live pair, every sample, for 1–2 h, to *find* Spared pairs, is not remotely 60fps-on-main-thread material, and the entry gives no on-demand restriction (e.g. "only computed when the officer selects a specific enemy pair to compare"). This is a derived quantity with no computation path at the scale the entry implies it operates at.

3. **The "Front" contested-surface is geometrically underspecified and silently assumes a flat, single-level battlefield.** The Front view treats "signed distance to the contested line" as a simple 1D coordinate transform, but the entry's own analysis model builds the surface from a 4 m Gaussian-weighted occupancy grid, i.e. a 2D scalar field that can have multiple zero-crossings, islands, or no crossing at all. Extracting one scalar "signed distance to front" per avatar from that field requires a contour-extraction step (e.g. marching squares) and a nearest-point-on-possibly-disconnected-curve projection that the entry never names, let alone budgets. Worse: the occupancy grid is described purely over "the terrain" (x/y), with no z-handling — but SL raids very commonly happen inside multi-floor bases, over walls, and across balconies, where "distance to front" on a flat ground plane is meaningless (two avatars 2 m apart on stacked floors would read as co-located). This is exactly the kind of `doc/viewer/ui_system.md`-adjacent 3D-in-2D transform that needs a stated algorithm and a per-floor or per-volume caveat; the entry has neither, and the Front view is one of only two rung-2 world abstractions offered (the other is the Ribbon, which is thread-based and doesn't have this problem).

## Serious

4. **Structure index and correlation-clustering are computed on "avatars present in the window," but 'present' is undefined and the events used are asymmetric in trustworthiness.** DAMAGE events can be `trusted`/`untrusted` (per the brief, script-generated custom events are tagged untrusted) and the entry's affinity model never distinguishes trust level — an untrusted, spoofable custom event could feed the correlation-clustering solve with the same weight as a system DAMAGE event, silently corrupting the ribbon's factions and confidence numbers officers may use for promotion/violation decisions. This should be a first-class input to `h_burst`/affinity, not silently ignored.

5. **Spawn-cohort detection has no stated derivation from the actual data.** "Spawn cohort (respawn positions clustered at 15 m within 60 s): `+1.0` once" assumes the tool can detect *respawn*. Nothing in the brief's data section provides a respawn/teleport signal — the closest fact is a DEATH event, after which the target's track presumably resumes somewhere. The entry needs to say explicitly "respawn = first track sample of `target` after its DEATH, at a position discontinuous from `target_pos`" (or similar) and account for the case where an avatar dies and is *not* actually pulled to a spawn point (many SL combat systems don't teleport on death) — in which case there is no "spawn cohort" signal at all for that raid, and every affinity computation that leans on it silently degrades. This should be flagged in the entry as conditional on the raid's respawn mechanic, which the tool cannot observe directly.

6. **The group-tag prior is correctly flagged as ablatable, but its magnitude (`+0.5`) is presented next to non-prior evidence terms without units, and the intra-group walkthrough leans on ablation working perfectly.** In the intra-group skirmish walkthrough, dropping the group-tag prior "at 3 sides — the split is real, not a tag artefact" is asserted as if ablation always cleanly isolates the confound. In practice a `+0.5` prior baked additively into the same signed sum used for clustering can dominate weak affinity signals (early in a raid, before many bursts have accumulated, `E(x)` is small and the prior is *most* of the signal) — so "drop it and see the same 3 sides" is a plausible outcome, not a guaranteed one, and the entry states it as though the machine will always cleanly recover ground truth once the confound is named. This is presenting a hoped-for property of the scoring function as an established fact of the design.

7. **The 20-node-per-band, 100×100-matrix scale claims are asserted, not checked against the brief's stated maximum.** The brief allows up to ~100 agents on the bridge; the Matrix screen and its "worst case ~10k rects" claim are fine at that scale, but the Ribbon's "Ribbon legibility above ~30 threads per band" risk (correctly listed in §8) means the flagship rung-2 view visibly degrades well before the ceiling the rest of the system is built for (100k events, 100 agents). The entry lists this as a risk but doesn't reconcile it with the fact that a 100-agent neutral-sim FFA (explicitly one of the brief's required scenarios) is exactly the case where the Ribbon is least legible — the FFA walkthrough dodges this by falling back to per-person threads with no bands, but per-person threads for 100 concurrent avatars in a 20 s trail window is itself a >30-thread legibility problem the entry doesn't resolve for the *events lane* or thread lane, only for bands.

## Minor

8. **The Burst-page "aftermath" test (`ΔJ`, shared-target overlap before/after) reuses the same shared-target affinity term that also *feeds* the classification it's judging** — a burst between two allies-by-focus-fire changes the very affinity number used later to decide if it was "friendly fire" vs "defection." The entry names this circularity for the affinity/clustering loop in §8 but doesn't extend the same caveat to the intra-side classifier, which has an identical feedback structure.

9. **`h_burst`'s `w_type` table hardcodes weights per damage type (0.35 for splash, 0.6 for suffocation, 1.0 direct) with no stated calibration procedure beyond "must be settings"** — acceptable as a v1 placeholder (already flagged in §8) but the entry should note that officers reviewing a promotion or violation off a `conf .86` badge are trusting a chain of un-calibrated constants (`w_type`, `T½`, `E0`, the 0.08 deathmatch margin) that compound multiplicatively; a confidence number three constants deep should probably not be shown as a bare decimal even inside the honest-uncertainty vocabulary (it uses "solid" correctly, but a `.86` printed next to "solid" invites the very false precision the entry otherwise guards against).

10. **The Person-page "reaction 210ms (p54 of peers)" and "pre-turn while BLOCKED... p61" percentiles need a peer population with comparable kit and engagement volume**, per §6 — but peer-group construction (who counts as comparable) is not defined anywhere in the entry; this is a derived quantity (a percentile) whose reference distribution has no stated construction rule, which matters a lot for Q8 (suspicious-behaviour) where the percentile is the entire point.

## Verdict on the "no computation path" / "needs a web view" checks

- **Web view needed: none.** Every screen (Ribbon, Front, Matrix, Sweep, Person/Burst/Ledger inspector pages) is drawable with XUI immediate-mode scroll lists/tabs/text plus one custom-drawn `LLView` per chart, consistent with `doc/viewer/ui_system.md` and the fixed plan's overlay conventions. This is a genuine strength of the entry — it never reaches for anything the medium can't do.
- **In-world overlay: fully drawable** with lines/rings/text/screen-rect picking as specified in the fixed plan (halos, yaw arrows, damage lines, LOS ray fans, ground quads). The ground-occupancy quad layer is the one piece that inherits the Front view's flat-terrain/no-z problem (Fatal #3) but the *drawing* mechanics themselves (translucent quads, alpha by margin, hatch texture) are all standard immediate-mode primitives already used elsewhere in the codebase.
- **Quantities with no computation path at the scale implied:** proximity-under-fire affinity (Fatal #1), Exposure/Spared (Fatal #2), Front contested-surface extraction (Fatal #3). All three need either an explicit restriction to on-demand/selected-pair computation, a much cheaper approximation, or removal from v1.
- **Quantities presented as fact but actually inference:** the ribbon's "fork" ticks (binary topology events out of a continuous, tie-prone heuristic optimizer — Minor/Serious #6 and §8's own admission), the group-tag ablation outcome in the intra-group walkthrough (Serious #6), and the untrusted-event handling gap (Serious #4) which the entry doesn't surface as a confidence factor at all despite the brief explicitly calling out spoofable custom events.

## What must be kept

- **The three-state uncertainty vocabulary (solid / grey-hatch / two-tone stripe), applied identically everywhere.** This is the single best idea in the entry: it is cheap to draw, cheap to compute (it's just `(M, E)` from the confidence formula), and it is the direct, correct answer to the brief's "present allegiance as inference with confidence, never fact" requirement.
- **The Matrix tab.** A grid of coloured rects with one-character glyphs is trivially XUI-feasible at the brief's scale, is directly clickable for assertions, and — unlike the Ribbon — degrades gracefully (sort/filter, not legibility collapse) as agent count grows toward 100.
- **Bursts as the atomic evidence unit** (collapsing DAMAGE events with the same owner/target within a 3 s gap) instead of raw events. This is well-grounded in the actual data shape and is the correct fix for "a single stray splash tick must never carry the weight of an execution."
- **The Ledger as append-only, undoable, attributed assertions that report what they overrule.** This directly answers the brief's requirement to "let the officer override" without ever letting an override silently rewrite history — good for the promotion/violation review workflows.
- **The DEATHMATCH fallback** (structure index below the null baseline for 60 s → stop naming factions, grey out bands, keep per-person stats/Kit/Place pages). This is the correct, honest response to the brief's neutral-sim/FFA scenario and should survive even if the affinity math underneath is reworked.
- **The LOS sweep-not-verdict presentation** (candidate shooter positions across the whole travel window, verdict stated with the earliest-clear caveat) is a faithful, buildable implementation of the brief's explicit false-positive warning and matches the already-decided LOS module.
