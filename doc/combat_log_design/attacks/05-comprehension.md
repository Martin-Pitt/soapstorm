# Comprehension review — Entry 05: Time-First: the Combat Timeline

## Verdict up front

The ladder mechanics are the strongest part of this entry — the cursor-as-shared-coordinate, the always-visible overview strip, and the wikipedia-style breadcrumb/pin/backlink graph are a genuine, well-thought-through answer to "dig and dig without losing the thread." But the thesis — time is the spine, space is one drill-down away — does not survive the freeform scenarios the brief explicitly requires the design to survive. When a raid has one clean push/front/collapse shape, this design is excellent. When it doesn't (freeform arrivals, neutral-sim FFA, three simultaneous skirmishes in different parts of the sim), the entry-point view degrades from "abstract but honest" to "wrong shape painted over a session it doesn't fit," and the escape hatch to space (front-distance transform, Place pages) is explicitly capped at two clusters. That is a comprehension failure at exactly the question (Q1: "where did the fighting happen") the brief puts first.

## Walking the officer questions

### Q1 — How did the raid go overall? Where, when, between whom?

For a classic raid (the entry's own worked example) this works well: Phase ribbon gives five bands with zero clicks, hovering gives duration/casualties/confidence, clicking the Front band narrows and shows two converging team blocks plus a rise-plateau-drop front-distance curve. That's a real ladder rung-3→1 answer, and it's genuinely satisfying.

It breaks under freeform combat, which the brief lists as the norm, not the edge case:

- **"Where" has no rung-1 answer.** The whole-session view is a time-only density sparkline and a phase ribbon. Both are 1D-in-time. The entry's own text places the *only* spatial abstraction — the front-distance transform — behind two gates: it needs a Phase selected, and §6 admits `F(t)` degrades to "median nearest-opposing-avatar distance" once there isn't a clean two-cluster front, and §8 admits outright that "a per-cluster-pair small-multiples version is the likely fix" for 3+ simultaneous fronts but is *not designed here*. So for a neutral-sim FFA, or a raid with fighting breaking out in two unrelated corners of the sim at once, an officer asking "where did the fighting happen" gets: a density spike (tells them *when*, not *where*) and a single blurred scalar that averages together two unrelated fights into a meaningless number. There is no small-multiples-by-location view, no minimap heat overlay, nothing that answers "where" without first successfully clustering teams and fronts — which is precisely the case the brief says may not happen.
- **Phase segmentation itself assumes the classic shape.** The hysteresis state machine (staging → push → front → collapse → respawn) is tuned around one coherent front. For simultaneous unrelated skirmishes, the state machine will either merge them into one misleading "Front" band (average intensity, average dispersion, confidence rendered as merely "weak" rather than "wrong shape entirely") or thrash between states. §8 acknowledges "may not fit five clean bands" but the mitigation offered (expose threshold sliders, show confidence) does not fix the underlying problem: the confidence number describes how well the *chosen model* fits, not whether the *model itself* (single global front) is the right model for this session. A low-confidence Front band still visually reads as "there was a front," which is a stronger claim than the data supports.
- Net effect: for the sessions the brief is most insistent the tool must handle (freeform, neutral-sim, multi-front), Q1's "where, between whom" is not answerable from the top of the ladder at all — the officer must fall back to manually scrubbing the overlay in trail mode across the whole session and eyeballing it, which is exactly the "stuck in the concrete" failure Bret Victor's ladder is supposed to prevent.

### Q3 — Why did X die at 14:32?

This walkthrough works and is the best-supported question in the design. Scrub → click `╳` → Inspector Death page → related damage in the preceding window → LOS candidates shown all at once with a denominator (4/6 CLEAR) → `[Show in world]` drops the officer at rung 0 with the exact geometry. The related-events window, the adjustment chain, and the "killing blow" callout are concrete and correctly reflect the available data (no health values, so cumulative damage + killing blow is the right substitute).

One real gap: nothing in the walkthrough or the Avatar/Inspector pages describes what happens if Nexii's row isn't currently visible (scrolled off, or the swimlanes have auto-collapsed to team-aggregate rows because the officer is zoomed out past the density threshold, per §4.1). "Scrub to 14:32 and click the ╳" presumes the row is on-screen and expanded. There's no stated search-by-name or "jump to avatar" action that also re-expands/scrolls the swimlane to the right row — the Avatars tab exists and can open the Inspector, but does it also relocate the timeline's scroll position? Not specified. Minor, but it's the kind of gap that turns "three clicks" into "did I count filtering time" in a 40-avatar session.

### Q8 — Did anyone behave suspiciously?

This is where the design's information architecture shows a real category mismatch. The brief's Q8 is a question about a *person* ("did anyone behave suspiciously... implausible reaction, speed, awareness through walls"). The walkthrough answers it by starting from an **Equipment** page ("Ashguard-9mm" through-wall suspicion 3/40), then treats reaction time as "a second signal" surfaced *from the equipment page*.

That is backwards for the officer's actual question, and it has a real failure mode: through-wall suspicion and reaction-time anomalies are properties of a *shooter's behavior*, not of a weapon. A weapon used by five different avatars, four of them behaving normally and one behaving very suspiciously, will show a diluted aggregate (e.g. "3/40 BLOCKED across all users") that can hide the one bad actor entirely, or conversely can implicate four innocent users by association with a "suspicious" weapon. Checking the Avatar page (§3: "session dossier: kills/deaths, weapons used, mouselook %, time seated, team history") — there is no per-avatar suspicion rollup at all: no per-avatar BLOCKED-kill count, no per-avatar reaction-time distribution versus cohort baseline, no "awareness through walls" signal attached to the avatar noun. An officer investigating a *specific person* (the actual promotion/violation workflow the brief names) has no page to go to; they'd have to enumerate every weapon that avatar used and open each Equipment page hoping the aggregate isn't diluted. That inverts the "dig from a person" workflow the rest of the design is built around, and it's the exact question (Q8) the brief asks to be walked through.

## Ladder of abstraction — does it hold together, or is it a pile of views?

Mostly holds together. Strengths worth keeping:
- A single shared cursor driving timeline, swimlanes, Inspector, and overlay simultaneously is the correct implementation of "pointing at a location on an abstraction steps back down to the concrete instance" — this is not decorative, it's mechanically enforced.
- The overview strip that never rescrolls regardless of zoom is a genuinely good anti-tunnel-vision device — it directly implements Victor's "never get stuck in the zoomed weeds."
- LOS candidates shown all-at-once rather than stepped through (rung 2) is a correct, literal reading of "abstract over a parameter" and is one of the few places uncertainty is shown with real texture (a ratio with denominator, not a verdict).

Weaknesses:
- Rung 2 in the ladder table (§2) advertises three sliders: phase-confidence, LOS-candidate strip, and **team-confidence slider**. Only the LOS strip and phase hover-tooltip actually appear in the Screens section (§4). There is no team-confidence slider anywhere in the `Combat Timeline` wireframe or the Teams tab description — the Teams tab shows "roster + confidence + reassignment log," which is a report, not an interactive rung-2 control for "redraw swimlane grouping at looser/tighter clustering" as promised. This is a rung that's asserted in the table but never actually built in the screens — a real instance of "pile of views" leaking through in exactly the place the entry claims to avoid it.
- The overlay's rung-1 trail window is stated to be *literally the same value* as the timeline zoom ("Widening the timeline zoom widens the trail window identically — one slider drives both"). The zoom range is explicitly "logarithmic: whole-session at one end, 0.5 s at the other" (§4.1), and the engineering plan bounds trail rendering with a separate, capped setting (`SSCombatLogTrailSeconds`, default 20 s) for exactly the performance and legibility reasons the brief calls out (2 h session, main-thread immediate-mode, must stay incrementally computed). Coupling trail length 1:1 to timeline zoom means an officer who zooms out toward "whole session" while alt-camming — a completely natural thing to do when trying to get an overview, which this design otherwise encourages — would ask the 3D overlay to draw two hours of trail per avatar simultaneously. That is not a rung-1 "abstract over time" view, it's an unreadable tangle, and it silently overrides the engineering plan's own trail cap. This needs an explicit decoupling (e.g. trail window capped independently, with a "trail follows zoom, up to N seconds" note) or it will fail exactly the "usable while alt-camming" bar the brief sets.

## Freeform survival

- **Intra-group skirmish**: handled well. Clustering by who-shot-whom rather than by active-group tag, renaming clusters, and reassignment-aware friendly fire ("owner's team-at-t equals target's team-at-t") together correctly separate "different side of an intra-group fight" from "actual friendly fire." This is the best-supported freeform case in the entry.
- **Neutral-sim FFA**: also handled honestly at the *team* layer — falling back to an ungrouped "Unaffiliated / low-confidence" swimlane block instead of forcing fake bands is the right instinct, and phase detection correctly continues to work off intensity/dispersion signals that don't need team identity. But as covered under Q1, the *spatial* layer does not degrade gracefully in the same way — front-distance explicitly assumes ≤2 clusters, and there is no fallback spatial small-multiples view for "many small unrelated skirmishes at once," which is the FFA case's most likely actual shape.
- **Mid-fight arrivals**: not addressed anywhere in the entry. Swimlane rows are implicitly assumed to span the whole session; nothing describes how a row for an avatar who wasn't present at t=0 initializes, whether team assignment for a late arrival is even attempted before they've exchanged any damage, or whether the Overview sparkline/Phase ribbon account for headcount changing mid-session (a density spike from 5 new arrivals joining an existing fight vs. an actual escalation look identical in `I(t)` as defined in §6). Minor on its own, but combined with the "single front" assumption above, this is a second way multi-front/mixed-timing sessions can produce a misleading top-level picture without any confidence signal calling it out.

## Uncertainty: honest or decorative?

Mostly honest, and this is a real strength to keep:
- Confidence numbers are attached to phases, teams, and reassignments, and low confidence changes the *rendering* (hatching), not just a tooltip.
- Uncertainty is deliberately kept on its own visual channel (solid/dashed/dotted outline) separate from color, which is reserved for team/damage-type — this is explicitly called out and is the right design discipline for a medium (XUI/overlay) where color is already overloaded.
- Suspicion and adjustment numbers are shown as ratios with denominators (4/6, 3/40), not bare percentages or binary verdicts — this correctly resists the temptation to manufacture false certainty.

Where it slips toward decorative: the phase-confidence number, as discussed above, measures fit-to-a-fixed-model, not model-appropriateness. A three-front session forced into one Front band will show a "confidence" score that reads as "this phase call is a bit shaky" when the honest statement is "this session doesn't have a single front, the question is malformed for this data." That's a subtle but real form of dishonesty-by-omission: the officer sees a number and reasonably reads it as "roughly right, some noise," when the correct read would be "wrong shape."

## What must be kept

- Single shared cursor across timeline, swimlanes, Inspector, and overlay — the mechanical backbone that makes the ladder real rather than aspirational.
- Always-visible whole-session overview strip, independent of current zoom.
- Breadcrumb + history stack + pinning + backlinks as the navigation model for the noun graph — directly answers "dig without losing the thread," and pinning specifically solves the promotion-review workflow (hold a candidate's dossier while digging elsewhere).
- LOS candidates and equipment suspicion shown as full sets with denominators, never collapsed to a single verdict.
- Uncertainty rendered on a channel orthogonal to color (dash/dot/hatch vs. solid).
- Reassignment-aware friendly fire and renameable who-shot-whom clustering for intra-group skirmishes.
- Honest FFA fallback to an ungrouped block at the team layer (needs the equivalent honest fallback at the spatial layer — see findings).

## What must change before this ships

1. Add a real spatial rung-1/3 view that does not depend on successful ≤2-cluster front detection — e.g. a minimap/heatmap small-multiples-by-location panel keyed off death/damage position density (deaths have positions even when damage doesn't), so "where did the fighting happen" has an answer independent of whether phase/team clustering succeeded. Without this, Q1's "where" fails on exactly the sessions the brief prioritizes.
2. Decouple in-world trail length from the timeline zoom slider, or explicitly cap it, so zooming out for an overview while alt-camming can't request an unreadable multi-hour trail tangle.
3. Give the Avatar page a suspicion rollup (per-avatar BLOCKED-kill count and denominator, reaction-time vs. cohort baseline) so Q8 can be answered starting from a person, not only from a weapon's diluted aggregate.
4. Either build the promised team-confidence slider (rung 2) into the Teams tab or remove it from the ladder table — as written it's a claim the screens don't back up.
5. State explicitly how phase confidence should be read when the underlying single-front model is simply the wrong shape for the session, not just noisy within the right shape — otherwise a "weak" phase reads as roughly-plausible when it may be structurally wrong.
