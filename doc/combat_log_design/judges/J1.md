# Judge J1 — Comprehension and Ladder-of-Abstraction fidelity

**Lens.** Do these designs create Victor's "dance between levels"? I weight **ladder** (are there real step-up / step-down transitions between concrete and abstract, and does the design make the *transition* itself legible?) and **dig** (information architecture; wikipedia-style navigation that never loses the thread) most heavily, but I score every criterion. Entry 03 was not in my review set.

Two things I looked for that separate the top of the field from the middle:

1. **Does the step *down* land somewhere named and non-arbitrary?** Most entries specify how to go up. Fewer specify what happens when you come back down, and fewer still notice that coming down from an aggregate you *climbed into* should be different from coming down into one you *linked into cold*.
2. **Does a discovery at one rung update the others, or leave them stale?** A pile of views with a shared cursor is not a ladder. A ladder needs shared state — one parameter set, one threshold, one cursor — so that moving a knob at rung 2 visibly recolours rung 0.

## Per-entry notes

### 01 — Ladder-first (57) — 1st

The only entry where the ladder is not a description of the design but *is* the design. Six rungs R−1..R4, each naming its subject type, its compression operator (which becomes a breadcrumb token) and its grounding gesture. Three things put it first under this lens:

- **Orthogonality.** "The ladder is vertical, the wiki is horizontal" — vertical movement changes altitude and keeps the subject, horizontal changes subject and keeps altitude, and the breadcrumb *draws them differently* (tinted operator chip with a direction glyph vs a plain `→` at the same indent). Nobody else notices that altitude change and subject change are two different moves that must not look alike.
- **Anchor vs witness.** `]` writes the anchor (the instance you climbed from); `[` reads it, so up-then-down is the identity. Arriving cold via a link or a pin, `[` lands on the aggregate's *medoid witness* and the rail says `witness`, not `back`. This is the sharpest answer in the entire field to "every abstraction must offer a step back down."
- **The rail pre-announces the landing.** You read the destination instance before you press the key, so descending is never a leap.

R−1 (the literal received bytes, with trust flag, salvage mask and `fd` dedup partner) is the correct extra rung *below* rung 0 — "any claim can be run to ground in the literal bytes" — and it is honestly costed as a plan delta (a char arena, ~25 MB) with a stated degradation if refused: the rail must say `reconstructed`, not `raw`. That is the discipline of the whole entry in miniature.

The generative IA rule table (any noun N × any rung, one rule) means the wiki doesn't have to be enumerated page by page; it welds the two navigation axes together. Analysis is top-tier: the ordering rule (`t̂ ± u`; no ordering claim between overlapping intervals, so the killing blow reads "ambiguous between X and Y" rather than picking) and the pre-clustering seed test that disables group-tag seeding *before* clustering runs (three named conditions, header names the failed test) are both correct and non-obvious. The Contact/Exposure split — free O(N) contact from tracks, raycast-confirmed exposure only on demand at a hard 600-cast cap, with "continuous session-wide exposure is not offered at any price" — is the most honest cost statement about the raycast budget anywhere.

Deductions. The overlay is excellent conceptually (rung-indexed: pressing `]` changes what the *world* draws) but the Chart renderer parity rule, while well justified by the gating problem, means two renderers with a self-named drift risk. Feasibility surface is large: six stage panels, an article floater, a Chart renderer, LLUrlEntry links (verified real — `LLUrlRegistry::registerUrl` takes any `LLUrlEntryBase`), plus the plan delta. Q9 briefing is a Case + clipboard, thinner than 02's or 08's. And at 6,387 words it is more than twice the brief's cap.

### 08 — Allegiance (57) — 2nd

The best-argued *analysis* document in the set and the best overlay, tied on total, second on lens. Its ladder is one ladder over one object — the signed, time-decaying allegiance field — and it earns that: rung 2 is explicitly "three renderings of one solve" (Ribbon = time, Front = space, Matrix = relation) reading the same snapshot from the same global ParamSet, so "two rung-2 tabs can never disagree about teams at the same moment." That is the shared-state discipline I was looking for, stated as an invariant rather than hoped for.

Rung 3 is the deepest parameter rung in the field: the session re-solved across the parameters of the *inference itself*, with evidence-class ablations and, critically, an **agreement count** per row from 20 jittered re-solves. `11/20 → "inconclusive — not enough evidence to test this"` is the outcome an officer most needs and the one a binary answer hides. The `[sweep this]` affordance on any uncertainty badge — "you climb from the specific reading you distrust, which becomes the sweep's subject" — is the best rung-2 entry mechanic anywhere.

Its single best idea for officer comprehension: **every solve runs twice, with and without the group-tag prior, and disagreement lights a `(!)` flag** next to a STRAINED% strain meter in the default view. An officer who has never heard the phrase "intra-group skirmish" is told, unasked, that RED is internally hostile and that its shape depends on group tags. That converts the brief's hardest scenario from something you must suspect into something the tool volunteers. The cost argument is right too — a second solve on ≤100 nodes is sub-millisecond and off the draw path.

Also: two navigation histories drawn identically and never merged, because "'where I was reading' and 'what I was reading about' fail separately" — independently derived and as sharp as 01's anchor/back-stack split. The degraded-terms strip (`no-respawn`, `prox-capped`) means the tool never silently loses a term. The overlay LOD table where *each rung owns a distance band*, with a HUD caption naming the current regime because "a view that silently drops glyphs is a lying view", is the best overlay specification in the field; the halo drawn as wedges by faction posterior (a 60/40 avatar is literally two-tone) and the pick-stack list for overlapping avatars in a staging heap are both correct.

Deductions. The ladder has only four rungs and no explicit return-path guarantee comparable to anchor/witness. Nearly everything is one inference deep, so if the field model is wrong, all four rungs are wrong together — the entry knows this (the sweep is the guard) but it is a structural concentration of risk. 6,813 words, the longest in the set.

### 02 — Hypertext (55) — 3rd

The purest realisation of the owner's "dig and dig, like wikipedia" line, and it wins the dig axis outright. One page skeleton, one reader, addresses as real clickable `secondlife:///app/sscombat/...` links through a registered `LLUrlEntryBase` (verified feasible), so noun mentions work inside every `LLTextBase` — prose, tooltips, chat, notes.

Its ladder correction is genuinely right and nobody else makes it: **a single rail is a lie, because a raid has three independent dimensions.** WHO / WHEN / WHERE each get their own step-up and step-down, so a Death page carries *two* WHO subjects (each separately step-up-able, so "up to career" never silently picks a party), a Model page gets a real WHO ascent (model → class → arsenal), and Phase and Place can never collide in one button. That is the ladder done per-dimension, as Victor actually does it.

Best single honesty mechanic in the field: **flip points printed in the lede**, not behind a facet. Because the sweep grid is the grid the claim already used, the nearest parameter value at which the verdict changes is free — so the skimming officer reads "BLOCKED for travel < 0.9 s; CLEAR at or beyond it (5 of 6 candidates)" without opening anything. That puts the rung-2 result into the rung-1 skim, which is exactly the dance.

Two more: **typed backlink polarity** (⚑ flag / ✓ cleared / ⇄ override / ✎ note) so a promotion reviewer six months later can tell two exonerations from a flag without opening them; and **named trails** (`ss:trail/promotion-nyx`) as the dossier and briefing artifact — the best Q9 answer in the set. The MARGINAL verdict, produced by jittering the deciding LOS candidate by its own position error along the two axes perpendicular to the ray (4 extra rays), is the only real uncertainty *propagation* anywhere, and FIRM-only through-wall rates with MARGINAL/UNKNOWN counted beside are correspondingly honest. "Two kinds of number, never mixed" — intervals with n for statistics, ordinal bands for inference, "because an officer will otherwise quote 0.81 in a violation report as if it were a probability" — is the right rule for the right reason.

Deductions. Feasibility is the softest of the top four: long `LLTextBase` pages with many URL segments, a reverse index maintained partly at ingest and partly at page build, an LRU of 32 rendered pages with epoch-based invalidation, and three raycast consumers contending on one 32/frame queue. All of it is acknowledged, none of it is measured. The band/restraint machinery adds real complexity for a term the entry itself calls the weakest.

### 11 — Comparative (53) — 4th

"An officer never asks *what happened* — they ask *compared to what*." One grammar (facet × cell view × sweep), and `Query = {filter, rowFacet, colFacet, cellView, sweep, baseline, timeWindow}` where a breadcrumb chip, a pin, a compare slot, a link and a noun page are all literally Query values. That is the most economical IA in the field and it genuinely unifies grid and pages into one navigable object; `Alt+←/→` restores Query *and* cursor.

Its ladder claim — **"uncertainty is not an error bar bolted onto a number, it *is* a sweep"** — is the best one-line statement of Victor's rung 2 that anyone wrote. Three unbent coordinate systems (distance-to-front, distance-to-spawn, time-before-death), each permitted to refuse to exist. "Sweep across cells" is a real R2→R3 operator; Δ-vs-baseline is a real R3→R4 one.

Analysis is superb. The **drift / noise / short trichotomy** from clustering across θ *and* time is the best allegiance-drift handling in the field: modal cluster changes between adjacent windows with high stability on both sides → chevron plus a printed change time; low stability inside a window → hatch, no chevron; small n → its own `short` badge, never conflated with ambiguity. The intra-group walkthrough demonstrates all three at once (two cross-healing medics hatched and unclassified, Tamsin chevroned at 14:41, Juno badged short) and correctly observes that a θ-only ribbon could not tell them apart. **Flip fraction** as the LOS headline with the existential "a clear shot exists" demoted to a chip — "because that test is uninformative when true and decisive when false" — is a genuinely sharp piece of reasoning. Spawn axis gated on a *tested* relocation pattern (φ ≥ 0.5 over ≥8 decided deaths) rather than assumed. Equipment keyed on (creator, root name, attach class, damage signature) because display names are spoofable, with a permanent UNATTRIBUTED sink row. Pick resolution on mouse-**up** with 4 px / 350 ms thresholds and modified presses never consumed is the most careful input spec in the field.

Deductions, and they are what keeps it off the podium: feasibility. It states its own cost honestly — four `LLView` subclasses plus **seven** paint routines, ~2.5k lines of primitive drawing — which is admirable and also disqualifying relative to the top three. It self-notes ~12 characters per cell label at 100 % UI scale, numbers only at 150 %; a 60-cell grid of custom-painted small multiples in XUI at readable density is the riskiest bet anyone makes here.

### 06 — Forensics (46) — 5th

The Death Report is the best single screen in the field, and the entry's central comprehension insight is one nobody else states so directly: **a discovery at one rung must *update* the others rather than leaving them stale**, so the team-confidence threshold is promoted to global state in the main floater's top bar and every rung reads it — Death Wall card fills, the front line, the in-world markers and the Team pages recolour together on one drag. That is the shared-state test, passed explicitly.

**Team Epoch** — one team's membership within one time bucket, so a Team page is a stack of epochs and an avatar can belong to different epochs of different teams — is the right way to put drift into the *IA* rather than into a chart annotation. Friendly-fire flags become bucket-local facts that report disagreement with neighbouring buckets. Two other fixes are excellent: the **occlusion point cloud** (every BLOCKED raycast already returns a hit point; scatter them instead of inventing a wall silhouette the tool has no geometry store for — free, incomplete, honest), and the rule that **BLOCKED draws red only if at least one dense sample was tested, otherwise grey regardless of the raycast result** — input uncertainty overriding output confidence, which is the correct direction and rare.

Ranked raw damage totals tagged dominant/contested/long-shot, with the internal weighting disclosed but never rendered as a percentage, is the right call on attribution.

Deductions. Only four rungs, and the step-up/step-down mechanics are stated rather than engineered — there is no anchor, no witness, no named operator. Pin rails owning their own back/forward stacks is good, but "Recently Visited (last 20)" is a workaround for a lost branch, not a navigation model. The overlay is the thinnest of the upper half (no LOD, no scale rule, little on clickability). And Q9 is explicitly abandoned — "Docket workflow stays intentionally unspecified" — which is an honest but real hole in one of the nine questions.

### 05 — Timeline (44) — 6th

The revision notes are the most candid in the set, and the central fix is correct: the original had no rung-1 "where" that did not depend on team/front clustering converging. The **Contact Map** — grid-binned density of DEATH plus resolvable DAMAGE positions, driven by the same cursor, computed with no team or phase precondition — is the right answer, and making it a *sibling* rung-1 over space rather than a drill-down is the right shape.

**Attacker position tiers** (TRACK / LAST-SEEN / APPROX-VIA-OWNER) are the single most useful analysis contribution in the whole field. It is the only entry that squarely confronts the fact that turrets, deployables, mounts and vehicles shoot, that the fixed per-avatar track store cannot locate them, and that pretending otherwise silently launders a weapon's suspicion score. Tier printed beside every verdict, tier-3 excluded from through-wall tallies by default with a labelled opt-in, hollow diamond in world. That is exactly right.

`K_space` forking the phase ribbon rather than blending is a good honesty-of-abstraction move — "the fork itself is the model-appropriateness signal" — and the participant-count line `N(t)` under the density sparkline (so an arrivals-driven rise reads differently from an escalation among the same people) is a small, cheap, real insight. The breadcrumb/history reconciliation ("Back pops the last breadcrumb segment too, so the two never disagree") is stated more precisely than most.

Deductions. Two rungs are both numbered 1 and rung 4 is conditional, so the ladder reads as a table rather than a structure. The dance is entirely cursor-mediated — everything shares one cursor, which is clean but shallow: there is no operator, no anchor, no shared parameter state beyond the cursor. The overlay is explicitly demoted to "one of its lenses", which costs against the brief's in-world emphasis. Q9 is absent. And the friendly-fire dependency is flagged as *unconfirmed against the fixed plan* rather than designed around.

### 04 — Cartographer (44) — 7th

The correct diagnosis ("every officer question is secretly a spatial question") and a genuine comprehension fix in the revision: the War Map now keeps **its own back/forward stack of view snapshots** (mode, layers, window, crop) plus a Home button and a breadcrumb dot trail of the last ~12 dig locations — spatial memory kept distinct from the 3-slot comparison pins. Digging on the map is now as reversible as digging in the Inspector, which was the thread the original lost.

Good analysis fixes: N-team occupancy with a per-cell margin (a strict generalisation of the two-team zero-crossing, not a rewrite); **coverage as a channel independent of margin**, so contested ground and data gaps never share one glyph (band width = contested, dash = coverage); PCA explained-variance printed so the officer can see *why* the tool trusts an axis, with an auto-split into per-segment ribbons when `r < 2`; evidence-scaled team confidence (`margin × evidenceFactor`) so a clean-looking margin on two exchanges renders hatched; and the Staging vs Arrival-point split that stops mid-raid teleport clusters being labelled gear-up areas. The "Show in-world" bearing compass strip that always draws something, gated or not, is a small excellent UX fix.

Deductions. Heat / flow / territory are three *layers at one altitude*, not three rungs; the ladder is mostly a map with switches, and rung 4 admittedly holds two different constructs. Team drift is the weakest of the upper-middle group — `teamConfidence(avatar, team)` is not time-indexed the way 06's epochs, 11's windows or 07's intervals are, which matters for the brief's explicit "membership can change during a session". Q5 and Q9 are unaddressed.

### 10 — In-world-first (43) — 8th

The best thesis sentence about the ladder in the whole field: **"the ladder of abstraction is a camera problem"** — rung 0 is standing at the spot; every rung above is a different way of painting the same ground; you never leave the world to go up. That is a real commitment, and the overlay specification that follows is the most complete in the set: a rung × draws × encoding × click-target × uncertainty-cue table where every cell is filled.

Its input discipline is the best anywhere and is argued from the codebase: the click-and-hold pie menu is *dropped*, not fixed, because no trigger fix removes the collision with alt-cam drag-to-orbit, the plan's plain-left-click hook, and SL's own right-click pie menu; the actions become plain-click chips with an Inspector text mirror. Chips hold a fixed minimum screen-space hit size. Breadcrumb chips are *view snapshots* (layers, trail window, slider, front toggle) with a pinned Overview home chip and later history kept rather than popped.

Two analysis contributions stand out. Heat excludes zero-coverage events into a **visible "unattributed" counter** rather than smearing them onto a nearby cell as if the position were known. And behaviour evidence is three chips never summed, with reaction time reported as a *window* — "400–600 ms, floored by the 2 Hz bridge tick" — plus an explicit note that the figure is a product of the track's interpolation model as much as the avatar's behaviour. That is the most epistemically careful sentence about a suspicion metric anyone wrote.

Deductions. Rung 2 is labelled "abstract over parameters" but delivers an aggregate field, which is abstraction over time and space — a genuine rung mislabel in an entry whose thesis is the ladder. No rung 4, no sweep depth beyond the LOS fan. The noun set is small and backlinks are footer chips only. No time-indexed teams at all. Q5 thin, Q9 absent.

### 12 — Fairness (41) — 9th

The best-focused entry, and correctly focused on the question the owner is most likely to be misled by: "the tool never renders a verdict; it renders a ledger of falsifiable signals, each shown next to its own innocent explanation and its own population baseline." Removing the summed "Signals" column and turning the slider from a rank into a **filter** — with each signal keeping its own raw count and no cross-column sum permitted — is exactly the right structural fix, and the entry knows the residual risk (a filter that greys rows can still read as a softened leaderboard).

Its strongest analysis contribution is genuinely important and unique: **every LOS verdict is re-checked at t−1 s and t+1 s** and labelled ROBUST if it survives, else both verdicts are shown. The brief says log events can lag by up to 1 s, which is *comparable to the entire travel window being swept* — every other entry sweeps τ while anchoring to a timestamp that can be wrong by more than τ. Only 12 catches it. The explicit "no reaction cue available" ambush state, per-time-window baselines re-segmented at clustering changepoints, the excluded-outlier audit trail that cannot be cleared without a logged reason, and the **Signal page** (a documentation noun per signal type: definition, formula, known false-positive causes, live instance list) are all right.

The new rung 4 is a good idea: project the currently-flagged instances back onto the Raid Shape canvas, so "a flag pattern that lines up with a front band reads as geography; a pattern with no front nearby reads as a real question." That makes counter-evidence spatial instead of rhetorical.

Deductions. The ladder climbs one axis only, and the §2 argument about why there is no rung 5 reads as self-litigation. Raid Shape is a bolted-on rung 1 that exists because a review said it had to. Q2, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7 are thin to absent — this is a single-question instrument, and the brief asked for nine.

### 07 — Equipment (39) — 10th

Correct central diagnosis in the revision: equipment-first cannot answer "where and when did people fight", because there is no equipment attached to that question yet — so the **Engagement Field**, a weapon-blind density backdrop, becomes the first thing the officer sees. Good, honest, and it makes the rest of the entry work.

Real contributions: breadcrumbs carrying the session time active at each visit (`Session@14:32 > Death "Rowan" > "SA-90 Mk2"@14:32 > Wielder: Kestrel@09:10`), so a deep dig never loses which moment motivated it — small, cheap, and nobody else does it. The Event Dossier generalised to non-lethal HITs closes a real dead-end the brief explicitly named. The classification checklist is the only one whose arithmetic actually sums (partial credit for 2-of-3 bridge confirmations = 17 of 25), with the HUD attach-point override *priced* at −10 and visible rather than silent. The solid-vs-dashed rule (DEATH positions solid at full alpha; every reconstructed position dashed at 60 % with a "reconstructed" tag) is applied globally, and the honest note that `LLScrollListCtrl` is flat so family expand/collapse is bespoke row-splicing is exactly the kind of correction I want to see (it is true).

Deductions. The rungs are per-equipment views with stated but shallow transitions — no operator, no anchor, no shared state. The Engagement Field is deliberately not a page, so "where" has no destination to dig into, which weakens the wiki claim at precisely the point the entry just conceded. Q2, Q6 thin; Q9 absent.

### 09 — Progressive (38) — 11th

The most likely to ship and the least likely to teach. The thesis is coherent and disciplined ("if it can be drawn on the world, draw it there; if it must be text, put it one level deep") and the main floater at 340×120 with one content pane is a real position, not a cop-out.

Two genuine contributions. **Branch-preserving pins**: popping to a mid-stack breadcrumb segment and then pushing somewhere different auto-pins the discarded forward branch, labelled by its leaf noun — taught in one sentence ("the breadcrumb is linear, but branching off it leaves a pin behind") with the trade-off stated honestly (restores a leaf, not a stack). And the **clustering objective critique**, which is the sharpest algorithmic argument anyone makes: unsigned edge weights, minimise total intra-cluster damage via KL-style local search, seeded from group id else the highest-weight edge — and the observation that this objective is *aligned* with the intra-group skirmish (two heavily mutually-damaging sub-teams sit on one dominant edge, so minimising intra-cluster damage separates them) whereas plain label propagation on signed weights is not. Time-bounded overrides `(avatar, team, effective_from)` are the right shape.

Deductions. Rungs 0–3 with no rung 4 "by design", a rung 3 that is sparklines plus a membership strip, and an "unbent" axis that is admittedly a perpendicular bisector between two centroids. One page visible at a time with no side-by-side is a self-acknowledged cost that bites hardest on the promotion-review workflow. Q9 absent, Q5 and Q6 thin. Much of the analysis section is "unchanged".

## Grafts — best ideas for a synthesis regardless of which entry wins

1. **01 — Anchor vs witness step-down.** `]` writes the destination's anchor (the instance you climbed from); `[` reads it, so up-then-down is the identity. Arriving cold via a link, pin or search, `[` lands on the aggregate's medoid **witness** and the rail says `witness`, not `back`, with `[`+Left/Right reaching the extremes. Plus: the rail prints the exact landing instance *before* the key is pressed. This is the field's best answer to "every abstraction must offer a step back down."
2. **01 — Rung-indexed overlay.** Pressing `]` changes what the *world* draws (moment → plan → LOS fan → front axis and density → session ground plan), so the officer watches abstraction happen in the place they already understand rather than in a chart beside it. Pair it with 01's Chart twin and parity rule so geography survives the stationary + alt-cam gate.
3. **08 — Mandatory tag-free second solve.** Run every clustering solve twice, with and without the group-tag prior, and light a `(!)` tag-dependence flag plus a STRAINED % strain meter on the band header in the *default* view when they disagree. Cost is one extra solve on ≤100 nodes, off the draw path. This turns the intra-group skirmish from something the officer must suspect into something the tool volunteers unasked.
4. **08 — Two navigation histories, drawn identically, never merged.** A noun chain (what I was reading about) and a View chain (cursor, window, tab, parameter set, selection = where I was reading), because those two fail separately. Anything that moves the cursor or the parameters pushes the previous View first. 01 arrives at the same insight via anchor-vs-back-stack; take whichever framing, but take one.
5. **02 — Flip points in the lede.** Because a sweep runs on the grid the claim already used, the nearest parameter value at which the verdict changes is free to compute — so print it in the headline and infobox, not behind a facet: "BLOCKED for travel < 0.9 s; CLEAR at or beyond it (5 of 6 candidates)." Pair with **11's flip fraction** as the companion stability number, and 11's demotion of the existential "a clear shot exists" to a small chip, since that test is uninformative when true and decisive when false.
6. **02 — Typed backlink polarity, plus named trails as the artifact.** Officer claims carry a polarity that renders in every backlink list: ⚑ flag / ✓ cleared / ⇄ override / ✎ note, so a reviewer seeing "3 claims" can tell two exonerations from an accusation without opening them. The visit sequence records as a graph that can be named and frozen (`ss:trail/promotion-nyx`) and dumped as a linked outline — the promotion dossier, violation case and post-raid briefing, for free, from navigation you already did.
7. **11 — Drift / noise / short as three distinct outcomes.** Cluster across θ *and* across time windows, match cluster ids across both, then distinguish: **drift** (modal cluster changes between adjacent windows, stability high on both sides) → chevron plus a printed change time; **noise** (stability low inside the window) → hatch, no chevron; **short** (small sample) → its own badge. Never conflate low n with low clarity. Normalise edge weight by tracked-and-armed seconds so late arrivals are judged on rate, not volume.
8. **05 — Attacker position tiers.** TRACK / LAST-SEEN / APPROX-VIA-OWNER, resolved in that order, printed beside every LOS verdict, damage-in-flight line and suspicion tally, drawn with a hollow diamond in world when below TRACK, and excluded from through-wall tallies at tier 3 by default with a clearly labelled opt-in. Without this, turrets, traps, mounts and vehicles either vanish from the analysis or silently launder a guessed position into a weapon's suspicion score.
9. **06 — Input quality gates output confidence.** A BLOCKED verdict draws red *only* when at least one dense (viewer- or bridge-quality) sample was tested; otherwise grey, regardless of what the raycast returned. Generalise the rule: the certainty of a mark is bounded by the certainty of its worst input, never by the crispness of the computation. 01's haze rule ("no aggregate may be drawn without the unknown fraction it was built from drawn in the same mark") is the same principle at aggregate scale.
10. **12 — Timestamp-lag robustness check.** Re-run every LOS verdict at `t − 1 s` and `t + 1 s` — the log's own stated lag, which is comparable to the entire travel window being swept — and label it ROBUST if it does not flip, or show both verdicts if it does. Every other entry sweeps τ while anchoring to a timestamp that can be wrong by more than τ; this is the second axis of uncertainty the sweep was missing.

Honourable mentions that did not make ten: **08's refusal discipline** (Front draws nothing when no contour survives; percentiles vanish below n=6; spawn-cohort affinity switches off when the raid has no respawn mechanic; untrusted events carry zero weight, with `+untrusted` available as a sweep axis) and **11's tested spawn anchor**, which together form a coherent house rule — an axis or a term must earn its existence from the data before it is offered. **06's occlusion point cloud** (scatter the hit points the BLOCKED raycasts already returned instead of inventing wall geometry) is free and honest. **10's reaction time as a window floored by the tick rate**, with the note that the number is a product of the interpolation model as much as the behaviour, is the right way to report every behavioural metric. **04's separation of contested from coverage** into two independent visual channels should be a global rule, not one chart's fix.
