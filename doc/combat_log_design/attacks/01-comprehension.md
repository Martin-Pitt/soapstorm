# Attack 01 — Comprehension review of Entry 01 (LADDER-FIRST)

Lens: does an officer actually move up and down a ladder of abstraction, or is this a pile of views wearing a ladder costume? Walked officer questions 1, 3, 8; stress-tested against freeform combat and the alt-cam constraint.

## Verdict up front

The mechanics are the strongest submission this brief is likely to get — a real vertical/horizontal split, a witness concept that makes step-down deterministic, and uncertainty that is genuinely load-bearing (haze rule, coverage chips, sweep-instead-of-verdict) rather than a decorative caveat line. But the document oversells one of its own two headline invariants, and it quietly outsources all of session-level *spatial* comprehension to a view that the brief itself gates to a narrow physical condition (stationary + alt-cam), with zero 2D fallback. Both are fixable, neither is cosmetic.

## Walking the officer questions

**Q1 — "How did this raid go, overall? Where did the fighting happen, when, between whom?"**

The walkthrough (§7) opens at R4, sees three phases, drills `[[[` into one interesting alley ambush, and stops. That's a good demonstration of the ladder mechanic, but it does not actually answer Q1 as asked. "Between whom" is answered by a panel that exists (the R4 teams box: A 17 av / 38 kills, B 19 av / 44 kills) but the walkthrough narrates past it without ever landing on it — a reader following the prose comes away knowing about one alley trap and nothing about the raid's overall shape. That's a writing gap, not necessarily a structural one, but it's exactly the failure mode the brief warns about: a pile of interesting instances presented as if they were the whole. If the *design* doc's own worked example skips the aggregate-first, drill-into-the-interesting-bit-second pattern it prescribes, the design is not fully trustworthy at doing so out of the box either.

More seriously: everything spatial in this design — "where did the fighting happen" — lives *only* in the in-world overlay (§5). The 2D floater has no map. R1's "Plan" face is in-world; R3's "Front" face unbends space into a 1D `u`-axis that only exists when a two-team structure has already been found; R4's ground plan (heat quads, push arrows) is drawn in-world, not in the Session Board (§4.6), which is pure text/ledgers. So for an officer who is not physically standing still and alt-camming in the correct sim right now — reviewing later in the session while walking around, chatting, or just less than perfectly parked — the "where" third of Q1 is simply unavailable. The brief's own owner's-vision text ("moving and being in first or third person all negate the use of the tool") makes this a real, not hypothetical, gap: the design leans 100% on the overlay for geography and offers no XUI-drawn top-down map as a degraded-but-always-available substitute, despite the brief explicitly allowing custom-drawn panels for exactly this ("node graphs... fine if drawn from primitives"). A simple top-down minimap panel (2D projection of tracks/deaths onto x/y, no camera needed) would have cost little and isn't in the design at all.

**Q3 — "Why did X die at 14:32? Who hit them, with what, from where, could they see them?"**

This one works cleanly. Scrub → click death → Article shows damage shares with the health-vs-damage caveat stated inline → `[2]` LOS sweep shows the BLOCKED/CLEAR split across τ → click a τ value → R0 with exactly the one ray implied by that τ, in-world, at the pillar. Four clicks, one continuous thread, and the uncertainty (mixed verdict, not a clean answer) survives the whole trip instead of getting laundered into a yes/no. This is the design at its best and should be kept close to verbatim.

**Q8 — "Did anyone behave suspiciously? Show evidence and honest uncertainty, not verdicts."**

Also works, and ends on the right note ("consistent with wall tracking and also with a lucky pre-aim; wireframe use cannot be observed") — this is exactly the register the brief asked for. But the walkthrough text has a real error, not a style nit: it says "R4's flagged panel says 'Rook: 4 deaths with positive awareness lead.'" Awareness lead (§6.12) is a property of a *killer* tracking a *victim* through blocked LOS before firing — it is Rook's *kills*, not Rook's *deaths*, that would carry a positive awareness lead. As written, the flagged panel would be telling the officer that Rook died four times while having tracked his own killer through a wall, which is either nonsensical or describes a completely different (and unmodeled) quantity. This needs to be caught and fixed — it's the kind of error that, if it survived into the actual UI copy, would make officers distrust every other flagged item on the panel once they noticed it.

## Does it survive freeform combat?

Better than most entries would. The intra-group skirmish and FFA walkthroughs are not afterthoughts — they exercise the actual seeding-disables-itself logic (§6.8, "seeded by shared active group id *only when* group ids differ across the graph's blocks") and the Braid's `?` state, and the FFA walkthrough explicitly states the discipline that matters most: "Nothing in the UI ever asserts a team the data does not support." That line, if actually honored throughout the implementation, is the single best sentence in the document for the brief's hardest requirement (allegiance as inference, never fact). Keep it as a design commandment, not just a scenario footnote.

One thing neither scenario tests: someone arriving mid-fight and immediately being on nobody's radar (no track history at all yet, not even a coarse one). The mechanisms (coverage gaps, unresolved team margin, `?` avatar) should cover it by the same logic used for Tam, but it's asserted by extension rather than walked — worth a fourth micro-scenario in a revision, since "arriving mid-fight" is called out by name in the brief as something to design for, not just tolerate.

## Uncertainty: honest, mostly

This is genuinely a strength. The haze rule is enforced almost everywhere it's claimed to be — hatching on low-support matrix cells, dashed/hollow track drawing, Wilson intervals instead of raw percentages for through-wall rate, the sweep-strip-instead-of-scalar substitution for every LOS verdict shown anywhere in the UI. The reflex to write "share of damage, not of health" directly into the mark rather than a tooltip (§4.7) is the right instinct — it's uncertainty that survives copy-paste into a Case, which is exactly where an officer under pressure to justify a decision would otherwise quietly drop the caveat.

But there is one place the document is *not* honest with itself about uncertainty, and it's structural rather than cosmetic (see next section).

## The identity invariant does not hold, and the document's own example proves it

Section 1 states two invariants: the haze rule (which holds up), and "up-then-down is the identity (ascend a death into a bout, descend, you get that death back)." Section 6.18 defines step-down as landing on the aggregate's **witness** — the medoid instance, "nearest the matrix cell's centroid; the death nearest the bout's space-time centre." Section 8 (Risks) then says, correctly: "Witness medoids can mislead: a bout's representative death may be atypical." These two claims cannot both be true. If bare `[` from a bout always lands on the medoid death, then for a bout with N deaths, only 1 of N round-trips (ascend that specific death, then descend) actually returns you to the death you started from; the other N−1 land you on someone else's death instead. The document's own flagship example bout, "West Gate" (§4.3), has six deaths — meaning the "invariant" as literally stated is false five-sixths of the time in the one worked example given.

This matters for more than pedantry: the whole selling point of the ladder is that digging never loses the thread. An officer who ascends *Vex's* death into the bout to see it in context, then presses `[` expecting to land back on Vex, and instead lands on a different avatar's death, has just lost the thread — silently, with no error, in the tool's own terms of use. The mitigation exists (the shared back/forward history stack is genuinely lossless, per `(rung, subject, face, cursor, window)`), but that's a different, unglamorous mechanism (Backspace) doing the actual work, while the prose sells the glamorous one (bare `[`) as if it had the same guarantee. Fix by either (a) demoting the claim to "bare `[` lands on a representative instance, not necessarily *the* instance you came from — use Backspace to retrace exactly," stated plainly next to the rung rail, not buried in the risks section; or (b) making per-subject "last visited" landings the default and medoid only a fallback when there is no recent visit. Either is fine; stating a false invariant as a design pillar is not.

## Smaller cracks

- **The breadcrumb doesn't visually distinguish vertical from horizontal moves**, even though that distinction is the thesis's central claim. The example breadcrumb — `Session ▸over time▸ Bout "West Gate" ▸over avatars▸ Team B ▸ Cadmus` — renders rung-changing operators (`over time`, `over avatars`) and a same-rung subject hop (`Team B ▸ Cadmus`) with the identical `▸` glyph. An officer who hasn't memorized the operator vocabulary cannot tell, glancing at the crumb, whether they're reading a change of altitude or a change of subject — which is precisely the confusion the ladder/wiki split exists to prevent. This needs a visual distinction (different arrow, colour, or indent) in any real build.

- **The per-noun rung mapping is demonstrated for exactly one noun (Weapon, plus Death for the witness mechanic) and asserted by fiat for the rest.** "Any noun, any rung, one click" (§3) is the claim that most directly answers "is this a pile of views," so it deserves more than one worked example. What is R2 for an Avatar — the awareness sweep, the reaction-interval sweep, or does the officer have to pick? What is R1 for a Team — a Bout Ledger filtered to members, spanning the whole session? Without a stated rule, different implementers (or different UI states) will make different, inconsistent choices here, and *that* inconsistency is exactly what would turn this into the pile-of-views the ladder is supposed to prevent.

- **Bout/phase segmentation constants (ε = 30 m, minPts = 4, κ = 1.2 m/s, the 90 s / 5 % lull rule) are fixed, not sweepable**, even though they structure every higher rung (which deaths belong to which bout, which bouts merge into which phase). Everything downstream of a wrong bout boundary — the Ledger, the Front chart, the Session phases — inherits that boundary silently. Given the design's own R2 rung is built around exposing exactly this kind of parameter sensitivity, leaving the most consequential parameter un-sweepable is a real gap, not just a missing nice-to-have.

- **Bout Ledger scaling**: one row per avatar (§4.3) reads fine for five participants; a classic-raid bout with fifteen or more active combatants either scrolls (defeating "all moments at once" as a single glance) or needs a plan not specified here (grouping by team block? collapsing quiet rows?). Worth stating the degradation strategy rather than leaving it implicit.

## What must be kept

- The vertical/horizontal split itself (ladder changes altitude, wiki changes subject) is the right organizing idea and should survive any rewrite.
- The **witness** concept — every aggregate names its representative concrete instance — is what makes "step back down" a real, tool-provided answer instead of homework for the officer. Keep it; just stop overselling its guarantees (see above).
- The **haze rule** and its consistent execution: coverage percentages, hatched low-support cells, sweep-strips-instead-of-scalars, Wilson intervals, "share of damage, not of health" written into the mark itself. This is the best answer in the field to "is uncertainty honest or decorative."
- The **team-seeding-disables-itself** behavior for intra-group skirmishes, and the explicit refusal to draw team structure the data doesn't support in the FFA case — these are the two hardest requirements in the brief and this design meets them directly, with worked scenarios.
- The Q3 walkthrough end-to-end (death → shares → LOS sweep → one concrete ray → Case) as the reference case for what "digging without losing the thread" should feel like.
- Evidence-first article layout (prose, then table, then evidence, then links) and the discipline of writing the caveat into the number rather than a tooltip.
