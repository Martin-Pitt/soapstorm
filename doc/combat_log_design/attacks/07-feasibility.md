# Attack on Entry 07 — Equipment-first Combat Log (ANALYSIS+FEASIBILITY lens)

## Fatal

1. **"Exact firing position" is not observable, and the entry says it is.** Rung 0 promises "a single glowing line from the *exact* firing position to the exact hit position." DAMAGE carries no positions at all; DEATH carries real `source_pos`/`target_pos`, but everything else (every non-fatal DAMAGE event, which is the overwhelming majority of the log — 4,812 events vs 37 deaths in the entry's own Overview mock) has no ground-truth muzzle position. The plan's only recourse is `sampleAt(owner_or_source_track, t)`, i.e. the avatar's body position, degraded by up to 1 s log lag, viewer/coarse/bridge sample quality, decimation, and Hermite/linear interpolation across gaps. A held rifle is not fired from the avatar root; a vehicle-mounted turret's "owner track" is the vehicle, not the gun; a thrown grenade's actual release point is nowhere in the data. The **Fire Fan** (Rung 1, §2, §5) is described as "a static 'photograph' of the weapon's whole campaign" built entirely from these positions — it is a photograph of the wielder's torso trajectory, not the weapon's firing geometry, and the entry never says so. This is exactly the "present inference as fact" failure the brief warns against, and it sits under two of the five ladder rungs.

2. **Splash radius has no computation path.** §4.2's Codex line for "Frag Trap" reads `splash r≈4m`. Nothing in DAMAGE, DEATH, or the equipment registry carries an area-of-effect radius, and §6 (Analysis model) never defines how to derive one. A defensible proxy exists — cluster simultaneous DAMAGE events sharing one `source`/`rezzer` at one instant and take the spread of `sampleAt(target_track, t)` positions — but the entry doesn't specify it, doesn't cite it as an estimate, and prints it as a bare number next to hard counts like "4 kills." Ship this as written and an officer will read `r≈4m` as measured fact.

3. **Team and per-avatar "confidence" percentages have no formula anywhere in the document.** §4.5 prints `Ashguard (14, conf 82%)`, `Wraith (11, conf 79%)`, `clustering ... confidence: overall 71%`, and `Doe(53%->Ashguard)`; §4.4 prints `team(Rowan)=Wraith(88%)`. Section 6 — the section whose explicit job is "how each is computed" — defines a classification-confidence checklist and a suspicion ratio, but says nothing about how label-propagation-on-a-damage-graph produces a scalar confidence per cluster or per node. Label propagation naturally yields cluster *membership*, not calibrated confidence; you'd need something like edge-weight margin (winning label's damage-share vs. runner-up) per node, but the entry never proposes it. This is the brief's central warning ("present allegiance as inference with confidence, never a hard fact") violated by omission: five distinct confidence numbers appear on screen with zero specified computation.

4. **The entry's own worked evidence contradicts the plan's classification rule.** §4.3's Dossier evidence line reads `[x] attach point 34 (HUD point, handheld override) seen on 3 avatars` while classifying the item WEAPON. The engineering plan's classifier states attach points 31–38 are categorically HUD points — that range is exactly how the Codex's own HUD bucket (§4.2, "HUD (9)") gets populated. An item at point 34 is either a HUD by the stated rule, or the entry needs an explicit "handheld override" exception with its own evidence and confidence weighting — but no such rule exists in §6's checklist (attach-point-known is worth flat +30, with no override branch). As written the worked example is either wrong or relies on an unspecified special case, and it's the entry's flagship illustration of "evidence, not a black box."

## Serious

5. **Fire Fan hit sparks share the same fabrication problem as firing positions, undisclosed.** Even where `target` position is sampled rather than firing position, the same track-lag/coarse/gap issues apply, and for non-death DAMAGE events there is no equivalent to DEATH's authoritative `target_pos`. The overlay description (§5) states the hit spark plainly, with no dashing/alpha distinction from the death case where a real position exists. Two different evidence qualities (measured vs. reconstructed) get the identical glyph.

6. **No aggregation rule from per-instance classification confidence to per-family confidence.** The Codex (§4.2, §6 "Equipment family key") groups raw instances into families and displays one confidence per family (e.g. `conf: 92%` in §4.3), but classification evidence like "bridge CombatObjectInfo confirm (2 of 3 instances timed out)" is inherently per-instance. Average? Worst-of-N? Require unanimous agreement and flag disagreement? The entry never says, so the single number on the Dossier header is unauditable even though the entry's stated philosophy is "never a black-box number."

7. **Position-source conflicts are unaddressed.** §6's engagement envelope formula reads `sampleAt(owner_or_source_track, t)`, and the plan separately allows a visible projectile (`source`) to have its own track distinct from the `owner`/`rezzer` track. When both exist and disagree — e.g. a thrown object arcing away from the thrower before impact — the entry gives no rule for which wins, yet the resulting single histogram bucket (§2 Rung 4, §4.3) is drawn as if it were one clean number.

8. **Collapsible per-instance rows under a family row are asserted as an existing pattern without evidence.** §4.2 states "a pattern already used elsewhere in the viewer" for tree-style expand/collapse inside `LLScrollListCtrl`; the viewer's list widget is flat, not hierarchical. It's buildable (rebuild the visible row set on toggle, inserting/removing instance rows), but that's custom bookkeeping the entry hand-waves past rather than specifies, in a screen (the flagship Codex) that depends on it working.

## Minor

9. §4.6 Loadout Comparison caps columns at 3 with no stated reason beyond "stay legible" — fine as a design choice, but should note it as a chosen limit, not a technical one, since XUI floaters can scroll horizontally too.
10. §5's fire-fan concurrency cap ("capped at 3 concurrent") and §8's line-subsampling disclosure ("showing 300 of 1,240 shots") are good self-aware performance mitigations, but neither is cross-referenced to a concrete draw-call budget; at 100k events and a 2 h session, a family's per-frame line count should be bounded by a stated constant, not "capped" in prose.
11. The activity sparkline's "min-max normalized per row" note (§6) correctly flags a legibility caveat but the leaderboard's incremental bucket maintenance (event count per 15 buckets) is never explicitly declared as append-only/incremental in §6 the way the plan requires; it's implied, not stated, for a 100k-event session.

## Feasibility verdict per screen

- **4.1 Overview** (transport bar, timeline, stacked bar, leaderboard with sparklines): buildable in XUI immediate-mode with custom `LLView` draws for the stacked quad bar and per-row polyline sparklines; no web view needed. Sparkline/leaderboard values must be precomputed/cached per bucket, not recomputed per frame, which the entry doesn't state but doesn't contradict either.
- **4.2 Equipment Codex**: buildable, but the nested expand/collapse needs bespoke row management beyond stock `LLScrollListCtrl` semantics (see Serious #8). Filtering by kind/search is standard.
- **4.3 Equipment Dossier**: buildable — panels are static-composed `LLPanel`s with custom-drawn histogram/sparkline widgets, all on-demand (single item selected, not all 58 at once). The numbers it presents (classification confidence, damage profile, engagement envelope) are the ones with unresolved computation paths (Fatal #4, Serious #6, #7), not the widget mechanics.
- **4.4 Death Dossier**: buildable and best-grounded screen in the entry — DEATH's real `source_pos`/`target_pos` and the plan's actual LOS candidate algorithm map directly onto the wireframe. The team-confidence figures inline (`Wraith(88%)`, `Ashguard(95%)`) inherit Fatal #3.
- **4.5 Teams / Armory Board**: buildable as two side-by-side `LLPanel`s with text tables; the confidence numbers are Fatal #3.
- **4.6 Loadout Comparison**: buildable, static side-by-side text columns.
- **Overlay** (§5): fully within the decided primitive set — lines, muzzle/hit glyphs, rings for LOS candidates, dashed unknown markers, screen-rect click picking, dual depth-tested/depth-off draw for occlusion signalling. No screen in this entry requires anything beyond lines/rings/text/quads/click-picking; nothing needs a web view.

## What must be kept

- **Family key with explicit split/merge override** (§6, §8) — sound, matches the brief's demand for officer override on every inference, directly buildable.
- **LOS candidate sweep bound to the plan's actual algorithm** (§2 Rung 2a, §4.4, §5) — the single best-grounded piece of the design; ring colouring (CLEAR/BLOCKED/UNKNOWN-dashed) draws uncertainty rather than stating it, exactly per the brief.
- **Through-wall suspicion ratio with an `n`-gated grey floor** (§6, §8) — well-specified formula (`BLOCKED/(BLOCKED+CLEAR)`, UNKNOWN excluded from ratio but kept in `n`), refuses to render a misleading percentage below `n=5`. Keep verbatim.
- **Classification evidence shown as an itemised checklist rather than a bare score** (§4.3) — the right instinct, even though the worked example currently contradicts the plan's own attach-point rule (fix the example, keep the pattern).
- **DEATH-vs-DAMAGE position honesty distinction as a design principle** — the entry should extend to firing/hit positions the same rigor it already applies to death dossiers: reconstructed positions from Rung 0/1 need visibly different rendering (e.g. dashed/lower-alpha) from DEATH's ground-truth positions, and the "exact" language in §2 must go.
- **Auto-suppression of the equipment-signature team hint during intra-group skirmishes** (§7) — correctly reasons about a scenario the brief calls out explicitly; keep.
- **Codex's explicit "why unknown" line for every unresolved object** (§4.2) — matches the brief's "never silent gap" requirement; keep.
