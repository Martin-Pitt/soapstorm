# Attack on Entry 06 — Forensics-First (lens: ANALYSIS+FEASIBILITY)

## Fatal

1. **Attribution "probabilities" have no stated formula, yet the UI prints them as precise percentages.** Section 6 says: group DAMAGE by `(owner, rezzer)`, weight by "damage contributed, recency, whether it's the killing blow," then "normalize to probabilities." That is three heterogeneous, unitless factors combined by an unspecified function. The Death Report mockup then prints `Finn 72% | Orr 18% | UNK 10%` as if this were a measured quantity. It is not — it is an invented scoring heuristic dressed in the vocabulary of statistics. Two different reasonable weightings of "recency vs. total damage" will produce different numbers with no way for the officer to know which is right, and the design gives them no way to see the raw inputs (the actual per-group damage totals and timings) *before* the "probability" is applied. This is the single largest instance of inference presented as fact in the entry: a made-up composite score wearing a percent sign. Fix: either drop the false precision (show ranked shares as raw damage totals with a qualitative "likely / possible / long-shot" label) or specify the formula and show its inputs alongside the output so the officer can audit it, not just trust it.

2. **LOS candidate position sourcing silently assumes the shooter is a moving avatar; it breaks for the majority of the equipment taxonomy the brief itself defines.** Section 6's LOS filmstrip computes candidate positions as `attacker's sampleAt(owner, t_k)`, i.e., the *owner avatar's* track. That is correct only for a handheld weapon fired by the owner standing there. For a **deployable** (turret, trap) the shot originates at the *deployable object's* position, not the owner's live position — the owner may be elsewhere or offline by the time it fires. For a **mount** (owner seated, firing from the mount) the shot originates at the mount's position, which may differ from the seated owner's nominal avatar position (SL seats offset the avatar into the prim). For a **vehicle-mounted weapon**, same issue. The brief is explicit that these categories exist and must be classified; the analysis model for the tool's flagship "could they see them" feature only has a computation path for the simplest case (handheld, owner=shooter=position source) and is silently wrong — not merely uncertain — for turrets, traps, and mounts, which are exactly the cases most likely to raise "kills through walls" suspicion in the first place (a stationary turret behind a wall is the paradigm through-wall-kill scenario). No fallback to a rezzer/root-object track is described anywhere in section 6.

3. **The Micro-Replay's 2D top-down panel implies a schematic of actual wall geometry with no computation or rendering path.** The wireframe in 4.3 draws `wall█████` as a filled obstruction inside a "custom `LLView`, primitives only" panel that the design insists is "always available" (i.e., not gated on stationary+alt-cam, unlike the 3D overlay). But the only geometry fact the system has, per the brief and the engineering plan, is a boolean raycast result (`lineSegmentIntersectWorldGeometry` — hit/no-hit against world collision, not a queryable floorplan). There is no store of wall polygons, no top-down render-to-texture pass, and no such capability anywhere in the fixed engineering plan. Drawing an actual building silhouette in a 2D immediate-mode panel independent of the 3D camera requires either (a) an offscreen orthographic top-down render (a real rendering subsystem, out of scope and unplanned) or (b) reconstructing wall segments from the pattern of LOS hit points accumulated over the raid (a genuinely new derived-data structure, itself worth designing, not mentioned). As written this screen cannot be built from the stated data; it reads as a screenshot of a tool that doesn't have the geometry access the entry assumes. It is not a web-view problem (XUI can draw lines/rects/text fine) — it is a **missing data source** problem that the ASCII wireframe glosses over by just drawing a wall.

## Serious

4. **"fd-confirmed" log lag is misrepresented as a general per-event capability.** Section 6 (Quality) and the Death Report mockup ("log lag 0.2s (fd-confirmed)") imply this measurement is available on ordinary case files. Per the engineering plan, `final_damage` records exist **only for the reviewing officer's own avatar** as the wearer of the bridge attachment — they do not exist for third-party victims, which is nearly every death an officer reviews (the walkthrough example, Nyx Ashworth, is not the reviewing officer). For the overwhelming majority of Death Reports, "log lag" can never be `fd`-confirmed; it can only carry the generic ≤1 s caveat from the brief. The design should say this plainly instead of showing a mockup that suggests confirmed sub-second lag measurement is the normal case — as written it overclaims precision the data source cannot deliver for other people's deaths.

5. **Yaw-based "reaction/awareness" signal is not computable for coarse-quality samples, and this isn't addressed.** The brief states far/coarse avatar updates are position-only at ~1 Hz with z quantized to 4 m; it does not claim coarse updates carry heading. Section 6's reaction flag needs "yaw-rate ... in the following 0.5–1.5 s" and "whether their facing began tracking the attacker's true bearing." For any avatar not producing dense viewer updates or bridge ticks at that moment (i.e., anyone the officer wasn't rendering nearby, or a lapse in bridge poll), no yaw signal exists to compute a yaw-rate from. Given this is explicitly flagged in the brief as "the most misuse-prone feature" (entry's own §8), silently degrading to no-signal without stating what the UI shows in that case (must not silently omit the flag as if "checked, found nothing suspicious" — that reads as exculpatory when it's actually "couldn't check") is a rigor gap with real consequences for the exact use case (violation review) the brief cares most about getting honest.

6. **Front-line estimate stacks two independent inferences without propagating their combined uncertainty.** Computing "median position of avatar-pairs exchanging damage that session-clustering marks as opposing" requires (a) the team clustering already being right and (b) resolving a position for a DAMAGE event via track `sampleAt` for both parties (itself track-quality-dependent, and — see point 2 — position-source-ambiguous for non-avatar-owned shots). The entry's only uncertainty statement for this quantity is "inferred, smoothed" on the lane chart; it doesn't show the officer that a wobbly front line might be wobbly because team confidence is low *this minute*, not because the fighting was actually diffuse. Given the entry's own emphasis on "confidence, never a hard fact," this is the one composite quantity in the whole entry that ships without any confidence readout at all.

## Minor

7. **Confidence buckets (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW) have no numeric thresholds.** Acceptable as a qualitative bucket per the brief's spirit, but worth naming as an open decision — without stated thresholds, two different deaths with visibly different evidence quality could land in the same bucket, undercutting the "digging" promise (the officer digs into the Quality panel expecting the bucket to mean something specific).

8. **Team Ledger node-graph layout stability during slider drags isn't specified.** If node positions are recomputed by the clustering pass on every drag tick (not just recolored), the graph will visually jitter, defeating the "watch it respond" Bret Victor moment the entry explicitly aims for in §4.6. The entry says "recolors nodes live," which implies positions are stable, but doesn't say so — worth confirming explicitly rather than leaving it implicit.

9. **Docket workflow is self-flagged as unspecified** (entry §8) — correctly scoped as future work, not a defect, but it means one of the two officer-facing "closing the loop" mechanisms (flag → review → briefing export) mentioned throughout the walkthroughs has no answer yet for how a briefing actually gets produced (there is no document/export target described anywhere, and XUI has no built-in report generation — this would need its own feasibility pass before being relied upon in a promotion/violation workflow).

## Screens needing a web view

None of the described screens strictly require HTML/web rendering — the Death Wall, Timeline/Overview, Case File pages, and Team Ledger graph are all achievable with XUI scroll lists/tabs plus custom `LLView` primitive drawing (lines, rects, text, per-vertex alpha), consistent with the engineering plan's existing conventions (`renderDebug`, `hud_render_utf8text`, `LLView` subclasses). The one exception is the **2D top-down micro-replay wall schematic** (Fatal #3): as specified it needs a rendering/data capability (real geometry access or a render-to-texture pass) that doesn't exist in the plan, which is functionally the same failure mode as "needs a web view" — a rendering surface the plan doesn't provide — even though the *widget technology* (XUI custom LLView) is fine. The fix is data/scope, not technology.

## Quantities with no computation path (summary)

- **Attack-share "probability"** (Fatal 1): formula unstated; presented with false numeric precision.
- **LOS candidate position for deployables/mounts/vehicles** (Fatal 2): no path from equipment classification to a correct position source; only handheld-weapon case is covered.
- **2D micro-replay wall geometry** (Fatal 3): no store or render path for wall shapes exists anywhere in the fixed plan.
- **Per-third-party-death "fd-confirmed" log lag** (Serious 4): the data source (`final_damage`) structurally cannot produce this for anyone but the reviewing officer's own avatar.
- **Yaw-rate reaction signal for coarse-quality moments** (Serious 5): no fallback when heading data doesn't exist.
- **Combined confidence for the front-line estimate** (Serious 6): two stacked inferences reported with a single generic caveat instead of a composite confidence.

## What must be kept

- The **Death Report as hero screen** with ranked (not collapsed) attribution, a bounded independent micro-replay clock, and first-class **Quality**/**Adjustments** panels — this is the correct answer to "why did X die," and the discipline of never collapsing a split attribution into a false single name is exactly right and should survive any rewrite of the underlying math (point 1's fix changes the formula, not this structural decision).
- The **LOS filmstrip as a swath across the travel window**, not a single boolean at `t` — this is the one design in the competition that takes the brief's travel-time false-positive warning seriously and makes it visible rather than computing a verdict and hiding the ambiguity.
- The **overlay uncertainty grammar**: dashed/thin for track gaps, translucent bands for COARSE-quality positions, and — critically — the rule that a BLOCKED verdict only draws red if at least one dense sample was actually tested, otherwise it draws grey regardless of the raycast result. That rule is the single best piece of engineering discipline in the entry: it prevents the tool from asserting "blocked" off of untrustworthy input, which is precisely the failure mode the brief warns about hardest (accusation engines built on bad data).
- **Breadcrumb + pin rail + backlinks** navigation model — matches the brief's "dig and dig, Wikipedia-style" requirement directly and is straightforwardly buildable in XUI (list/tab widgets, no exotic requirements).
- **Team Ledger's live-redraw confidence-threshold slider** — a genuinely interactive Bret Victor "parameter rung" that is cheap to compute (tens to ~100 nodes) and correctly demonstrated in both the intra-group-skirmish and FFA walkthroughs.
- The **evidence-first language discipline** throughout §7 ("evidence of a wallhack-consistent hit," never "confirmed cheater") — this is a policy commitment, not a rendering concern, and it should be treated as a hard requirement carried into whichever entry wins, especially given Serious finding 5 above (the reaction flag is exactly the feature most likely to be misused if this discipline slips).
