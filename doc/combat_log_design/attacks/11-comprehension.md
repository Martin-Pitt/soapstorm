# Attack on Entry 11 — Comparative (lens: COMPREHENSION)

## Verdict up front

This is the entry that comes closest to actually earning the Bret Victor citation: one query grammar (`filter, rowFacet, colFacet, cellView, sweep, baseline, timeWindow`) drives the Grid, the pages, the pins and the breadcrumbs, so "digging" really does mean "narrowing the same query" rather than jumping between unrelated screens. Uncertainty is wired into the grammar itself (a sweep) rather than bolted on as an error bar, which is the single strongest idea in the entry. But the design has one real structural gap for the exact scenario the brief calls out by name — mid-session allegiance drift — and a handful of smaller comprehension failures that would bite an officer in the first five minutes of real use.

## Does the officer move up and down the ladder, or is it a pile of views?

Mostly a real ladder, with one internal contradiction. The thesis claims "no detail view and no summary view... one view, the Grid" — but §4.2's Death page, §3's eleven distinct nouns each with their own header/sweep/links/backlinks, and the Board in §4.3 are all detail views and summary views by any normal definition. The document even says the four floater tabs "survive as... preset buttons," conceding that the mental model an officer actually uses is a set of named destinations, not a single grid. This isn't fatal — the underlying claim that everything *reduces to* one Query struct is true and valuable — but the thesis oversells "one view" when what's actually delivered, and correctly so, is "one grammar expressed through many pages." A reviewer who takes the thesis literally will be confused the first time they open the Death page and it doesn't look like a grid cell.

The rung definitions in §2 are coherent and the up/down pairs are concrete (click a trail → cursor jumps; click a sweep step → world redraws that hypothesis; click a cell → drill). That is real ladder discipline, better than most competing entries.

## Freeform combat, allegiance drift, intra-group skirmish, mid-fight arrivals

This is where the entry has its one serious hole. The brief is explicit: "Team membership can change during a session... present allegiance as an inference with confidence, never a hard fact." The design's team clustering (§6) runs label propagation once over the *whole session's* damage graph, and sweeps only a confidence threshold θ — not time. θ answers "how sure are we of this fixed clustering," not "did this person switch sides at 14:10." An avatar who genuinely defects mid-raid, or a neutral-sim participant who fights for one loose alliance in the first hour and a different one in the second, is indistinguishable in this model from an avatar whose classification is merely noisy — both show up as a "flickering row" in the allegiance ribbon. The intra-group skirmish walkthrough leans on exactly this ambiguity ("the medics who healed across the line and the one person who arrived late... flickering") and treats it as a feature (don't call it friendly fire), which is the right call for genuinely ambiguous cases — but the model has no way to say "not ambiguous, actually changed sides at minute 40," which is a different and equally real situation the brief asks for. The Phase facet lets an officer post-hoc filter a fixed clustering by time slice, but nothing in §6 describes *re-deriving* membership per phase or per engagement, so a real defection is invisible as such, only visible as unexplained instability. This should be fixed by making the graph windowed (e.g., re-run label propagation per Phase, or with a sliding half-life on edge weight) so the ribbon has a time axis alongside its θ axis, not just θ.

Engagement segmentation (§6, "single-link clustering... within 20 s and 25 m of any member") has a chaining problem the entry doesn't acknowledge anywhere, including its own Risks section. Single-link clustering is exactly the algorithm most prone to producing one giant blob when there is no clean gap: in a 1–2 h raid with continuous skirmishing (the brief's own "freeform" and "neutral sim" cases), it is entirely plausible that no 20 s/25 m gap ever occurs, and "Engagement" — the *default row facet for Q1*, the very first thing an officer sees — degenerates into a single row covering the whole session. That would silently break the flagship walkthrough. This needs either a max-duration/max-diameter cap with forced splits, or a different segmentation entirely (e.g. density troughs in the damage-rate timeline, which the timeline strip already renders), and it needed to be named as a risk.

People arriving mid-fight are handled fine for events and tracks (nothing here is batch-computed per "raid start"), but their team weight in the whole-session graph is diluted by design — a five-minute late arrival who fights hard has the same edge-accumulation opportunity as someone in it for two hours, so late-arrival team assignment will be systematically less confident even when their actual behavior is unambiguous. That's a correct property of confidence, not a bug, but it's not called out, and an officer reading a low-confidence tag on a late arrival needs to know *why* (short window, not weak signal) rather than assume the tool is being unnecessarily cautious.

## Digging from whole-raid to a single hit and back

Walkthrough Q3 genuinely works end to end: timeline tick → Death page with pre-grouped contributing damage → sweep grid → 3D fan → drag one sweep step to pin a single hypothesis → "Compared with" link back out to all of Vex's deaths in death-aligned time. That chain does not lose the thread, and the shared cursor/hover between Grid, breadcrumb and world (§2, end) is the right mechanism to keep "where am I" legible. This section should be kept largely as-is.

The weaker link is going *up*: "click a cell; that cell's subset becomes the whole Grid (drill)" replaces the current Query in place. The entry says there's a shared history stack with Alt+←/→, which is the correct mitigation, but it's asserted in one sentence in §3 and never exercised in any walkthrough — none of Q1/Q3/Q8 shows a drill-then-back. Given that "never losing the thread while digging" is the brief's headline requirement, at least one walkthrough should have shown a drill three levels deep and a clean Alt+← back to the starting Grid, including what the breadcrumb chip bar looks like at each step. As written this is an assertion, not a demonstration.

## Is uncertainty honest or decorative?

Mostly honest, and this is the entry's best material. Concrete mechanisms that actually change what's shown, not just how it's shown: flip fraction as the real headline instead of the binary CLEAR/BLOCKED verdict; the θ-swept friendly-fire spread bar that explicitly refuses to call ambiguous damage "friendly fire"; the front field refusing to render below 3 tracked avatars per side rather than drawing a fake line; the confound list attached to every behavior metric with an explicit refusal to print a verdict about a person. The "spread bar under every headline number" rule (§4.1) is a good universal discipline. One place it slips: "headline: CLEAR shot exists" is computed as "any cell is clear" out of up to 78 sampled cells — an existential quantifier over a wide, coarsely-gridded search practically guarantees most kills read CLEAR at the headline, making the headline close to meaningless and the flip fraction the entire signal. The design does teach the officer to read flip fraction (both walkthroughs do), so it survives, but the headline label itself is closer to decorative than the rest of the uncertainty machinery — consider dropping the binary headline in favor of leading with flip fraction/coverage directly, since the design already knows the binary isn't the real answer.

## Would the 3D overlay be usable while alt-camming?

Partially addressed, with one gap that matters mechanically. The entry correctly keeps ALT/CTRL untouched for camera control and routes selection through "plain left-click," which is the right instinct. But alt-cam navigation in this viewer is itself driven by left-click-drag to orbit; the entry never distinguishes a stationary click (select) from a click-that-becomes-a-drag (orbit), and with rings, crosses, damage lines, fan rays, lattice cells and isoline segments all registering screen-rect hit tests simultaneously, the two gestures are guaranteed to collide during exactly the free-roam alt-cam use the brief prioritizes as primary. This needs a stated policy (e.g., select only fires if pointer travel stays under N px between down and up) — it's a one-paragraph fix but it's currently unaddressed.

Separately, the overlay stacks a lot of concurrently-drawable layers (feet rings + yaw arrows + eye glyphs + damage lines for up to ~37 agents, trails, stroboscope rings, τ-fan rays, lattice fill, front isolines, spawn rings, ghost-pair duplicates) with no described LOD or camera-distance decluttering beyond the confidence-floor filter and the Grid's own cell cap — the Risks section names colour budget and text density but not overlay density itself. For a 1–2 h, ~37-agent raid replayed from a wide alt-cam vantage, this will plausibly be visually saturated before an officer selects anything to reduce it. Worth a stated default (e.g. world draws only the current selection/pin set plus nearby avatars by default, full trails opt-in).

## Walking the officer questions myself

**Q1 (how did the raid go).** The default Query (Engagement × Team, Map, locked scale) is a reasonable landing view and Δ-vs-session-mean genuinely answers "where did it go wrong" at a glance — good. But it assumes Engagement segmentation produced multiple legible engagements; if single-link chaining collapses the raid into one blob (see above), this walkthrough's first move fails before it starts, and there's no fallback shown for that case the way the FFA walkthrough has a fallback for undefined fronts.

**Q3 (why did X die).** Works cleanly as designed and is the strongest walkthrough in the entry; no material objection beyond the drag-vs-click ambiguity in the 3D step.

**Q8 (suspicious behavior).** The mechanics (population strip, percentile, confound list, "no verdict") are sound and match the brief's explicit demand for honesty over accusation. The one thing missing from the walkthrough itself: it never shows what happens when the officer disagrees or wants to check a *third* explanation not in the confound list (e.g., a spectator call-out on voice chat that isn't in any log) — the tool has no affordance for the officer to annotate "checked, plausible normal cause" versus leaving the flag dangling for the next reviewer. Given this is explicitly for promotion/violation review (officer workflow 9), an unresolved flag with no annotation path is a process gap, not just a UI one.

## What must be kept

- The single `Query` struct unifying Grid cells, breadcrumbs, pins and compare slots — this is the actual mechanism that makes digging coherent instead of a pile of screens, and no other entry is likely to match it cheaply.
- Uncertainty-as-sweep rather than uncertainty-as-annotation: flip fraction, θ-swept friendly-fire, front field's refusal to render — keep all of these verbatim.
- The Q3 walkthrough's drill chain (timeline → Death page → sweep grid → 3D fan → single hypothesis → sibling comparison) as the template for what "digging without losing the thread" should feel like.
- "Compared with" as a mandatory, always-populated column on every page — peer context should never be optional in this tool.
- The FFA fallback (front field refuses to resolve, axis switches to nearest-enemy distance, Teams tab states "no stable teams detected" rather than drawing a fake map) — this is the correct honest-degradation pattern and should be the model for other places where an inference can silently fail (team clustering under drift, engagement segmentation under chaining).

## What must change before this entry is usable

1. Give team clustering a time axis (per-phase or sliding-window re-clustering), not just a θ axis, so genuine mid-session defection is distinguishable from mere classification noise — this is a named brief requirement the current model cannot represent.
2. Cap or replace single-link engagement segmentation so continuous freeform combat cannot chain into one unreadable "engagement," since this is the default row facet for the flagship Q1 walkthrough.
3. Define click-vs-drag disambiguation for in-world selection so it doesn't fight alt-cam orbiting.
4. State a default overlay density/LOD policy so a 37-agent, multi-hour replay doesn't saturate the view before the officer narrows anything.
5. Resolve the thesis's own contradiction ("one view" vs. eleven noun pages) — keep the mechanism, drop or soften the claim.
